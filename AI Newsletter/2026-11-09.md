### Deutsche Bank and Ant Digital Technologies Propose "Bounded Agentic Execution" for Wholesale Finance

**Source:** [PR Newswire / Deutsche Bank](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deutsche-bank-and-ant-digital-technologies-release-white-paper-on-agentic-payments-in-wholesale-finance-302933156.html)  •  **Date:** November 9, 2026

Deutsche Bank and Ant Digital Technologies jointly published a white paper titled *Agentic Payments in Wholesale Finance*, arguing for a "bounded agentic execution" model in which AI reasoning and transaction settlement are separated by a deterministic rule layer. The three-tier reference architecture—drawn from an IMF framework—covers Intent and Orchestration, Control and Authorisation, and Settlement. Proposed safeguards include explicit and revocable agent authorisation limits, Know Your Agent (KYA) identity binding, and machine-readable authority constraints. Ant Digital's platform Anvita Flow is presented as an implementation case study, with treasury liquidity optimisation, cross-border payment routing, and trade-finance document handling as the target use cases.

The governance design is worth attention because it separates the question of what an AI agent can reason about from the question of what it can irrevocably execute. Institutions building toward agentic wholesale payments should map each proposed action to a tier, define what constitutes a valid authorisation proof at each boundary, and require the same accountability records from a third-party agent platform as they would from an internal system. White papers are proposals, not production evidence; independent evaluation of the referenced architecture is still needed before treating it as a deployment baseline.

### Goldman Sachs Restricts Claude Code and GitHub Copilot to Engineers Only

**Source:** [PYMNTS / Bloomberg](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/goldman-sachs-bars-bankers-from-employing-some-ai-tools/)  •  **Date:** November 2, 2026

Goldman Sachs updated internal guidance in October to limit Anthropic's Claude Code and Microsoft's GitHub Copilot to its roughly 12,000 engineers and quantitative strategists. Traders and bankers, who had been using the tools for months to monitor client flows, model risk, and build analytics dashboards, lose access. Bloomberg first reported the change. Goldman's spokesperson cited a post-experimentation review weighing "productivity and risk management," though the bank has not published the specific findings. Concerns named in secondary reporting include cybersecurity risk, the cost of enterprise subscriptions, and the observation that code produced by non-engineers frequently required significant rework.

This is a notable recalibration at one of the largest AI-investing firms in the industry, and it cuts against the narrative that broad, unrestricted AI access is straightforwardly productive. Banks that have rolled out coding assistants to non-technical staff should audit what those users are actually doing—writing code that gets reviewed and deployed, or working around standard controls—and whether the productivity case was measured or assumed. The Goldman decision should also prompt review of what data was accessible to those tools during the experimentation period.

### BofA, Blackstone, Citi, and Morgan Stanley Arrange $63 Billion AI Compute Financing for Anthropic

**Source:** [PR Newswire / Broadcom](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bank-of-america-blackstone-citi-and-morgan-stanley-announce-landmark-63-billion-financing-structure-to-enable-ai-compute-capacity-in-partnership-with-broadcom-302919849.html)  •  **Date:** October 26, 2026

Bank of America, Citi, and Morgan Stanley arranged the first-lien portion of a $63 billion AI compute financing structure; Blackstone provided the entire committed second lien. The capital flows into a newly formed SPV that purchases Broadcom XPU AI accelerators and leases them to Anthropic, with hardware deliveries expected to begin in 2027. Broadcom supplies residual value support equal to the first-lien amount as the principal credit enhancement. The release cites no tranche sizes, interest rates, or tenor. Executive statements from Jon Gray (Blackstone), Dan Simkowitz (Morgan Stanley), Vis Raghavan (Citi), and Jim DeMare (BofA) describe it as an "unprecedented scale" capital mobilisation for AI.

The structural point for BFSI is that four major institutions have collectively underwritten tens of billions of dollars of credit exposure to AI infrastructure. Capital markets desks and credit risk teams will need to price, monitor, and manage AI compute assets as a new collateral class, understand residual-value support structures for technology that depreciates faster than traditional infrastructure, and evaluate concentration and counterparty exposure in a relatively small number of AI hardware providers. This deal also signals that debt-financed AI infrastructure is arriving at a scale that regulators and analysts are beginning to flag as a macro risk.

### Emirates NBD on Agentic AI: "Execution Is Yet to Come"

**Source:** [Business Today ME](https://businesstoday.me/interviews-features/emirates-nbd-on-agentic-ai-and-the-future-of-banking/)  •  **Date:** November 9, 2026

Rohit Garg, Group Head of Retail Products and Chief Digital Officer for Retail Banking and Wealth Management at Emirates NBD, described the bank's AI development at Dubai Future Finance Week 2026 through a three-stage framework: understanding, deciding, and executing. He said the first two are operational—AI supports customer onboarding and hyper-personalised product offers—but the execution stage, in which an agent can initiate transactions autonomously, has not yet been deployed. Agentic AI is in production in middle-office document processing. On agentic commerce, Garg acknowledged that before giving mandates to agents, banks will need to resolve who the agent is, what mandate the customer gave, and who is accountable for failures.

The "execution is yet to come" framing is a useful maturity signal from a Gulf institution that is further ahead than most regional peers. The accountability questions Garg raised—identity, mandate scope, and failure ownership—are not rhetorical; they are the same governance gaps that HKMA's Project SAGE and Wealthsimple's Penny have both addressed explicitly. Banks that are still in the understanding-and-deciding phase should treat the execution gap as a planning horizon, not a distant future state: the technical capability to execute will arrive faster than the governance infrastructure to control it safely.
