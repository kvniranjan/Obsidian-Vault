### Payments Summit Highlights the Data Gap in Agentic Commerce
**Source:** [Secure Technology Alliance via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/20/3298520/22743/en/2026-Identity-Payments-Summit-Recap-AI-Transforms-Fraud-Agentic-Commerce-and-Digital-Identity-in-Action.html) - **Date:** May 20, 2026

The Secure Technology Alliance's 2026 Identity & Payments Summit recap put useful detail around agentic commerce, fraud, and digital identity. Participants discussed AI-driven fraud, continuous trust assessment, behavioral analytics, device intelligence, adaptive authentication, mobile driver's licenses, and wallet-based credentials. The most relevant banking point came from a Bank of America speaker, who said issuers rely on transaction data for risk decisions and that current agentic payment protocols do not yet provide enough clarity on what information will be shared or how agent involvement will be disclosed. The summit also framed open questions around consumer intent, delegated authority, and distinguishing legitimate agent activity from high-velocity bot-like behavior.

That is the issue payment networks and AI-commerce vendors keep glossing over. Agentic payments are not safe just because a token exists or a customer gave broad permission earlier. Issuers, acquirers, merchants, and wallets need enough structured data to identify the agent, understand the delegation, evaluate the purchase context, detect coercion or automation abuse, and resolve disputes later. If the agent is invisible or poorly described in the transaction payload, banks will either decline too much or absorb too much risk. The industry needs an agentic data standard before agentic commerce becomes mainstream. Otherwise the fraud and dispute mess will arrive after the marketing demos.

### nCino Benchmark Shows Bank AI Adoption Is Ahead of Accountability
**Source:** [nCino via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/13/3293933/0/en/banks-are-moving-to-a-dual-workforce-model-ncino-s-inaugural-ai-in-banking-benchmark-shows.html) - **Date:** May 13, 2026

nCino's inaugural AI in Banking Benchmark says 89% of surveyed senior banking executives expect a dual workforce of AI agents and humans within five years. The survey, fielded among 150 U.S. banking technology and business decision-makers in late March and early April, says 84% of institutions are already using AI at the enterprise level and 91% have defined AI strategies. The problem is measurement. Only 21% currently tie AI investments to increased revenue, 26% tie them to cost reduction, and 81% prioritize adoption over return on investment. Data confidence is also shakier than the headline suggests: 87% say they can access good data, but 93% cite at least one data governance challenge, including data silos, integrity issues, incomplete data, and poor quality.

This is the most honest bank AI story of the week. The industry is moving fast, but a lot of that motion still lacks accountability. Banking leaders should stop celebrating adoption as if it were an outcome. The relevant questions are narrower and harder: which AI workflows reduced cycle time, lowered losses, improved conversion, cleared compliance backlogs, or increased relationship profitability? Which ones created new exceptions, complaints, model-risk issues, or data leakage? A dual workforce is not a strategy by itself. It is an operating model that needs KPIs, ownership, escalation paths, controls, and a clean answer to who is accountable when the agent gets it wrong.

### ACH False Pretenses Deadline Turns AI-Scaled Fraud Into a Control Test
**Source:** [Proof](https://www.proof.com/blog/the-fraud-files-bank-insiders-bec-billions-and-the-ai-threat-to-ach-payments-may-2026) / [Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/news/new-nacha-risk-management-rules-now-effect) - **Date:** May 22, 2026

Proof's May Fraud Files brief ties several current fraud signals to the June 22, 2026 Phase 2 deadline for Nacha's new fraud monitoring rule. That phase extends the requirement beyond the largest processors to every corporate ACH originator, requiring documented, risk-based processes to identify ACH entries initiated because of fraud, including business email compromise, vendor impersonation, and payroll diversion. Proof highlights five signals: a bank-insider fraud ring that defeated identity checks for more than two years, roughly USD 3 billion in BEC losses flowing mainly over ACH and wire, FINRA's AI-focused threat intelligence work, fraudsters keeping disbursements just below review thresholds, and AI voice calls reaching one in four Americans as part of false-pretenses chains.

The takeaway is blunt: authorized payments are becoming the fraud frontier. Credentials can be valid, the user can be real, and the payment can still be criminally induced. AI makes the persuasion layer cheaper, faster, and more personalized, while instant and ACH credit flows reduce recovery time. Banks and originators need more than static callbacks and manual review queues. They need payee verification, behavioral context, vendor-change controls, transaction-intent evidence, exception documentation, and audit trails that satisfy the ODFI before access to ACH becomes a relationship problem. Fraud monitoring is moving from "nice control" to access-to-rails hygiene.

### Sherlocq Launches AI-Native Regulatory Intelligence for Financial Services
**Source:** [Sherlocq via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sherlocq-launches-the-first-ai-native-regulatory-intelligence-platform-for-global-financial-services-302770724.html) - **Date:** May 13, 2026

Sherlocq launched an AI-native regulatory intelligence platform for financial services, aimed at compliance officers, lawyers, risk professionals, and regulators. The platform covers regulatory output across more than 30 jurisdictions, including the U.S., UK, UAE, Singapore, and Hong Kong. It supports regulatory research, cross-border comparison, obligation mapping, document intelligence, policy analysis, and sanctions intelligence across OFAC, OFSI, EU, UAE, and more than 320 data sources. Sherlocq says it gives sourced and traceable answers in seconds, supports Claude and ChatGPT connectors at launch, and is certified to ISO 27001 and ISO 27701, with Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini integrations planned.

This is the right use case for vertical AI, but it is also where weak implementation can hurt. Regulatory intelligence is not a generic search problem. A wrong answer can become a bad policy, a missed filing, a sanctions exposure, or a false sense of compliance. The useful benchmark is not whether the tool sounds authoritative. It is whether every answer is sourced, current, jurisdiction-aware, permissioned, retained, and reviewable. Banks should treat AI regulatory research as supervised decision support, not legal authority. The firms that win here will reduce research time without breaking accountability.
