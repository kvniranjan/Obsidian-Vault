### Interactive Brokers Adds ChatGPT and Grok to Agentic Wealth Workflows
**Source:** [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/interactive-brokers-expands-agentic-wealth-ecosystem-with-chatgpt-and-grok-alliances/) - **Date:** June 27, 2026

Interactive Brokers expanded its AI connector ecosystem beyond Claude by adding ChatGPT and Grok integrations. The setup lets clients use natural language to research portfolios and generate order instructions across more complex asset classes, including options, futures, and futures options. The broker says account passwords and raw API keys are not shared with external AI providers, and generated orders are staged in an AI Instructions tab for manual review before execution.

This is a real step beyond chatbot finance. It connects public AI tools to brokerage workflows where mistakes can move money. The useful part is the control pattern: zero-key connectivity, staged instructions, and explicit human signoff. Banks and wealth platforms should copy that discipline before letting agents touch trading, advice, lending, or payments. Natural-language execution without review gates is not innovation. ### ECB Warns Advanced AI Could Make Bank Cyber Incidents Systemic
**Source:** [ECB Banking Supervision](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.mepletter260625_DeMasi~b3f7e17e42.en.pdf) - **Date:** June 25, 2026

ECB Supervisory Board Chair Claudia Buch told a member of the European Parliament that large-scale AI models with stronger cybersecurity capabilities could increase systemic cyber risk for financial institutions. The letter says vulnerabilities may be found and exploited faster at lower marginal cost. It also says AI risks are part of the ECB's 2026 to 2028 supervisory priorities, and banks are encouraged to test AI-driven cyber-threat scenarios prepared through the EU systemic cyber incident framework.

The message for bank leaders is blunt: AI cyber risk is now supervisory business, not a lab concern. Controls built around quarterly testing and human-speed response will not hold if attackers use models to compress discovery, exploit development, and targeting. Banks need scenario testing, supplier concentration mapping, rapid revocation playbooks, and board reporting that treats AI-enabled cyber events as plausible systemic shocks.

Smarsh reported results from its AWS strategic collaboration for regulated AI adoption. The company says a global investment bank reduced compliance reviewer workload by 77 percent across millions of alerts, with less than 2 percent loss in risk detection. Smarsh also says deployments using AWS Bedrock and related agent infrastructure are aimed at communications surveillance, discovery, noise reduction, governance, and regulatory defensibility.

This is the kind of AI metric compliance executives should demand: review volume, detection loss, false-positive reduction, and auditability. The caveat is that vendor case studies need independent validation before anyone rewires surveillance around them. Still, the direction is right. Compliance AI should be judged by defensible outcomes, not demos. If a tool cannot explain what it suppressed and why, the efficiency gain is a liability.

### Federato Takes AI-Native Insurance Platform Into APAC and Japan
**Source:** [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260622031014/en/Federato-Brings-Proven-AI-Native-Insurance-Platform-To-Asia-Pacific-and-Japan) - **Date:** June 22, 2026

Federato announced its expansion into Asia-Pacific and Japan, opening a Sydney office and appointing former Guidewire executive Roland Slee as regional vice president. The company says its AI-native platform spans the insurance policy lifecycle and is aimed first at commercial carriers and managing general agents in Australia and New Zealand, with Singapore, Japan, and Korea to follow. It describes the platform as replacing legacy policy administration workflows with AI built into underwriting and claims operations.

This matters because insurance AI is moving from edge experiments into core workflow replacement. That is where the returns are, but it is also where failures become expensive. Insurers should focus on whether the system improves underwriting discipline, loss ratios, cycle time, and auditability. If AI only speeds up bad submissions or hides exceptions inside a black box, the old core system was not the main problem.
