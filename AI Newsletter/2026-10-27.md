### Deutsche Bank's ADA Platform Cuts Some Corporate Loan Decisions from Weeks to Minutes

**Source:** [Global Banking & Finance Review](https://globalbankingandfinance.com/ai-cuts-deutsche-bank-corporate-loan-decisions-minutes)  •  **Date:** October 26, 2026

Deutsche Bank's technology chief Marie-Jeanne Deverdun said the bank's internal AI platform, ADA, is cutting the time for some smaller corporate lending decisions from weeks to minutes, and some multinational decisions from months to weeks. ADA, which handles development, monitoring, and switching between AI models, went live in summer 2026. Deverdun noted the acceleration applies to simpler corporate structures, not every deal, and the bank has not disclosed independent performance verification. Deutsche Bank has also announced €2.5 billion in additional technology investment for 2026–2028.

Loan-decision speed matters, but it is a throughput number, not a credit-quality result. Banks should require evidence that faster AI-assisted decisions produce equivalent or better default rates, coverage ratios, covenant quality, and adverse-action documentation before using cycle-time reduction as a measure of success. The key governance question is whether ADA's model changes, threshold shifts, and credit-rule updates require the same approval discipline as the human policy committee they are now accelerating.

### First Internet Bank Cuts SBA Compliance Review Time 87% with AI Agents

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/banking/2026/first-internet-bank-bets-ai-means-more-loans-not-fewer-people/)  •  **Date:** October 26, 2026

First Internet Bank of Indiana ($6 billion in assets) has deployed Bretton AI agents across four SBA compliance workflows: loan origination, enhanced due diligence, fraud reviews, and periodic customer reviews. The agents gather corporate records, identify beneficial owners, and screen for sanctions and fraud indicators, then compile a structured report for human analyst review. Bretton AI reports a reduction in SBA compliance review time from 8–10 hours to under one hour, saving an estimated 24,300 employee hours per year. CEO David Becker said the freed capacity should produce more lending: "I want you to do twice as much business." The 87% and 24,300-hour figures are vendor-reported; the bank's $610 million in SBA 7(a) loans funded in 2024, up 44%, predates the deployment.

The important design element is that analysts approve every report and retain decision authority. Banks following this model should still test source completeness, entity-resolution accuracy, false-negative rates on sanctions and fraud signals, and whether the human review step is substantive rather than nominal. An agent that consistently produces the same recommendation does not produce meaningful oversight if reviewers rarely override it.

### Schwab Builds Read-Only MCP Infrastructure as Banks Draw the Line on Agent Money Movement

**Source:** [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/schwab-blocks-ai-agents-meta-muse) / [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/schwab-blocks-unauthorized-ai-agents-points-clients-to-charley)  •  **Date:** October 16, 2026 (ongoing)

Charles Schwab CEO Rick Wurster told Bloomberg Television that the firm blocks third-party AI agents, including Meta's Muse, from moving client money—tens to twenties of times per week. JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, and Morgan Stanley are also restricting external agents from customer accounts. Schwab's constructive response is to build model context protocol (MCP) servers that give approved AI tools read-only access to account balances and transaction history, while money movement remains reserved for Schwab's own assistant, Charley. Wurster's core concern: "We can't control it, the client can't control it, something could go wrong and who's liable?" AI agent liability remains unresolved in US Congress.

The convergence on read-only MCP as a boundary is the substantive news, not just the blocking. It signals that financial institutions are settling on a two-tier model: open data access for third-party agents, scoped execution authority for institution-approved channels. The hard work is defining what "approved" means in practice—agent identity, per-account permissions, consent records, revocation, and dispute evidence—before a customer funds a transaction that neither the agent, the bank, nor a court can cleanly attribute.

### Visa Adds AI-Powered Spend Analytics and Supplier Fraud Controls for B2B Payments

**Source:** [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/visa-and-mastercard-launch-ai-and-stablecoin-tech-products)  •  **Date:** October 22, 2026

Ahead of earnings, Visa launched SESA 2.0, an AI-driven analytics tool for corporate payments that analyzes spending data and surfaces high-potential supplier opportunities for businesses. It also launched Visa Commercial Fraud Solutions, which helps issuers and businesses verify suppliers and authenticate virtual-card transactions before approvals are processed. Both products target the commercial card and B2B payments stack rather than consumer flows. Visa also continued its Trusted Agent Protocol pilot with BILL and Billtrust, already covered here on October 22.

B2B payments have traditionally lagged consumer fraud controls by years because the buyer-supplier relationship was assumed to provide its own verification. AI-driven pre-approval screening closes a real gap, but treasury and AP teams should test whether SESA 2.0's supplier signals are tuned to their vendor base and payment patterns, and whether the commercial fraud controls handle legitimate emergency payments, supplier bank-account changes, and cross-border invoices without generating false friction that delays critical payments.
