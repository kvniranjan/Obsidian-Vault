### Feedzai Embeds Farol Agent in Bank Fraud Operations

**Source:** [Feedzai](https://www.feedzai.com/pressrelease/as-banks-pivot-to-agentic-ai-feedzai-unveils-farol-to-transform-fraud-analysis-and-cut-investigation-times/)  |  **Date:** September 24, 2026

Feedzai launched Farol, an embedded AI agent inside RiskOps Studio for fraud strategy analysis, alert investigation, product guidance, and suspicious activity report drafting. The company says Farol can summarize case context, suggest rule improvements, and reduce alert-handling time by 20%, while its SAR drafting skill can cut manual effort by up to 12 times. Feedzai says the agent operates inside the financial institution's environment and keeps audit trails for its actions.

The important change is architectural: the agent is connected to transaction and case context inside an existing risk workflow instead of sitting beside the system as a generic chatbot. Fraud leaders should still treat the reported productivity numbers as vendor claims. The due-diligence question is whether the agent's suggestions can be reproduced, approved, and monitored without weakening evidence quality, investigator judgment, or regulatory accountability.

### HSBC Puts Promenaut Agent Orchestration Into Production

Promenaut has gone into production at HSBC for selected operational workflows, including business-case evaluation and pre-coding steps in the software delivery lifecycle. The platform provides permissions, audit trails, and human-in-the-loop controls, while HSBC has also invested in the company and appointed its chief AI officer to the board. This is a useful signal that banks are testing agent orchestration as an operating-model layer, not just buying isolated assistants. The initial workflows are bounded and internal, which is the sensible place to start. Technology and risk leaders should demand the same discipline before agents touch customer money or regulated decisions: explicit authority, least privilege, approval gates, run-level evidence, and measurable exception handling.

### Baselayer Launches an Identity and Risk Layer for AI Agents

**Source:** [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/110994/baselayer-raises-35-million-rolls-out-agentic-identtity-suite)  |  **Date:** September 22, 2026

Baselayer raised $35 million in Series A funding and launched an Agentic Identity Suite for software acting on behalf of people and businesses. The company is extending its existing business-identity and risk infrastructure to help institutions determine which agent is acting, who it represents, whether it is authorized, and whether the counterparty can be trusted. Baselayer says its network already serves more than 2,300 financial institutions and payments companies, but those figures are company-reported.

Agentic commerce will fail at the identity boundary if banks cannot distinguish a legitimate delegated action from a compromised or over-privileged agent. Institutions should not wait for a universal industry standard before defining their own control requirements. Agent registration, representation, authority scope, revocation, cryptographic evidence, step-up authentication, transaction limits, and dispute liability all need owners before delegated payment rights are expanded.

### Tink Founders Launch Freda as an Agentic Compliance Platform

**Source:** [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/news-fintech-unicorn-tink-founders-launch-new-venture-in-compliance-1cc85623)  |  **Date:** September 23, 2026

Former Tink founders Daniel Kjellen, Fredrik Hedberg, and Adam Ivarsson launched Freda, a bootstrapped platform that combines a regulatory engine, a company knowledge graph, and an execution layer for compliance work. Freda says its agents can map obligations, build tailored programs, monitor vendors, gather evidence, track incidents, and keep policies and controls aligned across systems. Its public product description emphasizes grounding actions in structured regulatory sources and customer-specific context rather than a general-purpose model alone.

The proposition moves regtech from a repository of rules toward a system that performs recurring compliance work. That raises the bar for evidence, not just automation. Buyers need to test how local legal interpretation is updated, how conflicts between obligations are resolved, who approves changes, how agents are stopped, and whether every output can be traced to both the source rule and the firm's own approved policy.

### Mirai RiskTech Raises EUR 5M for AI-Driven Bank Balance-Sheet Management

**Source:** [Mirai RiskTech](https://mirairisktech.com/press-center/mirai-risktech-raises-5m-from-inveready-to-expand-globally)  |  **Date:** September 22, 2026

Mirai RiskTech raised EUR 5 million from Inveready to expand its integrated asset and liability management platform and develop its AI product line. The platform combines interest-rate risk in the banking book, liquidity risk, funds-transfer pricing, and regulatory reporting on a shared data model. Mirai says its AI Modeling product can operationalize behavioral models inside production workflows, while its AI Agent brings ALM and banking-regulation knowledge into daily balance-sheet analysis with traceability.

Treasury AI is more consequential than a front-office productivity demo because poor assumptions can flow into liquidity, pricing, capital, and regulatory decisions. A shared data model is a prerequisite, not proof of model quality. Banks evaluating this category should test recalibration, stress scenarios, override governance, source data lineage, and whether finance and risk teams are genuinely using the same numbers before adding natural-language interaction.
