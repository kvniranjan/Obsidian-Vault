### Starling Founder Anne Boden Reveals Lockwood, an Agentic AI-Native Bank

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/banking/2026/starling-founder-launches-agentic-ai-focused-bank-lockwood/)  •  **Date:** November 16, 2026

Starling Bank founder Anne Boden revealed that she is building an AI-first bank called Lockwood (lockwood.money), funded in part from proceeds of her Starling share sale. Boden's stated aim is to build a bank "built by agents, to serve customers that want to use agents, and then be defended by agents." Lockwood will offer a traditional current account, will not lend or offer savings, and will charge no fees, aiming to earn revenue from interest on deposits. Computer Weekly reported that Lockwood is targeting an Electronic Money Institution licence from the FCA in 2027, with a full banking licence sought by 2028.

The venture is a direct test of the design question that incumbents have so far answered defensively: can a deposit account be built from scratch to work safely with AI agents, with the right identity, permission, and limit controls built in from the start? Banks blocking agents have bought time to define those controls; Lockwood is betting on defining them in architecture from day one. The gap will become visible in 2027 when Lockwood files for its EMI licence and regulators ask what distinguishes its agent governance from that of a conventional digital bank.

### PNC and Fifth Third CEOs Put Hard Limits on AI Agent Transactions

**Source:** [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ai-agent-protocols-bank-access-socure-pnc-fifth-third-fraud-aml-kyc/873970/)  •  **Date:** November 6, 2026

Socure CEO Johnny Ayers said in an interview that "there's a lot of code reds going on in the banks right now" over AI agent access, and estimated six months before clear protocols emerge across the industry. PNC CEO Bill Demchak said agent-only transactions "will never happen" and that any agent-initiated transaction must still require the customer to remain in the loop and authenticate — a position he called "tablestakes" at the BancAnalysts Association of Boston conference. Fifth Third CEO Tim Spence, CFO Bryan Preston, and CIO Jude Schramm said their bank blocks AI agents and that the industry needs "know your agent" protocols analogous to KYC. Socure, which counts 19 of the top 20 U.S. banks among its roughly 6,000 customers, launched an AI agent detection and trust-scoring tool tied to the verified human who authorized the agent. These are company and executive statements, not a regulatory standard.

This is the clearest public line drawn yet by large-bank CEOs on agent transactionality. The distinction Demchak makes — agentic suggestion versus agentic execution — is likely to shape how U.S. banks define minimum controls before they can re-enable agent access: verified agent identity, human authentication at the transaction boundary, and a clear liability framework for agent-caused errors. Banks that block agents today should publish a path to safe enablement rather than treating a block as a permanent posture.

A FinCrime Frontier 2026-27 survey cited at the event found AI and automation were the leading compliance investment priority for 61.9% of respondents. A separate session addressed whether the EU's Digital Services Act should require banks to report suspected scam content on social media platforms, with a European Commission official indicating a decision on a voluntary code of conduct for platforms could come soon.

The policy direction from Brussels is consistent with what UK and US regulators have said: AI governance for financial crime is now a shared public and private responsibility. The DSA scam-reporting discussion is new terrain for banks — mandating that financial institutions identify and flag scam content to digital platforms would add a new reporting obligation and cross-sector data-exchange expectation that compliance and financial-crime teams should track before it becomes binding.

The significance for incumbent small-business banking is competitive scope. Bundling deposits, payroll, taxes, and credit in one AI-driven interface shifts the acquisition question from "is the account better?" to "is the financial operating system better?" Banks competing for small-business primacy should map how their own AI features compare across the full operating stack, since deposit products alone are unlikely to retain the businesses most willing to integrate AI into their financial workflows.

### Starling Bank Extends Its AI Platform to Voice-Initiated Payments

**Source:** [Finovate](https://finovate.com/starling-bank-brings-conversational-ai-to-everyday-payments/)  •  **Date:** November 16, 2026

Starling Bank announced that its Starling Intelligence agentic platform will add a "talk to transact" capability, letting personal and joint account holders describe a payment in natural language — such as "Pay the plumber £200" — and have the Starling Assistant set up the payee, amount, and confirmation. The customer approves the final step by tapping the screen, and Starling states the Assistant does not independently initiate or complete transactions. The feature has not yet launched; Starling expects to reach all retail customers by end of 2026, with a small-business version to follow. Starling Intelligence has released 14 preconfigured Smart Tools since its August launch, with one new tool each week through year-end. These are company-announced plans, not live product metrics.

Starling's control design — agent sets up, human confirms — represents the current minimum viable architecture for agentic payments. The harder questions will surface as the feature scales: what happens when the natural-language prompt is ambiguous, how errors are reversed before or after settlement, and how the bank resolves disputes when payment reached the right account at the wrong amount or on the wrong date. Each of those is an operational control design problem, not a model quality problem.
