### Swift Launches Cross-Border Pay-by-Alias Proof of Concept with 14 Banks at Sibos

**Source:** [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/sibos-2026-day-one-fraser-tells-miami-to-move-fast-without-breaking-trust/)  •  **Date:** September 29, 2026

Swift announced a cross-border pay-by-alias proof of concept involving 14 financial institutions, including BBVA, Bradesco, DBS Bank, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, and Citizens Bank. The initiative allows a consumer or corporate to send an international payment to a mobile number or email address rather than requiring a full account number and routing code. The PoC builds on Swift's existing domestic alias infrastructure and tests whether the same abstraction layer can work across jurisdictions, currencies, and correspondent networks.

The friction of account number lookup and validation is a persistent source of misdirected payments and cross-border abandonment. A globally interoperable alias layer could significantly reduce initiation errors and friction for both retail and SME senders — and positions Swift's messaging infrastructure as the identity resolution layer for cross-border AI-initiated payments, where an agent needs to pay a counterparty without requiring a human to supply IBAN details.

### IMF at Sibos: AI Should Augment Payment Rails, Not Disrupt Them — But Cyber Risk on Shared Infrastructure Is Critical

**Source:** [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/sibos-2026-day-one-fraser-tells-miami-to-move-fast-without-breaking-trust/)  •  **Date:** September 29, 2026

IMF First Deputy Managing Director Dan Katz delivered a Sibos 2026 keynote arguing that the near-term priority for AI in payments should be improving existing systems rather than replacing them. He outlined three specific applications: AI agents comparing fees and execution quality to lower switching costs across payment rails; automating liquidity and contractual timing mechanics within payments; and reducing regulatory reporting and compliance costs through AI-assisted preparation. Katz also explicitly flagged "AI-driven cyber risk on shared infrastructure" as a critical financial stability concern, warning that "competition is a financial stability question" when AI adoption concentrates workloads on a small number of shared platforms and models.

The IMF's framing is a useful corrective to the replacement narrative that dominates vendor marketing. Near-term gains from routing optimization, compliance automation, and liquidity efficiency are achievable within existing rails and regulatory frameworks. The shared-infrastructure concentration risk Katz raised deserves board-level attention: as banks co-locate AI workloads on the same cloud providers and increasingly use the same underlying models, a single failure or adversarial event propagates across the system simultaneously.
