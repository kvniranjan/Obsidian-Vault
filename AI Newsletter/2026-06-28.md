### Taktile Raises $110 Million for AI Decisioning in Banking and Insurance
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2026/taktile-raises-110-million-to-automate-high-stakes-banking-and-insurance-decisions/) - **Date:** June 24, 2026

Taktile raised $110 million in a Series C led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives to expand its AI decisioning platform for financial institutions. The company says its platform combines AI agents, rules, contextual data, and human oversight to automate decisions such as customer approval, claims reimbursement, fraud prevention, and business-loan underwriting. Reported customer outcomes include 95% automation in B2B underwriting and a 75% reduction in AML false positives.

This is where AI moves from assistance into regulated decision infrastructure. That is useful only if the institution can prove why each decision was made, which policy applied, who reviewed exceptions, and how model drift is caught. Banks and insurers should not buy this category as a labor-saving shortcut. They should buy it only if it strengthens risk selection, auditability, and customer outcomes at the same time.

### FCA Says AI Regulation Has to Shift Toward Resilience and Stewardship
**Source:** [Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/rethinking-regulation-age-ai) - **Date:** June 24, 2026

FCA chief executive Nikhil Rathi said financial services will be central to the UK's AI economy because the sector provides capital, infrastructure, and trust. The speech said agentic systems will move beyond summarizing and detecting into coordinating and transacting, while tokenisation could provide programmable infrastructure for agentic finance. The FCA also said it is exploring agentic AI as a first responder for wholesale-market monitoring and expects more focus on competition, collaboration, and system-wide resilience.

The important point is not that the FCA is going soft on AI. It is saying traditional rule writing cannot keep up with markets that move this fast. That puts more burden on firms, not less. Boards need dependency maps for model providers, cloud providers, data vendors, and embedded AI tools. If a bank cannot explain where AI sits in its operating model, it is not ready for agentic finance.

### Dun & Bradstreet Adds Agentic AI to KYB and Third-Party Risk Workflows
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/23/db-agentic-ai-cuts-compliance-times-by-up-to-96/) - **Date:** June 23, 2026

Dun & Bradstreet launched agentic AI capabilities inside its D&B Risk Analytics platform and through a Model Context Protocol server. The company says the tools embed verified D&B data, models, and workflows into AI assistants and custom agents for onboarding, screening, due diligence, and monitoring. Reported early results include 70% to 96% lower processing time, 50% to 90% fewer false positives, review capacity up to 20 times higher, and false positive resolution up to 700% faster.

KYB is a good test case for financial-services AI because the workflow is messy, evidence-heavy, and unforgiving. Automating it without verified data just produces faster wrong answers. The D&B model is more credible because it joins agent execution to an established commercial graph and traceable workflow. Compliance leaders should still demand exception samples, audit logs, and proof that beneficial-owner checks survive difficult entity structures.

### Frank Launches an AI Loan Broker for U.S. Small Businesses
**Source:** [PYMNTS Newswire](https://www.pymnts.com/newswire-announcements/frank-launches-an-ai-loan-broker-built-to-fix-small-business-access-to-capital/) - **Date:** June 22, 2026

Frank launched publicly as an AI loan broker for U.S. small businesses. The company says it searches more than 500 lenders in under 60 seconds, negotiates on the borrower's behalf, shows the exact cost of each offer before commitment, and does not use a hard credit pull to show options. It says the service covers working capital, lines of credit, equipment loans, and SBA lending.

This is a direct challenge to banks that still make small businesses wait through opaque credit processes. The upside is clear: broader lender comparison, faster decisioning, and less dependence on expensive merchant cash advances. The risk is equally clear. If the broker optimizes for funding speed without suitability, affordability, and disclosure discipline, it simply automates bad borrowing. Lenders and banks should watch the transparency model, not just the speed claim.

### Commercial Insurers Push AI Governance Into Board-Level Operating Design
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/26/ai-governance-in-commercial-insurance-why-now-matters/) - **Date:** June 26, 2026

FinTech Global reported IntellectAI's view that commercial insurers are moving from "how fast can we deploy AI" to "how responsibly can we scale it." The article highlights AI use across submissions, fraud detection, broker servicing, claims, underwriting, and risk assessment. It also notes that bad AI decisions in commercial insurance can create million-dollar losses, pricing bias, inaccurate policy language, and decisions that cannot be defended to regulators, brokers, reinsurers, or clients.

This is the right governance conversation. Insurance AI is no longer a back-office productivity tool. It is entering pricing, coverage language, fraud judgment, and portfolio risk. Those are financial and conduct decisions. Carriers need human-in-the-loop design, model approval gates, drift monitoring, data controls, and appeal paths before they scale. "The model recommended it" is not a defense.
