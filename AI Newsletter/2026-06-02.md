### Citi Launches Arc — An Enterprise Platform for Deploying AI Agents

Citi unveiled Arc, an internal platform designed to create and deploy AI agents across the organization. The agents handle tasks including research, synthesis, client preparation, and execution workflows, with the stated goal of shifting bankers from administrative coordinators to client advisors. All agents will be monitored, auditable, and governed for transparency. The rollout begins with developers building agents for specific use cases before broader deployment. Over 80% of Citi's workforce already uses in-house AI tools regularly, making Arc the next phase in a layered AI buildout.

Arc matters because it is not a point solution. It is infrastructure for generating agents, which means the governance surface expands every time a team deploys a new one. The critical design question is whether Arc's monitoring and audit architecture scales with agent proliferation—or whether accountability gaps emerge as agents multiply faster than oversight does. Banks launching agent platforms need to solve for that asymmetry from the start, not after incidents force the issue.

### BBVA Puts Its Banking Products Inside ChatGPT
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/bbva-takes-its-banking-experience-into-chatgpt/)  •  **Date:** June 2, 2026

BBVA launched a conversational banking app directly within ChatGPT for customers in Italy and Germany. Users can ask questions about accounts, compare cards, and explore savings products without leaving the assistant and without a separate login. The current version is focused on discovery and information rather than transaction execution, but BBVA describes it as a foundation for a "digital companion" with deeper capabilities ahead. The context: more than 200 million users monthly already ask ChatGPT about personal finance, and 37% of power AI users now treat native AI platforms as their primary financial management interface.

This is the sharpest version yet of a question every retail bank is about to face. A conversational interface between a customer and their financial decisions is a relationship layer, not neutral plumbing. BBVA is making a deliberate bet that being present inside the channel customers already use is more valuable than defending the owned app. The risks are real—consent scope, liability for AI-generated financial guidance, data sharing boundaries—but so is the alternative: watching third-party AI platforms own the customer relationship while the bank provides the backend.

### EU AI Act High-Risk Deadline Hits Fraud, AML, and Credit Scoring in 60 Days
**Source:** [Unit21](https://www.unit21.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-2026-faqs-what-fraud-and-aml-teams-need-to-know)  •  **Date:** June 2026

The EU AI Act becomes fully enforceable for high-risk AI systems on August 2, 2026—roughly 60 days away. Fraud detection, AML transaction monitoring, and credit scoring are explicitly classified as high-risk under Annex III, whether the system was built in-house, procured from a vendor, or assembled as a hybrid. Mandatory requirements include explainable decision reasoning with complete audit trails, meaningful human review and override capability, documented training and inference data governance, ongoing risk management, continuous performance monitoring, conformity assessment before deployment, and incident documentation. Deploying institutions bear primary liability: banks cannot outsource compliance responsibility to their AI vendors.

The enforcement window is compressing fast for any European institution still treating this as a future-state project. The penalty ceiling is substantial—up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover. The hardest requirements in practice are explainability and human oversight at production scale: many fraud and AML systems were not designed with audit-trail depth or override ergonomics that regulators will expect to examine. Compliance teams that have not already mapped their AI inventory to Annex III should treat August 2 as a near-term production deadline, not a regulatory awareness milestone.

### Discovery Bank AI Doubles Customer Engagement With Proactive Recommendations
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/digital-first-banking/2026/discovery-bank-shows-how-ai-could-change-app-engagement/)  •  **Date:** June 2026

Discovery Bank deployed a generative AI system built on Azure OpenAI and Azure Databricks that continuously monitors customer behavior across its banking app and WhatsApp, proactively surfacing personalized financial recommendations rather than waiting for customer-initiated queries. Results: response times cut by more than 50% (from 5-6 seconds to under 2 seconds), client engagement with personalized recommendations doubled, 70% of clients now actively interact with next-best-action prompts, and app traffic nearly doubled in a single month. The bank's roughly 3,000 daily AI queries from service agents indicate internal adoption alongside consumer-facing use.

The design intent is explicit competitive defense: by keeping the AI recommendation layer inside the bank's own app, Discovery is holding the relationship position that external platforms like ChatGPT are trying to occupy. Speed was the enabling variable—at 5-6 seconds, customers ignored the recommendations; under 2 seconds, they engaged. That latency-to-engagement relationship is a useful data point for every bank evaluating personalization AI. The surface area for model-risk governance is also wide: proactive financial recommendations at this scale require bias testing, outcome monitoring, and clear disclosure to regulators and customers.
