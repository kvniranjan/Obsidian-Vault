### SBS Puts Sovereign AI Inside Core Banking

**Source:** [SBS](https://sbs-software.com/news/sbs-launches-bankings-first-sovereign-ai-platform/) | **Date:** October 13, 2026

SBS launched the SBS AI Foundation, powered by NVIDIA, for generative and agentic AI in a bank's own environment. The platform is already live with Scottish Building Society on Amazon Web Services and is available to the more than 1,500 financial institutions SBS serves. SBS says it connects core banking, lending, digital banking, and regulatory-reporting data to AI while keeping the data inside the institution's environment. Its reported early deployment result is 30% to 40% faster response times with more consistent outputs, explainability, and an audit trail.

The important development is infrastructure, not another assistant. A sovereign runtime can address data-residency and external-model concerns, but it does not remove model risk or third-party concentration risk. Banks should test tenant isolation, model and prompt change control, tool permissions, logging, rollback, and evidence that the claimed response-time gains hold outside the launch customer.

The company says the system can query a consortium spanning more than 650 lenders, 340 million loan applications, and $6 trillion in risk-assessed loans. ### Chase Sets Hard Boundaries for External AI Agents

**Source:** [Chase Terms of Use](https://www.chase.com/digital/resources/terms-of-use) | **Date:** Current terms accessed October 18, 2026

JPMorgan Chase's current digital terms define an AI agent as software that takes autonomous or semi-autonomous action on behalf of a person or entity. Agents must identify themselves, label agentic traffic deterministically, answer whether an interaction is coming from an agent, and follow the bank's terms. Chase reserves the right to limit, prevent, suspend, or terminate agent access through technical measures, and assigns users responsibility for actions or errors caused by an agent's incorrect interpretation or execution of instructions.

This is a clear signal that the first commercial boundary for bank agents will be bank-controlled identity and authorization, not password sharing by third-party assistants. Banks opening controlled agent access should publish capability scopes, approval rules, audit ownership, dispute handling, and a tested stop path. Agent providers should assume that opaque browser automation will be blocked or treated as an unauthorized access pattern.

### Ping An Digital Bank Completes HKMA Sandbox Trial for Deepfake Detection

**Source:** [Ping An Digital Bank via Media OutReach](https://businessintelligence.mo/2026/10/15/ping-an-digital-bank-completes-hkmas-second-gena-i-sandbox-cohort/) | **Date:** October 15, 2026

Ping An Digital Bank said it completed an Anti-Fraud Strategy Platform use case in the second cohort of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's GenA.I. Sandbox. Developed with OneConnect, the platform uses AI facial recognition to review user selfies in real time and flag forged or manipulated faces. Ping An reported detection accuracy above 96% in the sandbox and described the work as an AI-versus-AI defense against deepfake fraud. The result is a company-reported sandbox metric, not a live-production fraud-loss result.

The significance is the testing environment: deepfake defense is moving into supervised financial trials rather than remaining a generic security promise. Banks should evaluate false rejects, demographic performance, liveness and replay attacks, attack adaptation, customer friction, and human escalation alongside the headline detection rate.
