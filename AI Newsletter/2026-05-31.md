Fiserv's move also signals that the major banking technology vendors are consolidating agentic AI into managed platforms: rather than banks sourcing model APIs and building governance layers themselves, the expectation is increasingly that infrastructure providers will bundle both. That shifts model-risk questions from "how do we govern our own AI" to "how do we audit and exit-plan our vendor's AI."

### EU AI Act's August 2026 High-Risk AI Deadline Approaches: Credit Scoring and Insurance Under New Compliance Requirements
**Source:** [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/us-companies-face-eu-ai-acts-possible-august-2026-compliance-deadline)  •  **Date:** April 28, 2026

The EU AI Act's two-year implementation window for high-risk AI systems is set to expire August 2, 2026—applying full compliance obligations to AI systems classified as high-risk under Annex III. For financial services, this specifically includes AI used for credit scoring and insurance risk assessment, which are explicitly listed as high-risk regardless of the AI system's design or accuracy. Compliance requirements include completed conformity assessments, finalized technical documentation, a functioning risk management plan, human oversight mechanisms, and registration in the EU database for high-risk systems. Fines for non-compliance can reach €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, with the option to withdraw non-compliant systems from EU markets. Holland & Knight notes that the deadline may face delays to December 2027 or August 2028 pending Council action, but advises against waiting given the compliance workload involved.

The important jurisdictional point is scope. The EU AI Act applies to any AI system that produces outputs used in the EU—meaning a U.S. bank or insurer using AI for credit decisions or insurance pricing that affects European customers is in scope, even if the models run on servers outside Europe. Non-EU firms that have not started mapping their credit and insurance AI systems against the Act's high-risk requirements should treat the August 2026 date as a forcing function regardless of the delay risk: conformity assessment and documentation take time, the delay is not certain, and beginning compliance work now does not create risk if the deadline shifts.
