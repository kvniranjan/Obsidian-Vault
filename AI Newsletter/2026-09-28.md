### Feedzai Launches Farol: Embedded AI Agent for Fraud Investigation Inside RiskOps Studio

Feedzai launched Farol, an AI agent embedded directly inside its RiskOps Studio fraud operations platform. Farol provides four core capabilities: risk strategy analysis (identifying underperforming detection rules and recommending threshold changes, reducing a task that typically takes days to minutes); investigation assistance (summarizing alert data and context, reducing alert handling time by 20%); in-platform knowledge access; and automated SAR drafting (generating Suspicious Activity Reports up to 12 times faster). Feedzai says Farol addresses a common failure mode in agentic AI deployments — third-party models operating separately from real-time transaction data — by embedding contextual awareness and transparent audit trails directly into existing workflows.

The design choice to embed the agent inside the transaction-data environment rather than bolt it on externally is the key differentiator: Farol can reason over live context rather than summaries, and its audit trail is native to the compliance workflow. Banks evaluating fraud AI should ask vendors whether model outputs are traceable to the specific transaction data available at decision time, and whether the agent's rule recommendations can be tested in a sandbox before production deployment.

### Undeclared AI in Insurance: Shadow Usage Creates Systemic Blind Spot

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/25/undeclared-ai-is-insurances-biggest-blind-spot/)  •  **Date:** September 25, 2026

A Fintech Global analysis argues that employees and suppliers using AI tools outside organizational visibility now represent insurance's most significant unmanaged risk. Verizon data shows AI use on corporate devices quadrupled, with 67% of that activity occurring through personal accounts that businesses cannot monitor. Gartner expects over 40% of organizations to experience a security or compliance incident from unauthorized AI use by 2030. A separate IBM study found that roughly one in four malicious breaches were AI-enabled, at an average cost of approximately $6 million. On the systemic side, 60–80% of the market relies on the same small group of underlying AI models, creating concentration risk that affects the entire industry simultaneously — an exposure that standard carrier risk models were not designed to capture. Capgemini found that 42% of P&C insurers had not measured their AI outcomes at all.

Insurers face a compound problem: they cannot price exposures they cannot observe, and the product and claims vocabulary for AI-specific losses does not yet exist in most policy forms. The practical step is an AI-use inventory covering both sanctioned tools and the personal-account shadow category — followed by policy-form review to determine whether current cyber, professional liability, and E&O coverage language applies to AI-originated losses.
