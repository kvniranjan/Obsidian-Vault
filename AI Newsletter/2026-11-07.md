### FATF President: AI Is Making the Fraud Epidemic Worse — "The Worst Is Still to Come"

**Source:** [AML Intelligence](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2026/11/news-ai-digitalisation-and-better-connected-payments-mean-worst-still-to-come-from-fraud-and-online-scams-fatf-chief/)  •  **Date:** November 3, 2026

FATF President Giles Thomson, speaking at IAFCS2026, warned that AI is enabling impersonation attacks that target thousands of victims simultaneously, while better-connected instant payment systems give criminals the ability to move funds from a victim's account to an exit point in as little as three to five minutes — against an average detection lag of 20 hours. FATF estimates fraud costs roughly $500 billion globally each year, accounts for almost half of all crime in some countries, and goes unreported by approximately 70% of victims. Thomson also announced a landmark new FATF report on scam compounds, drawing on more than 100 case studies and published November 6, with recommendations for governments on how to disrupt AI-enabled fraud at every link in the chain.

For financial institutions, the three-to-five-minute window is the operative constraint. AML, fraud, and payments teams should map which of their real-time and near-real-time rails fall inside that window and test whether their detection, hold, and notification controls can intervene before funds exit. The FATF report should also be read alongside internal scam-liability frameworks — particularly in jurisdictions that have shifted or are shifting reimbursement obligations to receiving banks or platforms.

### India's I4C: Agentic AI Is Now Actively Attacking Bank Payment APIs

**Source:** [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/cybersecurity-agency-i4c-warns-banks-fintechs-against-ai-led-cyberattacks/)  •  **Date:** November 5, 2026

India's Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), through its National Cybercrime Threat Analysis Unit (NCTAU), issued an advisory on November 3 warning banks, NBFCs, and fintech firms that malicious agentic AI systems are actively being used to execute financial fraud at scale. The attack pattern involves exploiting unpatched software and misconfigured internet-facing servers — including customer-facing complaint interfaces — to reach automated fund disbursement systems and payment APIs. Funds are then routed through networks of mule accounts via IMPS and NEFT before withdrawal, and transaction records are deleted to conceal the trail. I4C said it had found multiple confirmed cases involving unauthorised transactions running into crores of rupees, and linked the advisory to a prior CERT-In critical alert on attackers using publicly available agentic AI tools.

The recommended mitigations are operationally specific: implement CERT-In advisories on payment and disbursement APIs urgently; set transaction limits and verify beneficiary details; monitor for sudden changes in transaction speed, volume, and patterns; ensure KYC and approval checks complete before funds move; and run authorised red-team exercises to test how fast controls respond to machine-speed attack sequences. I4C also instructs firms to report cyber incidents to CERT-In within six hours and to the RBI for regulated entities. The advisory is notable because it describes AI-driven attacks on bank infrastructure as an active, present threat — not a future-state risk.

### Wealthsimple Launches "Penny" — an AI Assistant That Prepares Trades but Cannot Execute Them

**Source:** [Yahoo Finance (The Canadian Press)](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/wealthsimple-launches-ai-banking-assistant-171034491.html)  •  **Date:** November 5, 2026

Wealthsimple unveiled "Penny," an AI banking assistant, at its Wealthsimple Presents event in Toronto. Penny can prepare e-transfers, flag duplicate credit card charges, manage expenses, pay invoices, provide financial insights, and advise on questions such as maximizing RRSP contributions. For self-directed investors, it can also help stage equity, ETF, and other asset trades. The company drew a hard line on execution: Penny will not move money, place a trade, or complete any financial action without explicit client confirmation, and a Wealthsimple director stated that "when it comes time to actually hitting send, that's an action that the user alone can take." The assistant runs inside the Wealthsimple platform with no access to the outside internet. The company also said it plans to allow clients to connect Wealthsimple accounts to external AI tools — including ChatGPT and Claude — by early 2027, with access levels set by the client.

Penny's design is a useful reference point for the industry debate about how much autonomy to extend to AI in consumer finance. The human-confirmation requirement for every execution step is the right baseline, but firms planning similar products should also consider: how confirmation fatigue affects real-world safety, what the liability position is when a client confirms an AI-staged action that turns out to be incorrect, how the system handles partial execution or time-sensitive opportunities, and what disclosure is required when AI is involved in suitability-adjacent recommendations.

### Forrester: One-Third of Banks Will Announce AI Transformation in 2027, but Only 5% Will Scale It

**Source:** [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/04/financial-services-forrester-research-ai-predictions-thecube/)  •  **Date:** November 4, 2026

Forrester's "Predictions 2027: Banking and Investing" report, based on 915 respondents across the US, UK, and Canada, forecasts a sharp bifurcation in bank AI outcomes next year. One-third of banks will announce AI transformation programs, but only 5% will succeed in scaling them — with legacy processes identified as the primary barrier preventing the majority from moving beyond proofs of concept and isolated pilots. The report also predicts that 40% of banks will launch consumer-facing AI assistants in 2027, and that AI-related banking technology M&A will increase 50% year over year. Forrester frames the transition as a shift from "digital banking" to "intelligent finance," in which intelligence rather than digital delivery becomes the primary source of competitive differentiation.

The 95% failure rate on scaling is consistent with prior enterprise technology transformations but should not be treated as a reason to slow down — it is a reason to build differently. Banks that succeed will likely share a common pattern: AI initiatives led by business owners with real P&L accountability, a workflow-level integration target rather than a platform-level build, and a governance structure that can keep pace with model iteration without slowing deployment to the cadence of annual model risk reviews. The M&A prediction also matters: banks that cannot build will buy, and due diligence on AI acquisitions requires evaluating model quality, data dependency, regulatory posture, and key-person concentration — not just revenue multiples.
