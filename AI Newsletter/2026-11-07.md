### CERT-In Escalates AI-Assisted BFSI Payment Attacks

**Source:** [CERT-In Advisory CIAD-2026-0058](https://cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0058&pageid=PUBVLNOTES02) | **Date:** November 4, 2026

India's Computer Emergency Response Team issued a critical update on an active campaign it calls CRAFTY FALCON. CERT-In says the AI-assisted threat actor surveys targets with machine intelligence, adapts attack scripts at machine speed, and invokes victims' own payment and disbursement APIs. The advisory covers NBFCs, digital lenders, fintechs, payment aggregators, payment service providers, and crypto exchanges. It says entities that had already remediated after an earlier warning were deliberately retargeted when remediation was incomplete. This is CERT-In's account of reported incidents. The public advisory does not quantify total losses.

The practical warning is sharper than a generic AI-cyber headline. Firms should assume that a valid API credential and a whitelisted execution path can still be used to bypass the business process that was supposed to authorize a payout. CERT-In specifically recommends server-side validation, maker-checker approval, reconciliation, new-beneficiary cooling periods, transaction velocity controls, and alerts for small test payments followed by large transfers. Payment and lending executives should test those controls against direct API invocation, not just the normal front-end journey.

### Anthropic Puts Read-Only Bank Data Inside Claude

**Source:** [Claude by Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-personal-finance-in-claude) | **Date:** November 5, 2026

Anthropic began rolling out Personal Finance in Claude as a US beta for Pro, Max, and Ultra subscribers. Users can connect selected bank, credit-card, loan, and investment accounts through Plaid and ask about balances, spending, bills, and trends. Anthropic says access is read-only, Claude does not see the bank password, and the product can access up to two years of transactions. Conversations inside the Personal Finance hub are not used to train Anthropic's models, while normal privacy settings apply to information shared elsewhere in Claude.

Read-only does not mean low-impact. A general-purpose AI assistant now sits directly on top of categorized transaction history, which moves financial guidance and customer attention outside many banks' own interfaces. Banks should review consent language, revocation and support flows, data retention, aggregation errors, complaint handling, and the boundary between financial education and regulated advice. The product is a beta and does not let Claude move or invest money, but it establishes a distribution path that banks will need to understand before action permissions arrive.

### Wealthsimple Introduces Penny With Approval on Every Move

**Source:** [Wealthsimple](https://newsroom.wealthsimple.com/wealthsimple-announces-ai-assistant-penny-and-11-more-releases-at-wealthsimple-presents-something-good-is-going-to-happen) | **Date:** November 5, 2026

Wealthsimple announced Penny, an AI assistant designed to understand a client's broader financial context and help with e-Transfers, wires, spending insights, duplicate-charge flags, invoice payments, and trading across stocks, ETFs, and other assets. Wealthsimple says Penny will not move money, place a trade, or complete another action without client confirmation, and that the interaction stays inside Wealthsimple's environment. The release describes an announced product capability and roadmap, not independently measured production results. Wealthsimple also says clients will eventually be able to connect accounts to ChatGPT or Claude, with setup approval and extra checks for critical actions, targeted for early 2027.

This is a different model from an external assistant that only reads account data. Wealthsimple is keeping the action surface, approval step, and customer relationship inside the institution while using AI to prepare the work. The control design still needs precision: show the exact beneficiary, amount, asset, and timing at approval; prevent stale or altered context from being approved; preserve the full instruction and confirmation record; and make recovery and dispute handling as easy as the original action.

### BMO Adds Human-Reviewed Agent Assist to Credit-Card Servicing

**Source:** [BMO](https://newsroom.bmo.com/2026-11-06-BMO-Using-Agentic-AI-to-Help-Credit-Card-Clients-Get-Faster-Answers-and-Resolutions) | **Date:** November 6, 2026

BMO introduced AI Agent Assist for its Virtual Connect credit-card contact centre. The internal capability matches transactions, retrieves policy guidance, and prepares cases for employee review. BMO says the team handles more than 500,000 annual calls about credit-card transactions, with current calls averaging 12 to 14 minutes. The bank is targeting a 20% to 30% reduction for eligible calls and automatic processing of about 40% of eligible credit refund requests. Those are bank-reported targets, not audited outcomes. BMO says employees remain accountable for reviewing the generated information and making servicing decisions.

This is the more defensible path for high-volume servicing: use the agent to assemble evidence and navigate policy while keeping the decision with an accountable employee. The risk is concentrated in the supposedly routine cases. BMO should measure refund accuracy, missed entitlements, repeat contacts, vulnerable-customer outcomes, override rates, and drift in policy retrieval. The reusable capability matters, but reuse across products should follow separate validation and approval gates rather than treating a successful credit-card workflow as a universal control pattern.

### Kalepa Raises $75M to Expand AI Through P&C Underwriting

**Source:** [Kalepa](https://kalepa.com/news-posts/kalepa-raises-75-million-series-b) | **Date:** November 5, 2026

Kalepa announced a $75 million Series B at a company-reported $470 million valuation to expand its AI underwriting platform. The company describes a workflow spanning submission intake, clearance, triage, risk analysis, rating, binding, and portfolio management. Kalepa says its insurance-specific AI harness matches the level of autonomy to each task and its risk, with human approval where needed and results that can be traced and audited. The company and its insurer customers report 30% to 60% more premium per underwriter, a 58% reduction in quote time, a 960-basis-point improvement in combined ratio, and double the quote-to-bind rate. These performance figures are company or customer reported, not independently verified in the release.

The interesting claim is not the funding. It is the attempt to make autonomy a task-level underwriting decision rather than a single platform setting. Insurers evaluating similar systems should demand the baseline behind every percentage, the population and period measured, the effect of appetite and pricing changes, the override record, the model-change process, and the evidence an actuary or examiner can inspect later. A cited rationale is useful, but it does not replace governance over the risk selection decision itself.
