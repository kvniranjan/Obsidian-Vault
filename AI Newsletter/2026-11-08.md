### Singapore's MAS Launches Future of Finance Institute with Three AI Programmes

**Source:** [Monetary Authority of Singapore](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/future-of-finance-institute-commences-operations-starting-with-ai-in-finance)  •  **Date:** November 3, 2026

The Monetary Authority of Singapore announced that its Future of Finance Institute (FFI) has commenced operations, with AI as its founding focus area. The institute runs three programmes: PathFin.ai, a knowledge-sharing platform that will establish Communities of Practice across banking, insurance, capital markets, financial advisory, and payments; BuildFin.ai, which will work with financial institutions, technology firms, and researchers to develop good practices, industry standards, and common tooling specifically for agentic AI deployment; and SafeFin.ai, which will produce practical resources for implementing MAS's Guidelines on AI Risk Management, including an enhanced MindForge AI risk management toolkit covering AI agents. MAS Managing Director Chia Der Jiun described FFI as "a public-private platform for MAS, financial institutions, technology firms and the research community to pool expertise and collaborate in fast-moving technology areas such as AI." The institute also plans to expand into tokenisation at a later stage.

FFI is notable because it moves beyond policy guidance into shared tooling and practitioner standards — the kind of infrastructure that reduces the per-institution cost of building defensible AI governance. The BuildFin.ai pillar's explicit mandate to develop standards and tools for agentic AI deployment makes it a likely reference point for the region as agent-based systems enter regulated workflows. Firms with operations in Singapore should monitor FFI output from BuildFin.ai and SafeFin.ai closely and consider participating in the Communities of Practice.

### HSBC Announces Hong Kong AI and Emerging Technology Institute for Q1 2027

**Source:** [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hsbc-open-hong-kong-ai-115231144.html)  •  **Date:** November 2, 2026

HSBC CEO Georges Elhedery announced a new AI and Emerging Technology Institute in Hong Kong at Hong Kong Fintech Week, with a planned launch in the first quarter of 2027. Research priorities include AI safety, fraud detection, post-quantum cryptography, and blockchain. Elhedery also said HSBC is rolling out AI training to its roughly 200,000 global staff. Speaking at the same event, HKMA chief Eddie Yue noted that approximately 80% of Hong Kong banks are already using AI or generative AI, suggesting the city's banking sector has moved firmly past the pilot stage.

The HSBC announcement is consistent with an emerging pattern among globally active banks: standing up dedicated research capacity rather than relying on vendor roadmaps. The HKUST partnership gives HSBC access to academic talent in areas like post-quantum cryptography that go well beyond current GenAI use cases, suggesting the bank is positioning for a 3-5 year technology horizon. The 80% AI adoption figure from HKMA is a useful data point for institutions benchmarking regional penetration, though it covers both production deployments and earlier-stage use.

### Gallagher Re: AI Is Closing the Data Gap in Credit and Trade Finance Underwriting, but Won't Replace Underwriters

**Source:** [Insurance Business Magazine](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/reinsurance/news/breaking-news/ai-is-closing-the-data-gap-in-credit-and-trade-finance-underwriting-report-finds-596281.aspx)  •  **Date:** November 5, 2026

Gallagher Re's Q3 2026 InsurTech report concludes that AI is primarily accelerating data preparation and analysis in credit and trade finance underwriting rather than replacing human judgment — but signals a coming structural shift. The report argues that AI-driven models could eventually give credit and political risk underwriters the equivalent of a catastrophe model for sovereign default risk, a capability that currently does not exist. At the same time, re/insurance technology investment fell sharply in Q3: sector companies backed 25 technology deals, down from 51 in Q3 2025. Jonathan Allard, Gallagher Re's global head of credit, surety and political risk, is quoted as saying that AI's best near-term use in credit underwriting probably is not replacing underwriters — a signal that the leading reinsurance broker sees augmentation, not automation, as the operative model for now.

The sovereign default risk analogy is worth watching. If AI can produce probabilistic credit default models with the coverage and resolution of catastrophe models, it would materially change how political risk and trade credit are priced and aggregated. Banks with significant trade finance or export credit exposure should include this research thread in their model risk planning. The drop in insuretech investment may also indicate a consolidation phase: capital concentrating on fewer, more mature use cases rather than broad experimentation.

### BlackRock 2026 Global Insurance Report: C-Suites See More AI Progress Than Their Own Managers Do

**Source:** [Insurance Business Magazine](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-csuites-see-far-more-ai-progress-than-senior-managers-do-blackrock-survey-595151.aspx)  •  **Date:** October 27, 2026

BlackRock's 2026 Global Insurance Report, based on a July–August survey of 463 senior executives in 31 countries, found that C-suite respondents assess their organizations' AI progress significantly more favourably than senior managers and practitioners do — with the perception gap widest in underwriting, claims, and compliance. The report also found that 54% of respondents said AI is at scale or operational in underwriting and pricing, while regulatory and compliance concerns ranked as the top barrier to AI adoption at 49%. These are company-reported survey figures from a major institutional asset manager with an interest in insurance industry technology uptake; the methodology has not been independently audited.

The C-suite/practitioner gap is a governance risk as much as a measurement problem: boards that are more confident than their own operating teams will tend to approve faster rollouts with weaker change management, and the compliance concerns identified by practitioners may be downweighted in investment decisions. Insurance firms should treat the gap itself as a diagnostic signal and commission structured reviews of which AI use cases are genuinely at scale versus which are production in name only. The 49% citing regulatory/compliance as the top barrier also foreshadows the kind of adoption deceleration that tends to follow early regulatory guidance — prudent firms should be building the compliance documentation now.
