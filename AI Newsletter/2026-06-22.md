### Nacha Fraud-Monitoring Rules Reach Their Practical Compliance Date
**Source:** [Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-2) - **Date:** June 22, 2026

Nacha's second phase of fraud-monitoring rules reaches its practical compliance date today. The changes extend risk-based monitoring duties to ACH originators, third-party service providers, third-party senders, originating banks, and receiving banks. They also broaden the fraud definition to cover payments induced by false claims about identity, authority, affiliation, or account ownership.

This is not an AI mandate, but manual review will not scale across the expanded control perimeter. Banks and corporate payment teams using machine learning or behavioral analytics need documented thresholds, alert ownership, evidence retention, and escalation paths. A vendor score alone is not compliance. Institutions must be able to show how suspicious credit-push activity is identified and what happens after a signal fires.

### Cambridge Study Finds Agentic AI Is Already Active at 52% of Financial Firms
**Source:** [Cambridge Judge Business School](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/faculty-research/centres/alternative-finance/publications/2026-global-ai-in-financial-services-report/) - **Date:** June 2026

The 2026 Global AI in Financial Services Report, produced with partners including the BIS and IMF, finds that 81% of surveyed financial firms are adopting AI at some level. Agentic AI is already in active adoption at 52%, but only 23% report scaling or transforming use. The report also finds that 55% of industry respondents struggle to measure AI value, rising to 76% among large institutions.

The gap between adoption and measurable value is the important result. Boards should stop accepting deployment counts as proof of impact. Every material use case needs a baseline and a scorecard covering unit cost, cycle time, error rate, review burden, customer impact, and control exceptions. Otherwise, fast adoption can hide expensive experimentation and unmanaged operational risk.

### Andera Raises $37 Million to Automate Internal Audit With AI
**Source:** [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/andera-37m-series-a-ai-internal-audit) - **Date:** June 19, 2026

Andera raised a $37 million Series A led by Lightspeed Venture Partners for an AI-native internal-audit platform. The company targets evidence collection, control testing, and workpaper production, including source material held in spreadsheets, PDFs, screenshots, and journal entries.

Internal audit is document-heavy and repetitive, so the economics are obvious. The control risk is equally obvious: an agent can retrieve the wrong evidence or produce a confident but unsupported conclusion at scale. Audit leaders evaluating this category need traceable source links, reproducible testing, exception sampling, reviewer signoff, and a clear policy for conflicts between an agent's conclusion and an auditor's judgment.

### EBA Flags Frontier AI as a Cybersecurity Risk for Banks
**Source:** [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/eba-warns-advanced-ai-models-are-creating-new-cyber-risks-for-european-banks/) - **Date:** June 19, 2026

Coverage of the European Banking Authority's June risk assessment says increasingly capable AI models are raising supervisory concern because they can identify and exploit software weaknesses more effectively. The warning sits alongside banks' growing dependence on cloud providers, software vendors, payment networks, and other third parties.

Banks should treat frontier-model capability as a change to threat velocity, not just another item in the cyber-risk register. Patch windows, vendor monitoring, access controls, incident exercises, and recovery plans built around human-paced attacks will age badly. The immediate management question is whether critical suppliers can detect and contain machine-speed exploitation before it propagates into banking operations.

### Cisco Moves to Add Identity Context to Agentic Security Operations
**Source:** [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-to-acquire-widefield-security-to-boost-splunks-agentic-soc/) - **Date:** June 19, 2026

Cisco announced an agreement to acquire WideField Security and integrate its identity-lifecycle capabilities into Splunk's agentic security operations stack. WideField maps human and non-human identities, credentials, sessions, authentication weaknesses, and potential blast radius. Financial terms were not disclosed.

This matters to financial institutions because AI agents are privileged non-human identities. An agent allowed to retrieve customer records, initiate payments, or modify controls creates damage quickly if its credentials or instructions are compromised. Banks need continuous identity discovery, least-privilege access, session monitoring, and fast revocation before expanding agents into regulated workflows.
