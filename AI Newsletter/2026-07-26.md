### American Express Puts Insurance Around Agentic Payments
**Source:** [FinAi News](https://finainews.com/payments/american-express-to-insure-agentic-commerce/) - **Date:** July 24, 2026

FinAi News reported that American Express is using Amex Agent Purchase Protection to cover eligible card members from charges tied to AI agent error when a registered agent makes purchases on their behalf. The update came through second-quarter commentary from CEO Stephen Squeri, who linked agentic commerce risk to fraud, hallucinations, trust, and the need for consumer protections. Amex also said AI is being used across customer service, travel agents, marketing, fraud management, unstructured-data sorting, and coding productivity.

For payments leaders, this is the important part: agentic commerce will not scale on authorization mechanics alone. Networks and issuers need an operating model for agent registration, customer intent, dispute evidence, fraud signals, and loss allocation when software buys the wrong thing. Protection products can create trust, but they also force hard underwriting questions about which agents are eligible, which errors are covered, and how merchants prove what the customer actually approved.

### Valley Bank Puts AI Savings Into Earnings Discipline
**Source:** [FinAi News](https://finainews.com/banking/finance-tech-relationships-help-valley-bank-identify-best-ai-use-cases/) - **Date:** July 23, 2026

Valley Bank said it is using finance and technology relationships, including Valley Ventures and Bank Leumi ties, to identify practical AI use cases. Executives cited customer care agent support, machine-learning models for AML and fraud alerts, autonomous outbound voice agents for auto-loan reminders and collections, AI-driven lead targeting, customer insight consolidation, and agentic AI for parts of credit underwriting and loan servicing. The bank reported USD 3 million to USD 4 million of AI spend this year against USD 15 million in expense run-rate savings.

This is more useful than another generic "AI transformation" claim because it connects use cases to operating expense. Banking leaders should still be careful. Run-rate savings are not the same as durable risk-adjusted value. The right follow-up is to track complaint rates, collections conduct, underwriting overrides, AML alert quality, fraud false negatives, model drift, and whether human oversight is actually changing outcomes rather than just approving automated work faster.

### Ocrolus and Newrez Show Borrower-Facing Mortgage AI Moving Into the Funnel
**Source:** [FinAi News](https://finainews.com/lending/ocrolus-newrez-fill-need-for-customer-facing-ai-mortgage-tools/) - **Date:** July 24, 2026

FinAi News reported that mortgage lenders and fintechs are moving AI closer to borrowers. A Veterans United Home Loans survey cited in the article found 89% of prospective homebuyers were comfortable sharing personal information with a lender's AI assistant in exchange for mortgage advice, while nearly 70% were comfortable using AI to manage loan application documents. Ocrolus is developing a customer-facing AI agent, and Newrez has launched Rezi Mortgage Assistant, a ChatGPT-based tool grounded in Newrez guidelines.

Mortgage executives should treat this as a conversion and compliance issue at the same time. AI can educate borrowers earlier and reduce loan officer load, but it can also create fair-lending, UDAAP, privacy, disclosure, and suitability problems if advice drifts from approved policy. Lenders need grounded answers, clear handoff to licensed humans, audit logs, prompt and response retention, and monitoring for whether protected-class borrowers receive different explanations or friction.

### CSI Builds an AI Tiering Model to Control Token Costs
**Source:** [FinAi News](https://finainews.com/strategy/inside-csis-ai-strategy-to-manage-rising-token-costs/) - **Date:** July 24, 2026

Banking technology provider CSI described a three-tier AI deployment framework that assigns different models to different work instead of defaulting every task to the most expensive frontier model. CSI uses Claude for high-complexity work such as product requirements, dependency mapping, legacy application analysis, and advanced code development; open-source models for work such as UI construction, API specification writing, and MCP development; and Microsoft tools for general office productivity. CSI also said it is building a machine-learning payment reconciliation layer for QuickBooks, Xero, and NetSuite.

This is the cost-control conversation banks need now. AI governance cannot stop at model risk and data leakage. Finance, technology, and business owners need usage metering, model selection standards, value thresholds, and procurement discipline before token bills become another unmanaged cloud-cost problem. The better model is task fit: expensive models where judgment and complexity justify them, cheaper models where structured output and repeatability are enough.

### Cognitive Credit Connects Institutional Credit Data Directly Into Claude
**Source:** [Cognitive Credit](https://cognitivecredit.com/blog/cognitive-credit-launches-claude-connector) - **Date:** July 23, 2026

Cognitive Credit launched a Claude Connector that lets institutional credit desks access machine-extracted, audit-ready credit data and full source disclosures inside Claude Conversations and enterprise AI workflows. The connector supports queries across corporate bonds and leveraged loan issuers, years of financial data, risk factors, official filings, prospectuses, and presentations. Cognitive Credit says Claude outputs reference back to official source filings to support compliance and audit standards.

Credit research is a good test case for production finance AI because wrong data can move investment decisions quickly. The value is not just conversational access. It is controlled access to verified data, source documents, and reference trails. Banks, asset managers, and credit funds should use this standard broadly: no source lineage, no client-ready output, and no investment-committee reliance.
