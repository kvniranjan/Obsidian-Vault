### UK AI Adoption Plan Pushes Financial Services From Pilots Toward Sector Rules
**Source:** [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-plan-financial-services) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

HM Treasury published the Financial Services AI Adoption Plan from independent AI Champions Harriet Rees of Starling Bank and Dr Rohit Dhawan of Lloyds Banking Group. The plan makes ten recommendations across regulatory framework design, AI-powered financial advice, the regulatory perimeter, resilience, skills and talent, and agentic payments. The government accepted the recommendations aimed at it and said it will work with regulators and industry on next steps, including alongside the FCA Mills Review.

This is not another generic AI strategy. It is a sector operating agenda. Banks, insurers, asset managers, and payment firms should expect AI adoption to become more explicit in supervisory conversations: what is in production, where customer authority is delegated, which third parties matter, how resilience is tested, and whether staff can govern the systems they are using. The important takeaway is that safe adoption is being framed as a growth requirement, not only a compliance brake.

### ConnectOne Deploys nCino AI Agents Across Commercial Lending
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/ai-in-fintech/connectone-rolls-out-ncino-ai-agents-across-commercial-lending) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

ConnectOne Bank expanded its nCino partnership by launching two AI agents built on the nCino Agentic Operating System for commercial lending. The agents include 16 specialized skills aimed at high-friction lending tasks, with more capabilities planned. The bank also uses nCino's generative AI Banking Advisor and Knowledge Base, which nCino says helped cut document lookup time from about 20 minutes to about 30 seconds. ConnectOne has about USD 14 billion in assets and 57 branches across New York, New Jersey, and Long Island.

Commercial lending is a sensible place for agentic AI because the workflow is document-heavy, policy-heavy, and full of handoffs. But the risk is equally clear. If agents retrieve policy, summarize borrower files, or prepare credit work, banks need evidence trails, source citations, permission controls, and quality sampling. Speed matters, but commercial credit decisions still need explainable judgment and defensible exceptions.

### Plaid Tightens Identity Verification as New Account Fraud Grows
**Source:** [Plaid](https://plaid.com/blog/new-account-fraud-prevention-features-idv/) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

Plaid added new account fraud prevention features to its Identity Verification product, saying new account fraud was the only major fraud type to grow in both victim count and dollar losses last year. Plaid said fraudsters are using generative AI to alter ID documents in ways that are difficult for human reviewers to detect. The company is positioning the updates around real-time risk signals, document and behavior checks, and faster detection of synthetic or manipulated identities.

This is the current fraud problem in plain terms: onboarding controls built for bad scans and mismatched names are now facing machine-generated evidence. Banks and fintechs should not rely on document review alone. The stronger pattern combines authoritative data, device and behavior signals, document forensics, phone and email risk, liveness checks, and post-onboarding monitoring. The goal is not perfect prevention. It is fewer false clears, fewer false declines, and a clean audit trail when fraud gets through.

### Clutch Launches Lending Automation System for Credit Unions
**Source:** [FinTecBuzz](https://fintecbuzz.com/clutch-announced-the-launch-of-lending-automation-system-las/) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

Clutch announced general availability of its Lending Automation System for credit unions. The platform combines digital account opening and loan origination, Fastlane AI-powered decisioning, and structured fulfillment workflows for loans requiring human judgment. Clutch says a dozen credit union partners from USD 256 million to USD 6 billion in assets have selected the system, and that customers typically reach 70% to 85% automated decisioning across fraud and underwriting.

Credit unions need automation, but they cannot just copy fintech underwriting with a member-service label pasted on top. The interesting part is the split between automated decisions and staff-guided exceptions. That is where smaller institutions can get speed without pretending every edge case belongs to the model. Leadership should track approval quality, adverse action explanations, fair-lending sampling, fraud misses, manual-touch rate, and whether the system is actually improving funded-loan economics.

### Spreedly Launches Standalone Payment Vault for Agent-Ready Commerce
**Source:** [FinTecBuzz](https://fintecbuzz.com/spreedly-announced-the-launch-of-standalone-payment-vault/) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

Spreedly launched a standalone payment vault that lets merchants store and control payment credentials without first adopting a full payments orchestration platform. The company says the vault supports PCI DSS Level 1 tokenization, portable credentials across more than 100 payment providers, built-in network tokenization, Account Updater services, and a path to orchestration without re-vaulting. Spreedly also said stored credential transactions account for 40% of transaction volume on its platform, up from 34% in 2022.

This matters because agentic commerce will put more pressure on credential control. If merchants, issuers, acquirers, and networks want AI agents to transact safely, they need portable credentials, scoped tokens, authorization context, and clean revocation paths. Payment credentials are no longer just storage plumbing. They are becoming a control layer for routing, fraud, liability, and agent permissions.
