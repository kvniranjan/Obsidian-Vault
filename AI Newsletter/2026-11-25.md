### FCA Draws a Regulatory Perimeter Around Agentic Finance

**Source:** [FCA, Our approach to Agentic AI](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai) | **Date:** November 24, 2026

The UK Financial Conduct Authority published specific expectations for firms, technology providers, and social media platforms developing or deploying agentic AI in financial services. It says an agent that initiates transactions, moves money, arranges borrowing, or makes investment decisions can cross into regulated activity. A technology company does not avoid authorisation simply by describing itself as a technology provider. The FCA also says regulated firms remain responsible for compliance when they develop agentic AI, deploy it, or rely on a third-party provider, and must understand autonomy, risks, and controls in normal and stressed conditions. This is regulatory guidance and supervisory positioning, not a new standalone AI rule.

The important change is that agent capability now determines the regulatory question. Firms should inventory agent tasks by regulated activity, define authority limits and stop conditions, retain evidence of inputs, permissions, actions and human interventions, and test redress before expanding autonomy. A polished personal assistant can become a payments, credit, advice, or financial-promotion product through incremental feature additions.

### Rabobank Moves Business Lending Toward a Single AI-Ready Platform

**Source:** [nCino offers lending platform to Rabobank](https://ncino.com/news/ncino-offers-lending-platform-rabobank-netherlands) | **Date:** November 25, 2026

nCino announced that it is offering its platform to Rabobank to consolidate business lending onto a single loan-origination foundation. The proposed setup would use automated, guided, and tailored journeys matched to borrower complexity, with relationship managers focusing on customer outcomes. nCino says embedded AI and governance capabilities will support responsible scaling across the lending lifecycle while keeping people in control of decisions, with transparency and auditability built in. The announcement discloses no contract value, implementation timeline, or go-live date, so this is a planned vendor and customer deployment rather than production evidence.

The sequence matters. Agentic lending on fragmented systems creates inconsistent data, permissions, and decision records before the model even enters the picture. Rabobank should define which steps are advisory, which can be automated, and which require credit authority. It also needs evidence that the platform preserves source data, policy versions, overrides, adverse-action reasoning, and rollback when an automated journey produces an incomplete or unsuitable application.

### Cashfree Tests a Bounded Wallet for AI-Agent Payments

**Source:** [Cashfree Wallet](https://www.cashfree.com/cashfree-wallet/) | **Date:** November 24, 2026

Cashfree launched an invite-only beta for a wallet that lets personal AI agents pay bills within user-defined rules. The company says users set a budget, payment count, and consent duration for each task; the agent never sees the user's card, UPI, or bank details; and users receive alerts and can revoke access. Bills are live, while travel, food, groceries, and shopping are listed as coming soon. Cashfree also says more than 100 agents and bot builders are integrated, which is a company-reported product claim, not evidence of transaction scale or safety.

A prepaid or segregated wallet is a more defensible authorization boundary than handing an agent unrestricted account credentials, but it does not solve the full payment problem. Cashfree and participating merchants still need clear rules for refunds, mistaken purchases, recurring bills, account takeover, sanctions and fraud monitoring, failed revocation, and customer support after a settled transaction. The liability model must be explicit before a bounded budget is mistaken for bounded consumer harm.

### BNY and Microsoft Put Human Approval Around Agent-Negotiated Treasury Trading

**Source:** [BNY and Microsoft complete first agentic negotiation of a U.S. Treasury trade](https://www.bny.com/corporate/global/en/about-us/newsroom/press-release/bny-and-microsoft-complete-first-agentic-negotiation-of-a-us-treasury-trade.html) | **Date:** November 17, 2026

BNY and Microsoft said they completed a pilot negotiation of a U.S. Treasury bill using AI agents. BNY's Agentic Negotiation Protocol verified participants, connected the agents through a secure gateway, checked counterparty and transaction details, and let the agents exchange offers within defined pricing limits. A central ledger compared the proposed terms from both sides. Human traders then reviewed and approved the terms, while execution and booking continued through existing systems. The industry-first label and control claims come from the companies, and this was a pilot rather than autonomous trade execution.

This is a useful bounded-autonomy pattern because the agents negotiate but do not hold final authority. The hard questions are now protocol questions: how identity and authority are proved across firms, how quote validity and market movement are handled, how the ledger captures rejected or malformed offers, and how a human reviewer proves that approval was informed. Capital-markets firms should treat the negotiation record as part of the trade record, not as disposable chat telemetry.

### OCC Gives Examiners a Framework for Bank Use of Agentic AI

**Source:** [OCC issues supervisory framework on bank use of Super Intelligence](https://occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-97.html) | **Date:** November 4, 2026

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued a framework for examiners evaluating banks' use of what it calls Super Intelligence, including agentic systems. The framework covers normal-course banking activities and their risks and controls. The OCC says examiners should not treat the technology as a separate risk category and banks should not be required to define a separate risk appetite for it. Instead, existing safety, soundness, legal, model, technology, compliance, and operational-risk expectations continue to apply. The OCC says it expects to update the framework as the technology evolves.

The practical message is blunt: novelty is not a control exemption. Banks should map each agent to the existing control owner and risk framework that governs its consequence, then produce evidence that permissions, data, testing, monitoring, incident response, vendor oversight, and human escalation work in production. Risk tiering should follow what the system can change or expose, not whether the vendor calls it an assistant, copilot, or agent.
