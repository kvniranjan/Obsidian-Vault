### Rabobank Bets on nCino to Unify Business Lending on a Single Agentic Platform

**Source:** [nCino press release](https://ncino.com/news/ncino-offers-lending-platform-rabobank-netherlands)  •  **Date:** November 25, 2026

nCino announced it is offering its Platform to Rabobank, a top-three Dutch bank by assets and a leader in business banking in the Netherlands. The goal is to consolidate Rabobank's fragmented lending systems into a single loan origination platform with automated, guided, and tailored journeys scaled to each borrower's complexity. nCino says embedded AI with built-in transparency, auditability, and governance controls will let Rabobank scale AI across the lending lifecycle while keeping relationship managers in control of decisions. No contract value, timeline, or go-live date was disclosed.

Consolidating origination onto one platform is a prerequisite before agentic AI can operate reliably across lending stages. Banks moving from siloed systems to a unified foundation should explicitly test which steps remain human-accountable and ensure the governance layer documents the model version, training data vintage, and decision rationale that applied at each credit event.

### Korea Fintech Week Makes Agentic AI Its Central Theme

**Source:** [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/11/25/ai-agents-take-over-investing-loans-at-korea-fintech-week)  •  **Date:** November 25, 2026

The 2026 Korea Fintech Week, running through November 27 at the aT Center in Seoul, opened with AI agents capable of completing customer financial tasks — not just recommending them — as its dominant theme. Woori Investment & Securities demonstrated an AI Studio that built a portfolio to a customer's return and risk targets and prepared the stock orders, leaving only final approval to the customer. Banksalad showed a loan-refinancing orchestration agent that coordinated several specialized sub-agents and found a refinancing option that would save a sample borrower 3.55 million won per year. KB Financial Group's "Kebby" senior-care robot integrated facial health checks with video banking for password recovery. Shinhan Financial Group introduced a wealth management AI agent, and Hana Financial Group presented an agent for government policy funding recommendations. Financial Services Commission chairman Lee Eok-won toured the demonstrations and signaled that regulations on network separation and data would be revised if sufficient safeguards could be shown.

The event is a demonstration stage, not a deployment audit. The control questions — what authorization a customer grants, how the agent records the mandate, how the bank handles an error or mis-execution, and what escalation path exists for each product — apply regardless of how smooth a live demo looks.

### AI Governance Is Now a Compliance Prerequisite, Not an Experiment

**Source:** [Fintech Global / Saifr analysis](https://fintech.global/2026/11/25/ai-governance-becomes-a-must-have-for-compliance-in-2027/)  •  **Date:** November 25, 2026

A Saifr analysis published November 25 identifies five trends shaping AI adoption in financial-services compliance for 2027. AI is moving from standalone tools to embedded intelligence across marketing reviews, KYC investigations, and communications surveillance, with firms increasingly judging tools by outcomes rather than alert volume. Regulators are not writing separate AI rules; they are applying existing obligations to AI-driven decisions, making firms accountable for outcomes whether a person or a machine made the call. The analysis says purpose-built, task-specific models are outperforming larger general models on specialized compliance work, and that signal intelligence — knowing which AML or financial-crime signals need action — is now the bottleneck, not data access. These are industry and vendor observations, not supervisory rules.

The governance inventory the analysis describes — AI system ownership, human review, vendor oversight, documentation — is not a future requirement. It is what regulators are already checking against existing laws. Compliance teams should build their AI inventory alongside their model inventory, not separately.

### Oliver Wyman Acquires Synpulse to Combine BFSI Strategy with AI Engineering

**Source:** [Oliver Wyman press release](https://www.oliverwyman.com/media-center/2026/nov/marshs-oliver-wyman-to-acquire-financial-services-and-technology-consultancy-synpulse.html)  •  **Date:** November 19, 2026

Marsh's Oliver Wyman agreed to acquire Synpulse, a technology consulting and AI platform company with roughly 1,200 professionals focused exclusively on banks, insurers, and wealth managers. Synpulse's capabilities include AI engineering, legacy modernization, responsible AI strategy, and digital ecosystem work. The two firms already operated combined client teams on end-to-end transformations. Oliver Wyman's CEO Ted Moynihan said the deal adds technology consulting and AI engineering depth; Synpulse's co-CEO Konrad Niggli said it provides scale to take on larger AI transformation engagements. Deal terms were not disclosed. Closing is expected in early 2027 pending regulatory approvals.

The acquisition reflects a broader consolidation in BFSI advisory: strategy advice is increasingly inseparable from the engineering needed to implement AI at scale. Clients evaluating such combined firms should ask where accountability sits when a strategy recommendation and its implementation both come from the same vendor, and what happens to engagement teams if the combined firm's incentives shift.
