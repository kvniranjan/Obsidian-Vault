### IMF and Bank Regulators Treat AI Cyber Risk as Financial Stability Risk
**Source:** [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/imf-ai-cyber-threats-impact-on-fintech-infrastructure) / [Reuters via KELO](https://kelo.com/2026/05/12/japans-bank-regulator-sets-up-forum-to-counter-mythos-powered-cyber-threats/) / [Economic Times](https://enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com/amp/news/industry/britains-bank-regulator-expects-quite-significant-disruption-from-latest-ai-models/131037247) - **Date:** May 12, 2026

The IMF warned that AI-powered cyberattacks could undermine financial stability by letting attackers find and exploit weaknesses across shared cloud providers, payment platforms, neobanks, software vendors, and financial market infrastructure. The concern is not theoretical. Japan's Financial Services Agency is setting up a 36-entity public-private working group with banks, the Bank of Japan, Anthropic's Japan unit, and OpenAI's Japan unit to address Mythos-related cyber risks. In the UK, Prudential Regulation Authority chief Sam Woods said the latest AI models could cause significant disruption because they can identify vulnerabilities faster than firms patch them.

This is the clearest regulatory signal today. AI cyber risk has moved beyond "security team problem" status. Regulators are now framing it as a resilience and systemic-risk issue because the same vulnerable vendor, core platform, cloud layer, or payment network can sit underneath many institutions. Banks need to stop pretending annual penetration tests and generic vendor questionnaires are enough. The serious work is continuous vulnerability management, kill-switch design, segmented recovery, cross-sector information sharing, and proof that critical services can keep running when AI accelerates the attack cycle.

### SAP Pushes Agentic AI Across CFO, Treasury, Tax, and Compliance Workflows
**Source:** [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/sap-ramps-push-autonomous-finance-agentic-ai/819929/) - **Date:** May 12, 2026

SAP is rolling out a finance-wide suite of agentic AI tools under its Autonomous Enterprise initiative. CFO Dive reports that the broader suite includes more than 50 domain-specific AI assistants coordinating subsets of more than 200 specialized agents, with coverage across financial close, FP&A, billing, governance, tax, compliance, accounts receivable, cash management, and treasury. SAP also introduced Joule Studio so users can build, deploy, and manage AI agents inside the business AI platform. The company says some capabilities arrive in Q2 and others in Q3.

This matters for banks because corporate finance workflows are becoming agent-operated from the ERP outward. Treasury services, payments, liquidity forecasting, receivables, and compliance will increasingly be initiated and monitored inside enterprise platforms, not bank portals. Banks that want to remain relevant to CFOs need APIs, embedded controls, real-time status, and clean data contracts that agents can use. The brutal version: if SAP becomes the finance team's AI operating layer and the bank remains a login screen, the bank loses strategic visibility.

### Global State of RegTech 2026 Says Enterprise Adoption Is Now Mainstream
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/05/12/what-is-the-global-state-of-regtech-in-2026/) - **Date:** May 12, 2026

RegTech Analyst and Parker & Lawrence Research released The Global State of RegTech 2026, based on survey responses from 300 senior compliance decision-makers, input from 100 RegTech vendors, regulator and expert interviews, and market analysis. FinTech Global reports that 95% of financial institutions said they have scaled enterprise use of RegTech across at least one regulatory domain. The report also introduces a taxonomy of 24 subcategories across six risk and compliance domains, with financial crime scoring highest on adoption at 68, followed by information and technology security at 61 and market conduct at 54.

The market is maturing, but that does not mean most institutions are good at this yet. Scaled use in one domain is not the same thing as integrated controls across onboarding, fraud, AML, sanctions, lending, market conduct, and reporting. The practical takeaway for bank leaders is simple: AI-enabled RegTech is becoming infrastructure. Point tools that cannot produce evidence, explain decisions, integrate into workflows, and survive model-risk scrutiny will get squeezed.

### Verifone Turns Fuel and Convenience Payments Into an AI Fraud Surface
**Source:** [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/verifone-advancing-fintech-at-the-forecourt-with-ai) - **Date:** May 12, 2026

Verifone is embedding AI into its payments infrastructure for petroleum and convenience retailers. Its Commander platform powers more than 45% of U.S. fuel and convenience locations and processes more than 35 million transactions daily. The company is applying AI across authorization, fraud detection, reconciliation, reporting, support workflows, and transaction-data analysis. Verifone's AI-powered fraud detection, planned through Commander Central, will score transaction risk in real time so low-risk payments can pass while anomalies are flagged before chargebacks or losses occur.

This is a good example of where payments AI is actually valuable: high-volume, low-margin environments where seconds and false positives matter. For financial institutions, the lesson is that fraud controls are moving directly into the payment flow and merchant operating system. That improves speed, but it also creates governance questions around model thresholds, merchant overrides, false positives, dispute evidence, and who is accountable when real-time decisions block or approve the wrong transaction.
