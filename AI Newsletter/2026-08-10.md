### UK AI Adoption Plan Puts Advice, Duty, and Accountability in Play
**Source:** [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/rsm-uk-welcomes-ai-adoption-plan-as-consumers-seek-ai-financial-advice/) - **Date:** August 10, 2026

The Fintech Times covered RSM UK's response to the UK government's Financial Services AI Adoption Plan, with a sharp focus on consumers using AI tools for financial guidance. RSM said the plan lands as firms move toward more autonomous AI-enabled decisioning, while Consumer Duty, operational resilience, the regulatory perimeter, and the Senior Managers and Certification Regime become the pressure points. The article also flags deepfakes, synthetic identities, and AI phishing as the downside of the same technology improving productivity and fraud detection.

The practical issue is not whether AI guidance is useful. It is who owns the outcome when an AI tool nudges a customer toward a pension, credit, savings, insurance, or investment decision. Boards should inventory where AI already influences customer outcomes, name accountable owners, and document why a journey is guidance, advice, execution, or servicing. Waiting for perfect FCA clarity is weak governance. Supervisors will still ask what the firm knew, what it controlled, and who was responsible.

### Accenture Says Agentic Commerce Threatens the Bank Front Door
**Source:** [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/banking/accenture-banking-trends-2026) - **Date:** 2026

Accenture's 2026 banking trends report argues that generative AI, agentic AI, digital assets, and new business models are breaking old constraints in banking. The report says USD 13 trillion in transaction value could shift to alternative payment methods by 2030, putting an estimated USD 13 billion in payment fees at risk. It also says 57% of business leaders believe agentic commerce will become mainstream within three years, while 76% of financial institutions still have work to do to enable smart money.

This is more than a strategy-deck trend. If customers and corporate treasurers let external agents compare accounts, initiate payments, optimize deposits, and choose financing, banks lose control of the interface where trust and margin are created. Banks should decide where they want to be issuer, custodian, rail, agent platform, or regulated control layer. The lazy answer is to bolt AI onto existing channels. The better answer is to design programmable money, consent, entitlements, fraud controls, and dispute handling together.

### Mortgage AI Governance Moves From Policy to Execution Evidence
**Source:** [Global FinTech Series](https://globalfintechseries.com/artificial-intelligence/crittora-calls-for-mortgage-ai-governance-to-move-from-written-policy-to-provable-execution-control/) - **Date:** August 10, 2026

Global FinTech Series reported on Crittora's AI authority-readiness campaign for mortgage lenders, servicers, and mortgage technology providers as Fannie Mae AI and machine-learning governance requirements take effect. The framing is blunt: written AI policy is not enough if a lender cannot prove what authority an AI system had, what it did, and which controls constrained execution.

Mortgage executives should treat this as a warning about evidence, not marketing. Loan origination and servicing workflows are full of regulated actions: borrower communication, document handling, underwriting support, pricing, exception routing, loss mitigation, and third-party data pulls. If AI assists or triggers any of those steps, lenders need authorization boundaries, approval thresholds, audit logs, exception reports, and kill-switch procedures. A clean policy binder will not save a firm that cannot reconstruct the AI decision path.
