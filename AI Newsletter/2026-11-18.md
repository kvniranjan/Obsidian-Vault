### OCC Issues Supervisory Framework for Bank AI Use

**Source:** [OCC Bulletin 2026-54](https://occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-54.html)  •  **Date:** November 4, 2026

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued Bulletin 2026-54 on November 4, giving bank examiners a 16-component framework organised around six pillars — purpose, risk, control, oversight, resilience, and assurance — for evaluating bank use of generative and agentic AI. The OCC follows the current executive branch convention of calling the technology "Super Intelligence" (SI). The bulletin explicitly states that the framework creates no supervisory expectations or enforceable requirements for banks, and that banks should not use it to assess their own programs. It is intended only as an examiner resource, and the OCC plans to update it as the technology evolves. Crucially, it fills a gap left by Bulletin 2026-13 (April 2026 model risk guidance), which had placed generative and agentic AI outside its scope.

The non-binding framing should not be read as low stakes. When examiners use a structured framework, examination findings will follow its categories even without a formal rule. Banks that have already built model inventory, governance, and operational-risk documentation will fare better than those relying on informal controls. The six pillars also offer a useful internal self-assessment structure: institutions should ask whether each pillar has a named owner, documented evidence, and a path to escalation before an examiner does it for them.

### Visa Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report: AI Is Automating Attack Operations

**Source:** [Visa PERC — Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/visa-report-ai-enabled-fraud-154000642.html)  •  **Date:** November 17, 2026

Visa's Payment Ecosystem Risk and Control team released its Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report during International Fraud Awareness Week, covering January through June 2026. Third-party provider incidents rose 62% over the prior six months, and Visa's data links these providers to roughly 56% of compromised payment credentials in the period. Visa Scam Disruption identified more than USD 1.4 billion in potential fraud, a 40% increase over the prior six months. Ransomware cases rose 19% over the same period and 312% since 2021, with the ten most active groups responsible for more than 80% of observed incidents. On the defensive side, Visa says its Risk Operations Center blocked 200% more enumeration transactions than in the prior six months, and losses from enumerated credentials fell 21.4% year over year.

The report's AI finding is calibrated: AI is not creating entirely new fraud categories but is reducing the human effort required for existing ones. Attackers use it for reconnaissance, vulnerability identification, multilingual phishing, refining malicious code, and testing payment controls across borders. Advanced AI agents capable of automating complex, adaptive attack chains are flagged as an emerging threat. For payment firms and issuers, the third-party concentration finding is the most actionable: more than half of credential compromise is now flowing through shared providers, so vendor oversight and incident notification speed matter as much as internal controls.

### IntellectAI Launches Xponent Portfolio Navigator for Commercial and Specialty Insurers

**Source:** [Fintech Global — IntellectAI unveils AI tool to steer insurance portfolios](https://fintech.global/2026/11/17/intellectai-unveils-ai-tool-to-steer-insurance-portfolios/)  •  **Date:** November 17, 2026

IntellectAI, the insurtech arm of Intellect Design Arena, launched Xponent Portfolio Navigator (XPN), an application that combines portfolio analytics, contextual AI, and underwriting workflow in a single environment for commercial and specialty insurers and reinsurers. The stated problem it solves is the lag between spotting a portfolio drift and acting on it: today, an underwriter typically generates a report, extracts data, and switches to a separate system to reprice, refer, or non-renew business — by which point losses may already have materialised. XPN allows portfolio-level actions on up to 30,000 policies simultaneously, with every figure traceable to the underlying policy, quote, and claims record, and each action routable to agentic workflows, referral underwriters, or the portfolio committee. The insurer determines whether AI agents execute the action or a human reviews it first. XPN is available as an add-on to Intellect Xponent or as a standalone product connected by APIs to policy administration and claims systems. No pricing or named customers are disclosed.

The design pattern here — keeping the insurer's decision authority explicit at each action boundary, with a full audit trail — is the right model for tools that can reprice or non-renew thousands of policies in a single session. The practical test for carriers evaluating similar products is whether the audit record is sufficient for a regulatory examination or a large-policyholder dispute, and whether the model's portfolio view matches what the claims and actuarial teams are seeing from the same data.

### Slice Insurance Puts Its MGA Operations into ChatGPT and Claude via MCP Server

**Source:** [Fintech Global — Slice pushes insurance into brokers' AI assistants](https://fintech.global/2026/11/18/slice-pushes-insurance-into-brokers-ai-assistants/)  •  **Date:** November 18, 2026

Slice Insurance Technologies, an AI-enabled managing general agent, moved its MCP server integration into live production for a selected group of agents, brokers, and wholesalers. Through the integration, users working in ChatGPT or Claude can access Slice's risk intelligence and insurance operations without leaving their AI platform. Internally, Slice Mind orchestrates tasks, the newly live RiskDNA module supplies structured risk data, and Insurance Cloud Services carries out transactions and keeps policy records current. All transactions still depend on underwriting rules and carrier authority, and each participant's access is scoped to their permissions. The integration currently covers Contractors General Liability, with an Excess product already available and commercial real estate planned next. This follows Slice's mid-market submission platform AI upgrades from July 2025. The company gave no participant counts, pricing, or authentication details beyond the ChatGPT and Claude platform names.

The MCP pattern — which Charles Schwab also used for retail brokerage accounts (covered November 16) — is becoming a standard method for embedding financial operations into general-purpose AI assistants. The control questions are consistent across implementations: how is the agent authenticated separately from the human, what is the precise action boundary before a transaction executes, how are errors and disputes resolved, and what log is retained to reconstruct what the agent did and on whose authority. Slice's access-scoping design is a reasonable start; the gap to watch is what happens when a broker makes a correct insurance placement from an incorrect extracted submission.
