### McKinsey: AI Agent Costs Could Reach 20% of Banks' Total Labor Spend

**Source:** [Global Banking & Finance Review](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/banks-seek-rein-soaring-ai-agent-costs-mckinsey/)  •  **Date:** November 10, 2026

McKinsey's Global Banking Annual Review warns that unchecked token spending on AI agents could easily reach 20% of a bank's total labor costs. McKinsey senior partner Vik Sohoni says early attempts to route queries across cheaper models failed because agents communicate with each other, compounding token consumption — single-agent workflows can cost $20,000–$30,000 per run, and multi-agent teams $100,000–$200,000. Banks are responding by forming dedicated AI FinOps teams and switching from broad rollouts to targeted deployments with measurable productivity or revenue payback.

The economics matter as much as the capability. A McKinsey survey of 46,000 retail banking customers found 72% would allow a banking agent to complete one financial task autonomously, but only within a $500 spending cap. Banks building out agent programs should model token cost trajectories alongside headcount savings, instrument agent communication overhead separately, and define a decision gate before scaling any multi-agent workflow.

### BioCatch Survey: 80% of Banks Have Already Faced Agentic AI Attacks

**Source:** [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/80-of-banks-have-already-faced-agentic-ai-attacks-according-to-biocatch-survey/)  •  **Date:** November 10, 2026

Behavioral-biometrics firm BioCatch published a 2026 survey of 1,440 fraud, AML, and risk-and-compliance professionals across banks in 25 countries. Eighty percent said their institutions had already encountered attacks using agentic AI, 84% called AI agents the industry's single greatest exploitable vulnerability over the coming year, and 72% expected it to be very difficult to distinguish legitimate AI-assisted activity from malicious or manipulated automation. BioCatch simultaneously launched AiRE (Agentic AI Risk Essentials), which it says can flag AI agents acting on behalf of account holders or bad actors before money moves, using behavioral signals that go beyond device and IP controls.

The liability gap is sharpening. Major UK banks including HSBC, Nationwide, NatWest, Barclays, and Lloyds reportedly blocked AI agents from customer accounts, while some retail brokers have done the opposite — connecting client accounts to agents via MCP integrations. Robinhood states customers are "solely responsible" for their agents' actions. Banks need a clear policy for which actions an agent may initiate on behalf of a customer, what behavioral evidence of authorization is preserved, and how liability is allocated when an agent is manipulated or misused.

### Dallas Fed Survey: Banks Drawing Access Lines for Customers' AI Agents

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/banking/2026/banks-draw-lines-around-customers-ai-agents/)  •  **Date:** November 9, 2026

A Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas survey of 70 bankers across its Eleventh District (collected October 27 through November 4) found that 11.4% said their institution or service provider had already restricted existing or prospective customers' AI agent activity on online banking platforms, and 22.9% planned to implement restrictions within six months. A further 27.1% said they did not know whether restrictions existed. The survey also found that 44.3% of respondents named cybersecurity and 41.4% named fraud among their institution's top-three concerns for the next six months. These are regional results, not a national measure.

The question of agent access is moving from product-team discussion to formal policy. The emerging distinction is between permissioned connections that a bank controls and unvetted third-party agents that authenticate using the customer's own credentials. Banks that have not yet decided should determine: what an agent is allowed to read versus act on, how delegation and revocation work, and what their fraud-liability position is when an agent the customer authorized is later manipulated.

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/geniosai-raises-19-1-million-to-bring-traceable-ai-analysis-to-financial-institutions/)  •  **Date:** November 9, 2026

Financial intelligence startup geniosAI emerged from stealth with $19.1 million in combined seed and Series A funding backed by FactSet, Zoom, and General Catalyst. The company's core pitch is that AI analysis in finance must preserve the assumptions, calculations, and review decisions behind an answer so employees can verify conclusions, challenge results, and build on earlier work. FactSet, which led the Series A and is a strategic partner, will bring its financial data into the platform; Zoom will support finance conversations such as earnings-call prep and internal reviews. The company says its technology is already used by finance and investment teams at Fortune 500 banks.

Traceability is becoming a commercial differentiator in financial AI, not just a regulatory compliance item. A PYMNTS Intelligence study found that fragmented or poor-quality data was the biggest obstacle to wider AI deployment, cited by 30% of financial-services leaders. GeniosAI's bet is that institutions will pay specifically for an audit trail that links an AI output back to the exact source, version, and reasoning step — particularly when those outputs feed earnings analysis, credit decisions, or investor communications.

### Oracle Launches AI-Assisted Analytics Applications for Banks and Insurers

**Source:** [PR Newswire / Oracle](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/oracle-helps-financial-institutions-make-faster-more-informed-treasury-and-customer-decisions-with-ai-assisted-analytics-302934373.html)  •  **Date:** November 10, 2026

Oracle announced general availability of two AI-assisted analytics applications built on Oracle Analytics Cloud: Oracle Financial Services Data Intelligence Treasury Analytics and Oracle Financial Services Data Intelligence Customer Analytics. Treasury Analytics covers liquidity, funding, cash flow, balance sheet performance, and market risk; Customer Analytics covers customer profitability, product adoption, portfolio performance, and retention. Both let business users query data in plain language, generate visualizations, and receive automatically surfaced patterns — without writing code or waiting for IT. The applications are designed to complement existing on-premises OFSAA environments as a cloud modernization path.

The practical test for these tools is whether business users trust and act on AI-surfaced insights or treat them as a richer dashboard. Oracle's senior vice president for product management said institutions need "faster answers they can trust" rather than more dashboards. Banks adopting these or similar tools should validate that natural-language query results match their own reconciled source data, define data refresh and lineage standards before allowing outputs to inform treasury or pricing decisions, and log which questions generated which decisions for auditability.
