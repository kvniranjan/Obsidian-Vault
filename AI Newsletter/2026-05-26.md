### Alipay Launches AI Wallet, Token Pay, and China's First Agentic Commerce Trust Protocol
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/alipay-launches-full-stack-ai-payment-suite-with-ai-wallet-and-token-pay)  •  **Date:** May 26, 2026

Alipay introduced a full-stack AI payment suite built for agentic commerce. AI Wallet lets users monitor and authorise payments initiated by AI agents directly inside the Alipay app, keeping human oversight in the loop while enabling autonomous execution. Token Pay targets AI model companies, providing subscription and token top-up payment infrastructure designed for machine-speed billing cycles. Alipay also established what it calls China's first Agentic Commerce Trust Protocol, a standardised framework governing interactions between AI agents and service providers, and announced a developer programme offering zero processing fees and token incentives to seed adoption. The platform claims compatibility with approximately 95% of OpenClaw-type AI agents.

This is the most consequential agentic payments move to come out of China, and it deserves more attention from Western payment strategists than it will get this week. Alipay operates at a scale most Western networks cannot match. By publishing a trust protocol now — before global standards are agreed — it is shaping the reference implementation for how a high-volume payment ecosystem handles agent identity, delegation, and merchant trust. Banks and payment networks outside China should read this as a signal that the window for influencing global agentic commerce standards is closing fast.

### Didit Raises USD 7.5 Million to Build AI-Native Identity Infrastructure
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/didit-raises-usd-75-million-to-build-ai-native-identity-infrastructure)  •  **Date:** May 26, 2026

Didit closed a USD 7.5 million seed round backed by Y Combinator, Pioneer Fund, Orange Collective, and Founders Future. The platform analyses more than 200 signals per verification session, covering document authenticity, biometric liveness, injection attack detection, and deepfake analysis, and connects to government data sources globally to validate identity against authoritative registries rather than self-reported documents alone. The company is positioning itself as identity infrastructure rather than a point solution, with a planned reusable credential wallet that would allow individuals to verify once and present credentials across multiple platforms without repeated checks.

The identity problem is escalating on two fronts simultaneously. AI tools are making synthetic identities, document forgeries, and biometric spoofing cheaper and more convincing, while agentic commerce and embedded finance are multiplying the surfaces where identity decisions happen without a human reviewing the output. Fourteen months ago, liveness detection was considered a strong control. Today banks need injection-attack awareness and deepfake analysis in the same verification flow. Didit's 200-signal approach and government-source anchoring are the right direction. The due diligence question for banks considering integration is whether the explainability, appeal path, and adverse-action documentation hold up under consumer protection and fair lending obligations.

### Only One in Three UK Consumers Comfortable With AI in Insurance
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/05/25/one-in-three-uk-customers-open-to-ai-in-insurance/)  •  **Date:** May 25, 2026

Guidewire's 2026 European Insurance Consumer Survey found that roughly 30% of UK respondents are comfortable with AI determining the price of a policy, 38% welcome AI assistance completing documents and applications, and 39% accept AI supporting human call handlers. Comfort rises sharply among daily AI users: 63% of that group trust fully automated pricing versus 30% of the general population, a 2:1 gap that reflects how lived experience with AI tools shapes tolerance for AI decisions. The three conditions consumers attach to AI acceptance are human override capability (33%), transparency about how AI is used (26%), and independent third-party regulation (23%).

The gap between where insurers are deploying AI and where consumer acceptance sits is not a communications problem. It is a design constraint. Roughly two-thirds of UK consumers are not comfortable with AI making pricing decisions that determine what they pay for coverage, and they are making acceptance conditional on human oversight, clear disclosure, and external accountability. Insurers that skip past these conditions in the name of speed will accumulate a latent pool of customer grievances that regulators, claims disputes, and renewal churn will eventually surface. The survey also carries a direct signal for banks: AI-driven underwriting, credit pricing, and product eligibility face the same trust deficit and the same governance requirements. Building for it now is cheaper than rebuilding after a complaint campaign.

### MoonPay Trade Opens a Single API to 200+ Blockchains for Institutional Execution
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/crypto-web3-and-cbdc/news/moonpay-launches-trade-platform-for-cross-chain-institutional-execution)  •  **Date:** May 26, 2026

MoonPay launched Trade, a platform that gives financial institutions access to digital asset markets across more than 200 blockchains through a single API, with settlement and conversion across 120-plus fiat currencies. Built on technology acquired from Decent.xyz, a Y Combinator-backed cross-chain routing company, the platform includes routing across Ethereum, Solana, Base, Hyperliquid, and Bitcoin, plus a proprietary automated market maker for stablecoin trading that delivers 1:1 quotes. Trade is the sixth acquisition MoonPay has made over 18 months and sits alongside its existing on-ramp infrastructure and the ChatGPT consumer crypto channel announced yesterday.

Institutional digital asset adoption has stalled at many banks and asset managers not because of internal appetite but because multi-chain fragmentation makes execution, custody, settlement, and reporting prohibitively complex. A single-API abstraction layer directly attacks that operational blocker. The questions for compliance and operations teams evaluating Trade are predictable but important: how AML screening works across chains, what the settlement finality guarantees are per rail, how reconciliation data is formatted for bank ledger systems, and whether fiat conversion creates FX or liquidity risk that needs to be hedged separately. The market infrastructure story in digital assets is moving faster than the risk infrastructure around it.

### In Payments, the AI Battle Is Commercial, Not Technological
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/payments-innovation/2026/legacy-payments-tech-gets-an-ai-era-rebrand/)  •  **Date:** May 26, 2026

Spreedly CEO Justin Benson argues in a PYMNTS interview that legacy payments infrastructure is a genuine competitive advantage rather than an obstacle — that incumbents survived by solving hard problems of scale, security, and resilience that new entrants have not yet been tested on. Benson's view is that the real gap is not technology but commercial models and customer relationships, and that AI could actually benefit established providers by reducing integration friction and unlocking the data advantages they already hold. He frames the most consequential question in agentic commerce as: who owns the transaction?

This framing matters for banking strategy. Every large incumbent network and processor has spent the past two years watching AI-native startups build faster and newer, and drawing conclusions about technology debt. Benson's counterargument is that when AI agents are the buyer, the trust, dispute resolution, and settlement infrastructure already owned by established networks becomes more valuable, not less — provided those networks use AI to modernise the interface layer without waiting to replace the underlying rails. The uncomfortable version of this for banks and processors is that the commercial stagnation risk is real even if the technical obsolescence risk is overstated. Relationships and pricing structures built for human-initiated transactions will need to be renegotiated when AI agents are the counterparty.
