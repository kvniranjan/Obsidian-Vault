### Strivve Moves Issuer Card Placement Into Agentic Checkout
**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/strivve-extends-top-of-wallet-to-agentic-commerce--making-the-issuers-card-the-one-the-ai-agent-uses-302828656.html) - **Date:** July 17, 2026

Strivve extended its Top of Wallet card-on-file platform to agentic commerce, aiming to make an issuer's card the default payment method when AI assistants shop and pay on behalf of consumers. The company said the capability works across long-tail merchant sites, uses its existing PCI DSS compliant placement engine, and implements Visa-led Trusted Agent Protocol concepts so merchants can confirm that an agent is authorized and acting with cardholder consent.

This is the issuer-side fight that banks and credit unions should take seriously. If AI agents become the shopping interface, the cardholder may stop actively choosing a card at checkout. Issuers need a strategy for agent identity, card-on-file placement, consent evidence, transaction auditability, dispute handling, and merchant acceptance. Waiting for networks or wallets to solve all of it is a good way to lose transaction primacy.

### Banco do Brasil Puts Agentic AI Inside Relationship Banking Workflows
**Source:** [StreetInsider / Business Wire](https://www.streetinsider.com/Business%2BWire/Banco%2Bdo%2BBrasil%2BEmbeds%2BAgentic%2BAI%2Binto%2BCore%2BWorkflows%2Bto%2BStrengthen%2BRelationship%2BBanking%2Band%2BCustomer%2BEngagement/26765046.html) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

Banco do Brasil is deploying NiCE Copilot inside the CXone workspace used by relationship managers and banking assistants. The deployment supports more than 90 million customers and gives employees AI-powered summaries, customer history, sentiment analysis, contextual guidance, and traceable records across customer interactions. NiCE said the pilot is showing productivity gains, reduced rework, better consistency, and improved organization of customer information.

The important point is not another bank chatbot. Banco do Brasil is applying AI to bankers first, where regulated customer conversations remain human-led but less fragmented. That is the right posture for high-volume relationship banking: summarize, cite, organize, and write back to CRM before letting AI speak or act directly for the bank. Leaders should watch whether the productivity claims survive quality sampling and conduct-risk review.

### INETCO Adds Agentic AI to Payment Fraud Investigation
**Source:** [The Green Sheet](https://greensheet.com/newswire%26article_id%3D64236) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

INETCO launched BullzAI Investigate, an AI-assisted fraud investigation module for banks and payment service providers. The system uses agentic AI fraud agents to collate transaction data, triage alerts, prioritize high-risk cases, and produce explainable risk scores while keeping sensitive data on-premise and analysts in control. INETCO said early results include reducing fraud investigation time from 10 to 30 minutes to about 20 seconds, with recommendation precision around 95%.

Fraud teams need this kind of workload relief, but the governance cannot be vague. Faster triage is useful only if investigators can see why a case was prioritized, override the system cleanly, and feed outcomes back into the model. Banks should also verify that on-premise language models do not become a blind spot for patching, testing, or model drift.

### Entrust Frames Agentic AI as an Identity and Authorization Problem
**Source:** [Finovate](https://finovate.com/category/entrust-datacard/) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

Entrust launched an Agentic AI Trust Accelerator to help enterprises build infrastructure for autonomous AI agents. The program focuses on four pillars: identity, authorization, cryptographic assurance, and accountability. Finovate reported that the program is designed to help organizations verify human users and AI agents, limit agent actions by roles and policies, secure actions through mechanisms such as digital signing, and create records that support audits and regulatory requirements.

This is the right framing for banks. The agent risk is not just model output quality. It is whether the institution can prove who authorized the agent, what the agent was allowed to do, what it actually did, and which human or control owner remains accountable. Any bank moving agents beyond back-office draft work should treat identity and authorization as production infrastructure, not an afterthought.

### FinTech Funding Rebounds, But Capital Is Concentrating Around AI and Infrastructure
**Source:** [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/fintech/funding-rises-deals-slump-h1-2026/) - **Date:** July 15, 2026

Crunchbase reported that venture-backed fintech startups raised USD 28.6 billion globally in the first half of 2026, up 22.7% year over year, while deal count fell 25.7% to 1,605 deals. The article said investors are concentrating larger checks in areas such as wealth management, financial infrastructure, and enterprise automation, with the U.S. receiving more than 52% of global fintech funding.

This is not a broad easy-money recovery. It is a concentration cycle. AI-native financial infrastructure, workflow automation, identity, compliance, and vertical software are getting capital because buyers have real cost pressure and risk pressure. Banks should expect stronger vendors to emerge, but also more aggressive sales claims. Procurement teams need evidence: live deployments, security controls, explainability, integration cost, and measurable operating lift.
