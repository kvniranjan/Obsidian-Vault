### Wells Fargo Makes Customers Liable for AI Agent Transactions

**Source:** [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/wells-fargo-wants-customers-to-prove-theyve-revoked-ai-agents)  •  **Date:** November 2026 (terms effective November 24, 2026)

Wells Fargo added a Section 15 on third-party AI agents to its Online Access Agreement, effective November 24, 2026. Customers who continue using its online or mobile services accept that they bear liability for unintended actions, errors from an agent's interpretation of instructions, actions based on manipulated inputs, and cases in which an agent exceeds the authority it was granted — a provision that explicitly covers prompt injection. The bank says it does not monitor, screen, or review AI agent activity, and customers must retain proof of agent revocation and provide it to the bank on request. The agreement also reserves the bank's right to limit, suspend, or terminate agent access and to require agent registration. How Regulation E applies to agent-initiated transactions remains a live legal question.

This is among the first explicit AI agent liability terms from a major US retail bank, and it sets a precedent the industry is likely to follow. For consumers, the practical risk is an agent that acts on a misread instruction or is manipulated by a third-party prompt injection, with the customer bearing the dispute burden. For banks deploying or accepting agent access, these terms underscore the need for mandatory agent registration, granular permission scopes, audit trails for each agent action, and a clear revocation pathway that customers can actually use.

### VisionX AI Launches AEGIS, a Large Graph Model for Financial Crime

**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/11/20/visionx-ai-bets-on-graph-ai-to-expose-hidden-crime-networks/)  •  **Date:** November 20, 2026

Singapore-based RegTech firm VisionX AI, a spin-out of Alpha Ladder Group, launched AEGIS (Attention-based Entity and Graph Intelligence System), a proprietary Large Graph Model pretrained on financial-transaction graph data. Unlike tools that assess individual payments, AEGIS learns the structural relationships among wallets, accounts, and entities to surface concealed networks and unusual fund movements. VisionX pairs AEGIS with third-party LLMs in a hybrid architecture: the graph model supplies network intelligence while the LLMs handle reasoning and investigative workflows. The first product is TraceX, a blockchain tracing and investigation platform that follows funds across linked addresses, flags suspicious cross-case patterns, and produces evidence-backed leads with human oversight retained. VisionX's KYT product already covers 1 billion wallets across 25 chains. No paying client names or model accuracy benchmarks are disclosed.

Graph-structure models represent a meaningful departure from per-transaction scoring: a network that reveals a money-mule cluster or cross-case connection may be more valuable than marginal improvements in individual-transaction detection rates. Banks and law enforcement evaluating this approach should request reproducible comparisons against their own baseline detection rates, false-positive distributions by entity type, evidence on cross-chain coverage gaps, and a clear account of what human investigators are expected to verify before acting on a machine-generated lead.

### Evident Publishes the First AI Maturity Index for Asset Managers

**Source:** [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/11/19/new-ai-index-for-asset-management-launches/)  •  **Date:** November 19, 2026

Evident, an AI benchmarking and intelligence company, released the inaugural Evident AI Index for Asset Management, scoring 30 of the world's largest asset managers across four pillars: Talent, Innovation, Leadership, and Transparency, using only publicly disclosed data. BlackRock ranked first across all four pillars and was highlighted for its agentic research platform, Asimov. Vanguard ranked second, leading on Leadership and disclosing more AI use cases than any other firm. Fidelity ranked third, accounting for 34% of AI workers, 43% of AI research output, and 78% of AI patents across the index. Hedge funds Bridgewater and Man Group ranked fourth and fifth, leading on depth of AI use in the investment function itself. The index identified 320 publicly disclosed AI use cases across the 30 firms; the top nine firms represent 30% of the index but 45% of those disclosures. Only three firms — AllianceBernstein, Carlyle Group (USD 470 million), and Vanguard (USD 450 million) — have disclosed realized AI returns, compared with 26% of banks in Evident's parallel banking index. Evident's co-CEO noted that evidence shows AI improving investment research but that it is not yet proof of alpha.

The low rate of return disclosure is the signal worth watching. Asset managers are building capability at scale — Fidelity's patent and talent concentration is striking — but only three of the 30 largest firms have put a dollar figure on outcomes. Managers should treat the index as a prompt to match their public disclosure to their internal evidence, and to close the gap between capability investment and demonstrated client benefit before regulators or clients ask.

### State Bank Examiners Now Have a Formal AI Supervision Playbook

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/state-financial-regulators-bring-ai-into-the-examination-process/)  •  **Date:** November 16, 2026

The Conference of State Bank Supervisors published an AI Supervisory Framework in September 2026 that gives state examiners a standardized, step-by-step approach to reviewing AI use at regulated banks and nonbank institutions. The framework includes initial scoping questions, document request lists, examination procedures, illustrative high-risk use case profiles, and supplementary guidance specifically for agentic AI and third-party AI tools — which the framework identifies for heightened scrutiny. It draws on existing federal and industry AI frameworks and is designed to complement, not replace, federal guidance. Michigan, Connecticut, and New York have each separately issued state-level AI guidance covering accuracy and anti-discrimination requirements, governance program obligations, and cybersecurity risk assessment duties. These are framework and guidance documents; they are not rules carrying penalties of their own.

For compliance teams, this means state examiners will arrive with structured questions about AI inventory, policies, monitoring, and third-party vendor oversight. Institutions that have not yet documented where AI appears in their products, services, and operations should do so before the next examination cycle. The specific focus on agentic AI in the CSBS framework also signals that authorization scope, action logging, and human-override capabilities are likely to be direct examination topics, not just general risk-management considerations.

### FIS CEO: Trust Is the Architecture Problem, Not Just the Marketing Message

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/fis-ceo-says-ai-raises-the-stakes-for-trust-in-financial-services/)  •  **Date:** November 19, 2026

FIS CEO Stephanie Ferris spoke at The Clearing House annual conference on November 18. She said the company's AML agent — which gathers records and recommends whether to file a suspicious activity report — reduced manual data retrieval by 90%, but that tests revealed the agent and human reviewers sometimes gave different reasoning for the same filing decision; the human reviewer's reasoning reflected the institution's own risk policy, which the model did not fully capture. She said banks' boards are asking how fast to adopt AI, what an AI-enabled bank will look like, and how much cyber risk they already carry. On agentic commerce, she argued that permissioning is the foundational problem: a bank must settle what a customer has actually authorized before an agent can act on their account, including whether the customer wants the agent able to move all their money instantly. FIS's systems support 14% of the world's economy. On stablecoins and tokenized deposits, she said customer demand for stablecoins is limited but banks want the capability ready for RFPs; she sees more near-term interest in tokenized deposits for reducing the bank's own transaction costs.

The AML anecdote carries a broader lesson: a 90% efficiency gain in data retrieval does not validate the downstream recommendation if the model's reasoning does not match the institution's risk policy. Banks should require that model reasoning be auditable against their own written policies, and that compliance officers can explain a filing decision to regulators without relying on a system they cannot interrogate. The permissioning point Ferris raised echoes the Wells Fargo terms story: the industry does not yet have a settled answer on what a customer's agent authorization actually covers.
