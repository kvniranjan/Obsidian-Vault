### FSB Turns Responsible AI Adoption Into a Board-Level Control Agenda
**Source:** [Financial Stability Board](https://www.fsb.org/2026/06/sound-practices-for-responsible-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-consultation-report/) - **Date:** June 10, 2026

The Financial Stability Board opened consultation on sound practices for responsible AI adoption by financial institutions. The report proposes a menu of 12 practices covering organisation-wide AI governance and the relevant stages of the AI lifecycle. It explicitly asks whether the practices address newer forms of AI, including generative AI and agentic AI, and invites responses by July 22, 2026. The FSB frames the issue as both firm-level risk management and financial-stability risk, not just technology governance.

This is the most important governance signal of the week. The FSB is not writing a vendor checklist. It is telling boards and senior management that AI adoption now belongs inside strategy, technology adoption, risk management, and lifecycle controls. Banks, insurers, asset managers, and payment firms should treat this as a prompt to check whether AI inventories, model ownership, third-party dependencies, incident reporting, audit trails, and human accountability are real or just policy language. If the board cannot see where AI is active and who owns the risk, the governance program is not serious.

### Coinbase Lets AI Agents Trade and Spend From User Accounts
**Source:** [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/coinbase-for-agents) / [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/06/11/coinbase-launches-ai-agent-accounts-that-can-trade-and-spend-on-your-behalf) - **Date:** June 11, 2026

Coinbase launched Coinbase for Agents, available as an MCP and CLI, allowing users to connect AI agents directly to Coinbase accounts so agents can trade, pay, and execute workflows within user-defined limits. CoinDesk reported that the product lets agents access market data, trade spot crypto and derivatives, pay for online services, and eventually support purchases, with planned support for equities and prediction markets. Coinbase is also tying the product to x402, its machine-to-machine payments protocol for small payments to services such as research, APIs, and compute.

This is a cleaner example of agentic finance than another chatbot shopping demo. The agent is being connected to a regulated financial account and given execution capacity. That creates obvious control questions: portfolio isolation, spend caps, trade limits, suitability, customer authentication, error reversals, suspicious activity monitoring, and what happens when the user blames the agent. Banks and brokerages should assume customers will soon expect third-party agents to connect to financial accounts. Blocking that forever is not a strategy. Governing delegated authority is.

### Getnet Builds Merchant Infrastructure for AI Agent-Initiated Payments
**Source:** [Santander](https://www.santander.com/en/press-room/press-releases/2026/06/getnet-develops-infrastructure-that-enables-businesses-to-accept-ai-agent-initiated-payments) / [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/getnet-launches-infrastructure-for-ai-agent-initiated-payments) - **Date:** June 9-10, 2026

Santander's merchant payments platform Getnet developed infrastructure that lets businesses accept and process payments initiated by AI agents through a single integration. The release says the system includes identification and authentication mechanisms, is protocol-agnostic, and is built on open standards. Getnet, Mastercard, and Mexican startup Neivor completed a real-world case in Mexico and Latin America using Mastercard Agent Pay.

Yesterday's Pine Labs story showed conditional AI-triggered payments on UPI mandates. Getnet shows the merchant-acquiring side of the same shift. Agentic payments will not work if only issuers and networks adapt. Merchants and acquirers need to know whether the payer is a human, an authorized agent, a compromised agent, or a bot pretending to be one. Payment acceptance is becoming an identity and evidence problem. Acquirers that cannot pass agent context, mandate scope, authentication evidence, and dispute metadata will be weak links.

### ING Starts Scaling an Agentic Mortgage Assistant
**Source:** [ING](https://ing.com/news/2026/how-ing-uses-agentic-ai-to-speed-up-mortgage-decisions.html) / [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/how-ing-uses-agentic-ai-to-speed-up-mortgage-decisions/) - **Date:** June 8-10, 2026

ING introduced an agentic AI assistant to speed mortgage applications in the Netherlands after a pilot launched in March 2026. The assistant analyses mortgage applications, helps explain possible outcomes, and suggests ways to move cases forward, especially where manual assessment would normally be needed. ING says an employee remains responsible for the assessment and makes the final decision on every mortgage application. The bank plans gradual scaling as the assistant takes on more operational tasks.

This is a real lending workflow, not generic productivity AI. Mortgage decisioning is full of messy documents, affordability checks, exceptions, broker interactions, and conduct risk. The attractive part is faster processing and more consistent case handling. The risk is that "suggesting ways to move cases forward" can become de facto decisioning if staff rubber-stamp the output. Lenders need clear evidence of what the agent saw, what it inferred, what it recommended, what the human changed, and why the final decision was fair.

### Banking Leaders Say AI Agents Are the Next Fraud Vulnerability
**Source:** [BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/press-release/world-banking-leaders-see-ai-agents-as-industrys-greatest-vulnerability-in-next-year) - **Date:** June 10, 2026

BioCatch published survey results from 1,440 fraud-management, AML, risk, and compliance leaders at banks across 25 countries. The survey found that 84% of respondents see AI agents as the industry's greatest exploitable vulnerability in the next year, 88% say AI has already increased fraud sophistication, 80% say their institution has already encountered attacks using agentic AI, and 72% believe it will be very difficult to distinguish legitimate AI-assisted actions from malicious or manipulated activity when agents commonly initiate transactions. The survey also found reported year-over-year fraud losses rising from 59% of respondents in 2025 to 76% in 2026.

Vendor surveys need skepticism, but the direction is credible. Agentic commerce and agentic banking create a detection problem that old login, device, and transaction rules were not built for. The central question is intent: did the customer mean this, was the agent authorized, was the agent manipulated, and did the transaction match the delegated scope? Fraud teams should stop framing agents as a future UX feature and start treating them as a new actor type inside fraud strategy, customer authentication, reimbursement policy, and interbank intelligence sharing.

### U.S. Bank Examiners Start Asking Harder AI Questions
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/regulation/2026/bank-regulators-probe-industry-use-ai/) / [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/us-bank-regulators-increase-oversight-of-ai-use-reuters) - **Date:** June 12, 2026

PYMNTS, citing Reuters, reported that U.S. banking supervisors have begun asking banks detailed questions about AI during routine examinations. The reported focus areas include client-data protections, governance frameworks, third-party risk, vendor oversight, subcontractor exposure, contingency plans, whether AI systems can access or infer data outside approved limits, and whether banks can shut systems down when needed. Bloomberg Law separately summarized the Reuters report as covering AI use in higher-risk areas such as lending, know-your-customer checks, and sanctions screening.

This is the practical version of AI regulation. It is not a new rulebook. It is examiners asking whether the bank can explain, constrain, monitor, and stop the systems it already uses. That matters more than another speech about responsible AI. Banks should expect AI inventories, vendor chains, data access maps, kill switches, exception handling, model validation, human oversight, and incident playbooks to move from committee slides into examiner conversations. If a bank cannot answer those questions cleanly, its AI program is not mature.
