### Worldline, ING, and Visa Execute a Live Agentic Payment in Europe
**Source:** [Worldline](https://worldline.com/en/home/top-navigation/media-relations/press-release/pr-2026_07_02_01) - **Date:** July 2, 2026

Worldline and ING announced a live end-to-end agent-driven payment transaction in Europe with Visa. The transaction used an AI-enabled purchase journey in which the consumer set purchase conditions, a merchant AI agent acted within those limits, the user authenticated purchase intent with Visa Payment Passkeys, and ING authorized the payment. Worldline says the transaction ran across its infrastructure and was designed to work inside existing regulatory expectations, including Strong Customer Authentication.

This is a serious signal because it keeps the hard controls in the payment chain. The customer sets limits, biometric authentication confirms intent, the issuer authorizes, and the infrastructure records the transaction. That is the shape agentic payments need. Banks should not treat this as a green light for full autonomy. They should treat it as a control pattern: identity, consent, authentication, authorization, and traceability must stay explicit.

### DNB Moves Corporate Lending Onto nCino With AI Banking Advisor
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/02/dnb-bets-on-ncino-to-overhaul-corporate-lending/) - **Date:** July 2, 2026

DNB has gone live on the nCino Platform for corporate lending, with an SME lending expansion planned for next year. The Norwegian bank serves more than 2 million retail customers and over 200,000 corporate clients, and plans to extend the platform across branches in nine countries. The rollout includes nCino Commercial Lending and Banking Advisor, an AI-driven conversational tool intended to place intelligence inside bankers' daily workflows.

The useful takeaway is that lending AI is moving into operating platforms, not sitting beside them as a demo layer. For credit leaders, that raises the quality bar. If AI is inside workflow, model output must be tied to source data, policy, approval evidence, and audit history. Faster corporate lending is valuable only if the bank can still explain why a credit decision moved, who approved it, and what exception path was used.

### Sherlocq Puts Regulatory Intelligence Inside Claude and ChatGPT
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/02/why-compliance-teams-cant-ignore-sherlocqs-ai-leap/) - **Date:** July 2, 2026

Sherlocq, an AI-powered regulatory intelligence platform for financial services, compliance, legal, and regulatory professionals, is now available as a native connector inside both Claude and ChatGPT. The company says compliance teams can perform multi-jurisdiction regulatory research inside the AI tools they already use, with Copilot and Gemini integrations in development. Use cases include licensing comparisons across markets and sanctions-screening research across more than 320 data sources.

This is a better direction than forcing compliance staff into yet another portal. The risk is that convenience can hide provenance. Compliance teams should demand cited outputs, source freshness, jurisdiction scope, and record retention from any AI regulatory assistant. A fast answer that cannot be defended to audit, legal, or a regulator is not productivity. It is rework with better formatting.

### Corgi Launches AI-Native Insurance Claims TPA
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/30/corgi-launches-ai-native-platform-accelerating-insurance-claims/) - **Date:** June 30, 2026

Corgi Insurance launched Corgi Claims, an AI-native third-party administrator for insurance claims. The platform combines AI-powered claims triage with more than 5,000 licensed adjusters. It is designed to review claims at first notice of loss, assess severity, flag potential coverage issues, identify missing documentation, and route the work before an adjuster starts manual review. Supported lines include commercial liability, property, catastrophe, renters, trucking, workers' compensation, and specialty programs.

Claims is a practical place for AI because the file work is heavy and the judgment still matters. The model should do the first sort, evidence checklist, and missing-information prompt. It should not quietly decide coverage or liability. Insurers need clear separation between triage automation and claim decision authority, or they will end up with faster bad decisions and weaker dispute files.

### Dawnguard Raises $3.3 Million for AI-Native RegTech Cybersecurity
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/02/dawnguard-raises-3-3m-for-ai-cybersecurity/) - **Date:** July 2, 2026

Dawnguard raised $3.3 million in pre-seed funding for an AI-native RegTech and cybersecurity platform built around secure-by-design cloud architecture. The company says its platform helps teams design secure cloud architectures before deployment, generate infrastructure as code, and continuously validate live environments against approved architecture and compliance standards. The round was led by BNVT Capital, with participation from Curiosity VC and eCAPITAL.

This matters for banks because AI-assisted software delivery is making weak architecture show up faster. Detection, response, and patching still matter, but they are not enough when systems are being generated and changed at machine speed. Financial institutions should push security controls earlier into design, infrastructure code, and deployment validation. Waiting for production monitoring to catch architectural drift is a losing strategy.
