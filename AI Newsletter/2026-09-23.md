### Six Major Banks Publish Principles for Trusted Agentic Commerce

**Source:** [Bank of America newsroom](https://newsroom.bankofamerica.com/content/newsroom/press-releases/2026/09/global-banks-collaborate-on-principles-for-trusted-agentic-comme.html)  |  **Date:** September 22, 2026

ASB Bank, Bank of America, Capital One, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, ING, and NatWest published shared principles for AI agents that choose and pay for products. The framework covers transparency, safety, privacy and data, customer choice, and interoperability. The banks explicitly connect trusted agentic commerce to identity, authorization, fraud prevention, liability management, and customer protection.

This is an industry position paper, not a binding standard, but it gives payment leaders a practical checklist for the next design review. Banks should map where an agent's identity, authority, payment credentials, disclosures, and dispute liability are recorded today, then identify gaps before agents move from assisted shopping into autonomous payment execution.

### Form3 Adds a Read-Only MCP Layer for Payment Operations

**Source:** [Form3](https://www.form3.tech/resources/press-releases/this-launch-marks-the-evolution-of-form3-from-clou)  |  **Date:** September 23, 2026

Form3 launched an AI-enabled payments platform for banks and fintechs. Its initial Model Context Protocol adaptor lets authorized AI applications retrieve and interpret payment information through a natural-language interface for investigations, exception handling, and operational support. The agent is read-only and cannot initiate changes or payments, while existing data and security controls remain in place.

The boundary is the important part. Form3 is offering a controlled way to expose payment data to agents without immediately granting execution rights. Payment operations teams can use this pattern to test usefulness, logging, access control, and escalation with a lower blast radius before deciding whether any agent should be allowed to change payment state.

### Deutsche Bank Deploys Agentic AI for Source of Wealth KYC

**Source:** [Deutsche Bank Private Bank](https://wealth.db.com/en/about-us/news/2026/deutsche-bank-private-bank-launches-agentic-ai-kyc-processes.html)  |  **Date:** September 22, 2026

Deutsche Bank Private Bank put an agentic AI solution for Source of Wealth work live in its Singapore and Hong Kong booking centres, with a broader rollout planned. The solution researches and documents client wealth information from case materials and approved external sources, identifies gaps or inconsistencies, and prepares assessments for human review inside the bank's digital KYC platform.

This is a meaningful production use case because it automates a document-heavy control while keeping accountability with bank staff. Wealth and compliance leaders should focus on source provenance, missing-evidence handling, review quality, and the audit record for each generated narrative. The bank's stated control boundary is that tasks can be automated, but accountability remains with people.

### EU Supervisors Flag AI Cyber Risk and External Dependencies

**Source:** [European Banking Authority, EIOPA, and ESMA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/esas-call-vigilance-over-external-dependencies-cyber-threats-and-private-credit-risks)  |  **Date:** September 23, 2026

The European Supervisory Authorities identified external dependencies, emerging technologies, and private credit as key vulnerabilities in their Autumn 2026 risk update. They highlighted dependence on non-European ICT providers and growing cyber risks linked to increasingly capable AI models. For insurers, the update also points to exposure through cyber-insurance underwriting and the possibility of AI-enabled attacks increasing claims and accumulation risk.

The message is broader than model risk. A bank or insurer can have a well-controlled model and still be exposed through a concentrated cloud, data, or software dependency. Boards should connect AI inventories to third-party concentration, exit planning, incident response, and cyber-insurance assumptions instead of managing those issues as separate programs.

### UAE Bank Expands AI Trade-Based Money Laundering Controls

**Source:** [Pelican AI investor release](https://ir.pelican.ai/pelican-ai-advances-deployment-of-ai-powered-trade-based-money-laundering-solution-with-leading-uae-financial-institution/)  |  **Date:** September 23, 2026

Pelican AI said a leading UAE financial institution, described in the release as the country's largest bank, selected its Advanced Trade-Based Money Laundering Module. The expansion is in the final phase of production deployment and extends an existing trade-compliance relationship. The module uses AI, natural language processing, machine learning, trade documents, commodity pricing, vessel movements, voyage histories, and maritime intelligence to detect red flags such as over-invoicing, under-invoicing, and phantom shipping.

Trade-based money laundering is a hard problem for rules-only systems because evidence is distributed across documents, transactions, and external data. The deployment is a useful signal for financial-crime leaders, but the performance claims are vendor-reported and the bank is unnamed. Buyers should demand measurable false-positive, investigation-time, and typology-coverage results in their own trade corridors before treating this architecture as proven.
