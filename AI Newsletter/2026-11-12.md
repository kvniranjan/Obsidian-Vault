### Feedzai Expands Digital Trust to Detect AI Agents at the Front Door

**Source:** [Feedzai via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/your-next-bank-customer-might-not-be-human-feedzai-helps-banks-spot-ai-agents-at-the-front-door-302937139.html)  •  **Date:** November 12, 2026

Feedzai announced an expansion of its Digital Trust solution specifically targeting AI agents accessing customer accounts. The update adds persistent device fingerprinting, a fraud score built on Feedzai's network view, and cryptographic device binding that links customer, device, and bank in a single verifiable chain. The company also says the update targets MFA bypass including passkey attacks, and it claims 44% more fraud detected in the riskiest 0.1% of sessions with no increase in false positives, 6,000 fewer unnecessary authentication challenges per million logins versus competing technology, and 41% fewer SMS one-time passcodes. These are vendor-reported metrics.

As authorized AI agents multiply across banking channels, the detection problem splits into two: agents acting for account holders that the bank should allow, and agents acting without real customer authorization that the bank should block. Feedzai's approach relies on behavioral and device signals, which is harder to spoof than credential-based identity alone. Banks should define the allow-list policy before deploying agent detection — blocking all agents is not the goal — and test false-positive rates across customer segments, escalation paths when a legitimate agent is blocked, and the evidence chain needed for disputes.

### Bain & Company: AI Will Reshape Where Banks Make Money, Not Just How Much

**Source:** [Bain & Company via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-could-reshape-where-banks-make-money-market-leadership-positions-will-decide-who-keeps-itbain--company-302937783.html)  •  **Date:** November 12, 2026

Bain released its "Future of Banking" brief arguing that AI's primary impact on banks over the next five years will be competitive redistribution rather than industry-wide efficiency gains. The firm notes that among the top 30 banks by 2025 year-end assets, those ranked first or a close second in their business lines already earn a 15% ROE versus 9% for weaker-positioned peers. AI, the brief argues, may lower customer switching barriers and erode the inertia that lets weaker players survive. Bain outlines three postures: build and attack where AI enables new propositions, defend profit pools that remain structurally attractive, and exit participation where the economics depend on friction AI is likely to remove. These are Bain's forecasts and analysis.

The brief's market-position framing is more strategically useful than cost-reduction promises. The practical question for banks is which of their current revenue lines depend on customer inertia versus genuine product advantage, and whether their AI investments are targeted at defensible positions or at efficiency in lines that may face margin compression regardless. Banks in weaker market positions should be explicit about which business lines they intend to defend and which to exit before AI-driven competitors make that decision for them.

### Wells Fargo Requires Customers to Prove AI Agents Were Revoked

**Source:** [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/wells-fargo-wants-customers-to-prove-theyve-revoked-ai-agents)  •  **Date:** Published November 2, 2026; effective November 24, 2026

Wells Fargo added Section 15 to its Online Access Agreement, effective November 24, titled "Third-Party Artificial Intelligence Agents or Agentic AI." Under it, customers who authorize an AI agent are liable for the agent's actions unless they can produce evidence they revoked access. American Banker reported Wells Fargo appears to be the first bank to require proof of revocation. The agreement also gives the bank discretion to require agent registration and to restrict or end any agent's access at any time. These are reported terms; Wells Fargo has not explained the change publicly, according to the article.

The liability shift creates a meaningful gap between what customers can practically demonstrate and what the terms require. If an agent continues acting after a customer's revocation instruction — a failure mode that is not hypothetical — the customer may bear the loss. Banks following Wells Fargo's approach should publish clear revocation mechanisms, confirm the revocation is propagated to every access path the agent uses, and define a dispute process for cases where an agent acted after a customer claims to have revoked it. The indemnity clause needs a customer-facing answer, not just a legal one.

### OCC Issues First AI Supervisory Framework for Banks

**Source:** [Decrypt (covering OCC Bulletin 2026-54)](https://decrypt.co/382560/us-bank-regulator-issues-first-ai-framework-banks-battle-rogue-agents)  •  **Date:** November 5, 2026

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued Bulletin 2026-54, a 22-page examiner guide that is the first OCC supervisory framework specifically addressing AI in banking. It is non-binding and creates no new regulatory requirements, but it tells examiners to assess AI risk by consequence and reversibility rather than model sophistication. The bulletin introduces four illustrative risk tiers, placing fully autonomous payment agents and real-time credit decisions in the highest category. It also covers external AI agents run by customers or fintechs — not controlled by the bank — as a risk examiners should consider. Suggested safeguards include identity verification, delegated permissions, transaction limits, monitoring, the ability to suspend or revoke access, circuit breakers, and emergency shutdown capabilities.

Non-binding guidance still shapes exam conversations, and the OCC's focus on consequences and reversibility gives compliance teams a practical lens: map existing AI deployments to the four tiers, then confirm that safeguards are proportional to the tier, that human approval points are defined for high-tier actions, and that a tested shutdown path exists. The external-agent tier matters specifically for open-banking and third-party agent integrations — a bank cannot disclaim AI risk simply because the agent belongs to its customer.

### Marsh Asks Fed to Count Credit Insurance Against AI-Loan Capital Requirements

**Source:** [Insurance Journal (Bloomberg)](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/11/12/892334.htm)  •  **Date:** November 12, 2026

Marsh & McLennan is advocating for US regulators to allow banks to hold less capital against loans covered by credit risk insurance, as AI-related lending concentrations grow. Azi Larsen, a managing director in Marsh's credit specialties practice, said the firm hopes for a positive regulatory answer by year-end. Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman confirmed on November 10 that the Fed intends to finalize capital rules in the coming weeks, with the package expected to lower the surcharge for the largest US lenders and revise risk-based capital rules for midsize banks. Barclays has sought credit insurance coverage on over USD 10 billion of data center debt, and HSBC has explored a similar arrangement. McKinsey projects global data center spending at USD 6.7 trillion by 2030. These are reported figures from Insurance Journal's Bloomberg-sourced reporting.

The underlying problem is real: banks approaching concentration limits on AI-sector and data center loans are looking for balance-sheet tools, and credit insurance is a logical candidate if regulators recognize it as effective risk mitigation. The capital treatment question matters because without it, US banks may be at a structural disadvantage relative to European counterparts in financing the AI infrastructure build. The unresolved issue is whether the insurance market has the depth and willingness to absorb losses at the scale of a data center cycle downturn, and whether the coverage actually transfers the risk the bank is holding.

### Kakao Pay Securities Deploys Live AI Fraud Detection Across All Transaction Types

**Source:** [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/market/2026/11/12/kakao-pay-securities-rolls-out-ai-fraud-detection-system)  •  **Date:** November 12, 2026

Kakao Pay Securities announced that its AI-based fraud detection system has been running on live transactions since November 2026. The system adds machine-learning risk scoring to the firm's existing fraud detection framework and covers deposits, withdrawals, account openings, stock sales, currency exchange, share transfers to and from other brokerages, and user information changes. It is designed to detect account takeovers and unauthorized sales or transfers of holdings. When the system finds compound risk signals, it generates a risk score and reasoning for staff review. The firm reorganized so fraud detection and AML run under one function in March 2026. No accuracy or detection-rate figures were disclosed.

Integrating fraud detection and AML is operationally sensible because fraudulent fund movements and money-laundering patterns can share the same infrastructure, and separating the teams often separates the signals. The staff-review model, where the AI flags and reasons but people decide, is appropriate for high-consequence transaction blocking in a brokerage. Institutions building similar systems should publish their false-rejection rates by account type and transaction size, test whether the reasoning is accurate enough to support a customer-facing explanation, and confirm that the AML referral path from fraud detection works under volume.
