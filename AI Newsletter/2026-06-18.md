### Lloyds Tests AI Scam Check Before Customer Money Leaves the Account
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/17/lloyds-new-tool-flags-scams-before-money-leaves/) - **Date:** June 17, 2026

Lloyds Bank is preparing Scam Check, an in-app feature that sits inside certain payment journeys and tries to stop shopping scams before customers transfer funds. When a customer attempts to pay a new payee for an online purchase, the app asks questions and requests screenshots of the item being purchased. AI then scans for warning signs such as implausibly low prices, new seller accounts with no ratings, vague descriptions, deposit requests, and pressure tactics. Lloyds says 68% of its fraud reports relate to shopping scams, most of them originating on Meta-owned platforms, and that it blocked more than GBP 1 billion in attempted fraud in 2025.

This is the right direction for authorized push payment fraud. Post-payment recovery is structurally worse than pre-payment intervention, especially when instant rails compress the response window and reimbursement expectations shift more liability toward banks. The hard problem is calibration. If the model blocks or warns too much, customers learn to ignore it. If it is too loose, the bank still eats the loss and the customer loses trust. Lloyds needs to prove that Scam Check reduces actual scam losses without turning ordinary marketplace purchases into a high-friction experience.

### Visa and Mastercard Push Tokenized Credentials Into Agentic Commerce
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/visa-and-mastercard-put-tokens-in-charge-of-ai-commerce/) - **Date:** June 17, 2026

Visa and Mastercard are building trust infrastructure for AI-driven commerce rather than trying to own every assistant. Visa has announced Agent Score, Agentic Directory, and new token capabilities meant to enrich transaction data and help merchants and issuers evaluate whether a purchase came from an approved participant in an agentic commerce ecosystem. Mastercard is taking a similar route with Mastercard Agent Pay, Wizard, and Stripe, using tokenized payment credentials to connect AI-driven product discovery with transaction execution while preserving visibility into who is acting and under what authority.

The durable layer in agentic payments is not the shopping assistant. It is the authorization layer underneath: identity, credentials, spend limits, revocation, merchant trust, issuer visibility, and dispute handling. Card issuers should not treat this as a distant commerce experiment. If customers authorize agents to transact, banks need policy for delegated intent, customer consent evidence, transaction monitoring, and liability when an approved agent does something the customer later disputes.

### Flagright Raises Series A for Explainable AI Financial Crime Compliance
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/fraud-prevention/2026/flagright-secures-12-5-million-to-expand-ai-powered-financial-crime-compliance/) - **Date:** June 17, 2026

Flagright raised US$12.5 million in Series A funding led by Infinity Ventures to expand its AI operating system for financial crime compliance. The platform combines transaction monitoring, watchlist screening, risk scoring, case management, AI forensics, and governance workflows. Flagright says it will use the funding to expand explainable AI across investigations, alert intelligence, rule optimization, decision support, and audit-ready workflows, while growing in the U.S. market among banks, credit unions, fintechs, and regulated financial institutions. The company says more than 100 fintechs and banks across 30 countries use its platform.

The vendor claim that the platform can deliver up to 93% fewer false positives and 80% lower compliance costs deserves scrutiny, not blind acceptance. Those numbers depend on the baseline, the customer population, the rule set, and data quality. The more useful signal is the product direction: financial crime teams want AI that can gather evidence, follow standard operating procedures, and make investigations reviewable. If AI agents resolve low-risk alerts, the bank still needs clear escalation rules, human accountability, and an audit record that explains why an alert was closed.

Broadridge joined Anthropic's Project Glasswing, an industry initiative using frontier AI models to secure critical software and strengthen cyber defense. Broadridge says it will apply frontier AI models to its own systems to stay ahead of emerging threats and support a safer financial ecosystem. Project Glasswing brings together organizations that build or maintain software for critical infrastructure, including financial services, and participants will use Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview to strengthen defensive security across foundational systems that make up a major part of the shared cyberattack surface.

This belongs in a financial-services AI digest because Broadridge is market plumbing. Its technology supports investing, governance, communications, and trading workflows across tokenized and traditional securities. AI-assisted defensive security is useful, but it also raises a sober question: if frontier models improve vulnerability discovery for defenders, attackers will eventually get similar leverage. Market infrastructure firms need to assume vulnerability identification, patch prioritization, third-party assurance, and incident escalation will operate at higher speed than old cyber playbooks expected.
