### UK Fraud Losses Hit GBP 1.28 Billion as APP Scams Jump
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/financial-crime-fraud-prevention/uk-payment-fraud-losses-topped-1-28bn-in-2025-new-uk-finance-report-finds) / [UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/) - **Date:** June 15, 2026

UK Finance's Annual Fraud Report 2026 found that payment fraud losses across the UK banking sector reached GBP 1.28 billion in 2025, up 4% year over year. Authorized push payment fraud losses rose 19% to GBP 576.4 million, while unauthorized payment fraud accounted for GBP 703.4 million. The report says there were more than four million fraud cases, that APP scams accounted for 32% of all losses, and that two-thirds of APP fraud originated online. Banks reimbursed GBP 354.3 million to APP victims and prevented GBP 1.68 billion in unauthorized fraud.

This is where AI fraud policy becomes real. Faster scams, better deepfakes, synthetic identities, mule networks, and social-platform targeting are outpacing the old bank-only perimeter. UK Finance is also pushing for stronger fraud prevention obligations on high-risk online platforms. Banking leaders should read the number as a liability allocation problem: if the scam starts on a platform, the money moves through a bank, and reimbursement lands on financial institutions, the sector needs better upstream signal sharing and enforceable platform duties.

### PNC's AI Factory Makes Compute Strategy a Banking Control Issue
**Source:** [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/why-pnc-is-building-its-own-ai-factory) - **Date:** June 11, 2026

American Banker reported that PNC is building its own "AI factory," including proprietary models and data-center capacity, to reduce dependence on third-party AI providers. PNC CEO Bill Demchak framed the strategy as a cost and control move, arguing that AI productivity gains can be erased if inference and token costs are entirely dictated by external technology vendors. The bank is taking a hybrid approach that combines internal compute and models with outside partnerships for selected use cases.

This is the next serious AI infrastructure question for large banks. Owning compute does not automatically produce better models, but it can improve cost predictability, data residency, latency, resilience, and control over regulated workloads. The strategic decision is not "build everything" versus "buy everything." It is which workloads are sensitive, high-volume, or strategically important enough to justify internal capacity. Banks that ignore this will discover later that their AI operating model is also a vendor concentration problem.
