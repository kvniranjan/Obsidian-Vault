### Visa Says AI Has Moved Payment Fraud Toward Human Manipulation
**Source:** [Visa](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Threats-Report-As-Network-Security-Strengthens-Criminals-Accelerate-Shift-to-AI-Enabled-Social-Engineering/default.aspx) / [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/visa/2026/visa-says-criminals-target-people-core-payment-security-tightens/) / [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/ai-fueled-scams-drive-fraud-visa/820800/) - **Date:** May 20-22, 2026

Visa's Spring 2026 Biannual Threats Report says payment security is improving at the network and token level, but fraud is migrating toward AI-enabled social engineering. Visa identified nearly USD 1 billion in scam-related activity from July through December 2025, making scams the largest category of consumer payment fraud in its data. The company also reported that device-token fraud declined 9.6% year over year in the same period, while ransomware activity increased 26% and only 23% of victims paid. Visa's message is blunt: criminals are increasingly targeting trust, urgency, impersonation, and customer decision-making rather than breaking core payment infrastructure directly.

This is the practical fraud lesson banks need to absorb. Better authentication and tokenization matter, but they do not solve a scam where the customer is convinced to approve the payment. AI lowers the cost of personalization, script generation, voice cloning, and rapid campaign testing. That means fraud operations need to shift from "was the credential valid" to "does this transaction fit the customer's intent, pressure level, device context, payee relationship, and recent behavioral pattern." Banks that keep treating scams as an education problem will lose. Education helps, but it is not a control framework.

### National Bank of Canada Deepens Sardine Bet for Agentic Financial Crime Controls

Sardine raised a USD 25 million Series C extension led by National Bank of Canada, bringing total funding to USD 170 million. The bank is also expanding its commercial relationship with Sardine after a live platform evaluation showed improved fraud detection and reduced false positives. National Bank of Canada plans to roll out Sardine's device intelligence and real-time risk scoring across retail, commercial, and wealth operations. FinTech Global described Sardine as an agentic risk platform focused on financial crime prevention, and NAventures said the company is setting the standard for financial crime agents.

This is more meaningful than a routine fintech funding item because a large incumbent bank is tying investment to measured operational performance. The useful question for other banks is not whether Sardine's specific model is better than a homegrown stack. The question is whether financial crime teams can use real-time device, identity, behavior, and transaction context across business lines without burying investigators in false positives. AI fraud tools that only add alerts are not progress. The winning systems will triage, explain, suppress noise, and preserve the evidence trail well enough for compliance, customer remediation, and law enforcement.

### Japan Tells Financial Institutions to Prepare for Frontier AI Cyber Risk Now
**Source:** [Japan Financial Services Agency](https://www.fsa.go.jp/news/r7/sonota/20260522-5/20260522.html) / [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/articles/2480955/japan-s-financial-sector-urged-to-prepare-for-ai-powered-cyberattacks) / [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/japan-government-to-get-access-to-anthropics-latest-ai) - **Date:** May 22, 2026

Japan's Financial Services Agency and the Bank of Japan jointly asked financial institutions to take short-term action in response to threat changes caused by frontier AI. The request follows earlier Japanese work on AI-related cybersecurity threats in the financial sector and renewed attention to Anthropic's Mythos model, which has been discussed publicly as a powerful vulnerability-discovery capability. The Straits Times reported that Japan's FSA urged institutions to implement short-term countermeasures, including the option to suspend system operations, in response to heightened AI-powered cyberattack risk.

This is the clearest version of a regulatory message that has been building for weeks: frontier AI risk is not just model governance. It is operational resilience. Banks should assume vulnerability discovery, exploit chaining, phishing, malware adaptation, and attack planning will speed up. The board question should be ugly and specific: if a frontier model or adversarial tool surfaces a critical weakness tonight, can the institution find exposure, patch, isolate, communicate, and keep critical payment, trading, deposit, and customer channels running? If the answer is a slide deck instead of a tested playbook, the institution is not ready.

### Faster Payments Council Puts Fraud Disputes at the Center of Real-Time Rails
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/us-faster-payments-council-issues-instant-payments-fraud-guidance) / [U.S. Faster Payments Council](https://fasterpaymentscouncil.org/) - **Date:** May 20, 2026

The U.S. Faster Payments Council published guiding principles for fraud dispute resolution in instant payment environments. The report covers unauthorized fraud, authorized push payment scams, first-party fraud, and request-for-payment fraud. It does not impose liability rules, but it lays out 11 principles for structured dispute workflows, shared responsibility across ecosystem participants, vulnerable-consumer and small-business support, and ISO 20022-aligned transparency. The Paypers noted that the work draws lessons from the UK's APP scam regime, Brazil's Pix ecosystem, Nacha's credit-push enhancements, and card network dispute practices.

The AI connection is direct even if the report is not branded as an AI launch. Real-time payments plus AI-scaled scams create a brutal control problem: money moves instantly, persuasion improves constantly, and old chargeback logic does not apply. Banks cannot wait for disputes to become a public-policy mess before designing evidence capture. They need sender and receiver responsibilities, data-sharing rules, scam typology fields, customer vulnerability indicators, and explainable risk interventions built into the payment journey. Speed without dispute infrastructure is not innovation. It is a liability engine.

### ComplyAdvantage Moves Payment Screening Into Its AI-Native Mesh Platform
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/complyadvantage-launches-payment-screening-on-mesh-platform) - **Date:** May 22, 2026

ComplyAdvantage launched Payment Screening on Mesh, moving its payment screening capability into the company's AI-native risk platform. The product screens names in under 250 milliseconds at scale across bank, card, remittance, and crypto rails, while giving compliance teams no-code controls for match sensitivity and internal watchlists. The system is designed to update sanctions lists in minutes, intercept suspicious payments before processing, mute repeated false positives, and connect payment screening with transaction monitoring and customer risk scoring in one auditable environment.

This is the compliance version of the same real-time pressure seen in payments fraud. Screening that takes too long breaks the customer experience. Screening that is too loose creates sanctions and AML exposure. Screening that is too noisy burns analyst capacity. AI-native compliance platforms are trying to solve all three at once, but banks should be disciplined in vendor evaluation. "Under 250 milliseconds" is useful only if the false-negative risk, list provenance, alert explanation, model monitoring, and audit record hold up under regulatory scrutiny. Faster black boxes are still black boxes.

### Insurance AI Risk Moves From Experimentation to Coverage and Supervisory Pressure
**Source:** [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/professional-liability/are-ai-exclusions-in-dando-becoming-inevitable-576116.aspx) / [NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/business/insurance-ai-driven-fraud-breaches-put-insurers-on-notice-irdai-seeks-review-11521874/amp/1) - **Date:** May 20-21, 2026

Insurance Business reported that Gallagher executives expect AI exclusions in D&O and financial institution policies to become more likely as AI use moves closer to mortgage underwriting, customer interactions, and decision-making tools. For now, broad exclusions are not yet the market norm, but underwriters are watching AI disclosures, AI-washing risk, and policy silence closely. Separately, NDTV Profit reported that India's IRDAI asked insurers to submit status reports on preparedness for AI-driven cyber risks, including vulnerability and response capability, by May 22. The reported concern is that existing cyber frameworks do not yet provide AI-specific controls for underwriting, claims, fraud, and customer servicing.

This is what happens when AI becomes operational enough to create losses but not mature enough to be comfortably priced. Banks and insurers should read the coverage signal carefully. If policy language is silent, that does not mean the risk is covered cleanly. It means litigation will define the boundary later. Financial institutions need inventory, use-case classification, customer-impact analysis, disclosure controls, and incident playbooks before underwriters and regulators force the issue. Pretending AI risk lives only in the technology department is a bad bet.
