### Bank of England Warns Agent Interactions Could Create Systemic Instability

**Source:** [Bank of England Financial Policy Committee Record, November 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-policy-committee-record/2026/november-2026) | **Date:** November 26, 2026

The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee said frontier AI capabilities, especially AI agents and open-weight models, are becoming more sophisticated. It warned that agents operating alone or in multi-agent settings with limited human involvement raise misalignment risk, while weaknesses in widely used technology and common material third parties could cause correlated disruption across firms and financial market infrastructures. The committee called for strong access controls, monitoring, rigorous testing, better visibility into material-third-party dependencies, and sector-wide resilience exercises. It also welcomed aligned reporting requirements taking effect March 18, 2027. This is a regulator's assessment and supervisory direction, not evidence of a specific new bank incident.

The board-level question is no longer only whether an agent can complete a task. It is whether the financial system can contain a class of agents acting at speed and scale. Banks should map agent permissions, common dependencies, and kill-switch and fallback paths across business units, then test whether logs identify which agent acted, for whom, with what authority, and which other agents or providers were affected.

### GoCardless Completes FCA Test of Agentic Account-to-Account Payments

**Source:** [GoCardless completes FCA AI Live Testing](https://gocardless.com/blog/gocardless-completes-fca-ai-live-testing) | **Date:** November 24, 2026

GoCardless said it completed the second cohort of the FCA's AI Live Testing programme and tested an agentic checkout for account-to-account payments. The company says it processed the UK's first agentic account-to-account payment in September for a recurring donation, then expanded to one-off payments in October. In a YouGov survey of 2,184 UK adults commissioned by GoCardless, 36% worried about fraud, hacking, or stolen payment details, 32% did not know who would be responsible if something went wrong, and only 13% said they were likely to let AI make a payment in the next 12 months. These are company and survey-partner claims, and FCA participation is not a safety certification or a general approval to launch.

The adoption barrier is liability and redress, not just checkout friction. Payment providers and banks need to define delegated authority, transaction limits, revocation, mistaken-payment handling, and evidence of consent before agentic payment volume matters. A user approval screen is not enough if nobody can prove what the agent was authorized to do or who must repair the loss.

### Visa Reports AI Scaling Fraud Through Payment Ecosystem Third Parties

**Source:** [Visa Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Report-AI-Enabled-Fraud-and-Third-Party-Attacks-Are-Scaling-Worldwide/) | **Date:** November 17, 2026

Visa's Payment Ecosystem Risk and Control team said AI is helping criminals execute existing fraud attacks faster, at greater scale, and with greater precision rather than creating wholly new categories of fraud. Visa reported that third-party incidents rose 62% from the prior six-month period, that third-party providers were linked to about 56% of compromised payment credentials it identified, and that its Scam Disruption work identified more than $1.4 billion in potential fraud. Visa also said its network-level controls blocked more enumeration activity and reduced related fraud losses. These are Visa-reported observations and results from activity observed between January and June 2026, not an independent industry benchmark.

The practical risk is upstream concentration. Payment firms should treat processors, PSPs, identity vendors, fraud platforms, and other shared service providers as part of the fraud perimeter, with telemetry and incident playbooks that cross organizational boundaries. AI fraud controls that only inspect the final authorization event will miss reconnaissance, credential testing, and coordinated scam infrastructure earlier in the chain.

The company says its agents now handle critical decisions across financial-crime compliance, onboarding, payment operations, and lending, while people set policy and oversee results. Arva also says it has deployed at scale with a top-10 US bank, an S&P 500 payments institution, and Wise, and that its first model outperformed leading general-purpose models by 13% in internal testing. These funding, customer, product, and benchmark claims come from the company and are not independently verified in the announcement.

The important change is the claim of end-to-end handling in high-consequence workflows. Banks considering similar systems should require reproducible evaluation sets, evidence-linked reasoning, clear escalation thresholds, model-change controls, and a record of every source and policy version used in a decision. Automation is not a control if the institution cannot reconstruct why an agent closed, escalated, or approved a case.

### Zytra Releases a BFSI-Specific Guardrail Benchmark

**Source:** [Zytra AI announcement via Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111788/zytra-ai-launches-ai-governance-platform) | **Date:** November 26, 2026

Zytra AI released FinProof under a Creative Commons license and expanded its AI governance platform. The company describes the benchmark as covering 17 banking, financial-services, and insurance attack categories mapped to regulatory obligations, including prompt injection, compliance evasion, regulatory hallucination, tool poisoning, multi-agent trust failures, and mandatory human escalation. The benchmark site lists 11,474 evaluation prompts, 36 subtypes, and an 81.5% attack rate against the guardrails it tested. These are vendor-developed benchmark and platform claims, not independent assurance of any bank's production controls.

Domain-specific testing is useful because generic safety scores do not tell a bank whether an agent will leak account data, bypass KYC, give unlicensed investment advice, or refuse to escalate a vulnerable-customer case. Risk teams should test their own models and orchestration layers against realistic benign traffic as well as adversarial prompts, and measure false positives, tool-use behavior, evidence quality, and human handoff rather than accepting a single headline score.
