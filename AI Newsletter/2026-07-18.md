### FIS Tests Anthropic Frontier AI on Critical Financial Infrastructure Security
**Source:** [FIS](https://www.investor.fisglobal.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fis-and-anthropic-extend-partnership-trusted-ai-financial) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

FIS joined Anthropic's Project Glasswing and is testing Mythos 5 against its own systems to identify and address security vulnerabilities in software that supports core banking, payment clearing, and money movement. FIS framed the work as separate from its commercial AI partnership with Anthropic and as an additional layer inside its defensive security program.

This is more important than another AI assistant launch. Core banking and payments vendors are starting to use frontier models against the software base that financial institutions depend on. Bank technology and risk leaders should ask vendors how AI security testing is scoped, how findings are validated by humans, how sensitive code and logs are protected, and whether remediation evidence can be shared during third-party risk reviews.

### Jia Opens AI SME Underwriting Infrastructure to Banks Through Netbank
**Source:** [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/16/3328859/0/en/jia-signs-netbank-as-first-institutional-partner-opening-its-sme-lending-infrastructure-to-banks-and-lenders.html) - **Date:** July 16, 2026

Jia signed Netbank as its first institutional partner, opening its SME lending infrastructure to banks, cooperatives, and lending companies. The company says its Ossicone AI underwriting engine reads documents such as purchase orders, supplier invoices, and delivery receipts, returns a credit decision in under 30 minutes, and has been proven on USD 20 million of Philippine SME loans with a sub-3% nonperforming loan rate.

The useful lesson is not that AI can read invoices. It is that SME credit models are moving toward operating-data underwriting where traditional bureau files are thin. Banks should be careful. Alternative evidence can expand access, but only if lenders can explain adverse decisions, detect document manipulation, monitor drift by segment, and prove the model does not quietly encode supplier, geography, or platform bias.

### Glia and Alloy Labs Turn 2027 Bank AI Planning Into a Governance Exercise
**Source:** [Glia](https://www.glia.com/news/2027-strategic-annual-planning-kit) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

Glia and Alloy Labs released a 2026-2027 Banking AI Strategic Annual Planning Kit for regional and community financial institutions. The kit combines research, roadmaps, templates, and checklists for C-suite planning, while Glia said AI is now the top technology budget item for banks and credit unions and that roughly 80% of institutions report early AI adoption has failed to improve the bottom line.

That 80% figure is the board-room problem. AI spending is easy to approve and hard to convert into measurable value. Smaller institutions should stop treating AI as a loose innovation bucket. The planning cycle needs named business outcomes, process owners, risk-tiered controls, vendor inventories, adoption metrics, quality sampling, and a kill list for pilots that do not earn their cost.

### Flex Raises USD 70 Million for AI-Native Private Banking on Stablecoin Rails
**Source:** [Portage](https://portageinvest.com/blog/halo-fund-leads-70m-investment-into-ai-native-private-banking-platform-flex-to-accelerate-the-launch-of-flex-global/) - **Date:** July 14, 2026

Flex raised a USD 70 million Series B1 led by Halo Fund, with participation from Portage Ventures, Wellington, Crosslink Capital, 53 Stations, Titanium Ventures, Spice, Florida Funders, and others. The company says annualized revenue has tripled since its prior round and that Flex Global will expand its AI-native private banking platform for business owners to more than 100 countries using stablecoin rails for faster cross-border money movement.

This is where private banking, treasury, and stablecoins start to blur. The attraction is obvious: business owners want global movement of money without slow bank handoffs. The control questions are also obvious: sanctions screening, source-of-funds evidence, wallet and beneficiary controls, transaction monitoring, customer disclosures, and what happens when an AI workflow recommends or initiates movement across jurisdictions.

### Supervest Warns Private Credit Investors to Reprice AI Exposure, Not Just Chase AI Yield
**Source:** [FinTecBuzz](https://fintecbuzz.com/supervest-explores-ais-impact-on-private-credit-investing/) - **Date:** July 18, 2026

Supervest published analysis arguing that private credit investors should examine how AI changes the resilience of underlying borrower cash flows, not only whether a portfolio has exposure to AI beneficiaries. The piece frames AI as a structural force that can change revenue durability, margin pressure, labor costs, and collateral assumptions across sectors.

This matters for lenders because AI risk is becoming credit risk. A borrower can look healthy under trailing financials while facing automation pressure, pricing compression, or customer churn from AI-enabled competitors. Credit teams should add AI exposure questions to underwriting memos, borrower monitoring, covenant discussions, and sector stress tests. Ignoring it is lazy credit work.
