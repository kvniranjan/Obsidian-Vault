### Microsoft at Sibos Day 2: "Context, Execution, Control" — The Trust Architecture Banks Need

**Source:** [Technology Record](https://www.technologyrecord.com/article/sibos-2026-microsoft-calls-for-trusted-ai-in-financial-services)  •  **Date:** September 30, 2026

At Sibos 2026's Day 2 in Miami, Microsoft's Jacqueline O'Flanagan argued that access to AI models alone will not provide a lasting competitive advantage for financial institutions. Instead, banks must combine AI with their own data, institutional knowledge, and governance through three explicit layers: **Context** (institutional data, market data, relationship histories, risk signals), **Execution** (AI agents coordinating work across workflows rather than answering isolated questions), and **Control** (identity, permissions, observability, auditability, and human accountability built in at design time). Microsoft cited measurable production outcomes: 20% faster client onboarding at BNY, processing times collapsed from days to hours at Genworth, with LSEG and Bradesco also referenced. O'Flanagan's central line: "An agent can gather evidence, reconcile information, or propose a response. A person keeps authority where professional judgment, fiduciary obligations, or consequential decisions require it."

The framework's practical value is its operationalizability: "context, execution, control" maps directly onto procurement and architecture decisions. Banks evaluating AI vendors should score each deployment against all three layers — a strong execution layer with weak control architecture creates precisely the accountability gap that regulators and auditors are beginning to probe. The production metrics Microsoft cited (BNY's onboarding improvement, Genworth's cycle-time reduction) are the rare publicly named outcomes against which peer institutions can benchmark their own deployments.

### FIDO Alliance at Sibos: "When the Agent Does the Buying, Who Owns the Customer?" — Agentic Commerce Standards Move to Centre Stage

**Source:** [FIDO Alliance / Fintech Futures](https://fidoalliance.org/fintech-futures-sibos-2026-digital-finance-in-an-ai-driven-economy-when-the-agent-does-the-buying-who-owns-the-customer/)  •  **Date:** September 30, 2026

Sibos 2026 Day 2 featured a session — co-published by FIDO Alliance and Fintech Futures — examining who owns the customer relationship when an autonomous AI agent is the one initiating a search, negotiating terms, and executing a payment. FIDO Alliance is developing interoperable standards for agentic interactions through a new Agentic Authentication Technical Working Group, drawing on initial contributions from Google (its Agent Protocol 2 specification) and Mastercard (Verifiable Intent). The three standard areas under development are: Verifiable User Instructions (phishing-resistant authorization of AI agents by humans); Agent Authentication (services verifying an agent is acting within its delegated parameters); and Trusted Delegation for Commerce (verifiable authorization for agent-initiated transactions within user-controlled boundaries).

The customer ownership question is not abstract — it is the strategic threat beneath the agentic payments trend. When a consumer delegates purchasing to an AI agent, the agent's preferred rails, verification methods, and counterparty relationships become the de facto channel. Banks and card networks that do not have an authenticated, standards-compliant agent channel risk becoming infrastructure that agents route around. FIDO's standards work effectively sets the table for which institutions will be able to credibly participate in the agentic commerce layer.

### "AI in Risk & Compliance 2026": 30% of RegTech Budgets Now AI, 58% of Deployments Take Autonomous Action

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/30/how-much-of-risk-and-compliance-spending-will-go-to-ai/)  •  **Date:** September 30, 2026

Parker & Lawrence Research and RegTech Analyst released their "AI in Risk & Compliance 2026" report, drawing on 300 senior compliance leaders, 100 technology vendors, and interviews with regulators. Key findings: financial institutions will direct approximately $703.7 million to AI in 2026, representing roughly 30% of total RegTech spending of $2.37 billion. All 300 surveyed institutions are either using or actively exploring AI; 66% of AI activity has reached production or advanced stages; and — most notably — 58.4% of AI use cases involve the system taking autonomous action rather than providing information to a human. ROI results are improving: 27.7% of respondents report returns exceeding 50%, while 13.3% saw returns of 1–10%. The report covers seven compliance domains: financial crime, conduct risk, cybersecurity, technology risk, data risk, compliance management, and ESG.

The 58.4% autonomous-action figure is the headline metric to track. It means that for the majority of AI deployments in production, the system is not just advising — it is deciding. That distribution of autonomous vs. advisory deployments should be explicitly mapped and disclosed in every institution's AI governance inventory, with recourse pathways defined for each autonomous action category. The 30% of RegTech spend figure also signals that AI is no longer a line item — it is the budget's plurality narrative.

### Elio Mortgage Launches with $5.1M Pre-Seed to Build AI-Native Origination "from Inside a Working Mortgage Operation"

**Source:** [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260929718646/en/Elio-Mortgage-Launches-with-$5.1-Million-in-Pre-Seed-Funding-to-Build-the-Mortgage-Company-for-the-AI-Era)  •  **Date:** September 29, 2026

Elio Mortgage emerged from stealth with $5.1 million in pre-seed funding led by Motive Partners and Social Leverage. The company was co-founded by Oren Michaely (engineering at Microsoft; Director of AI at Motive Partners) and Arad Lev Ari (real estate investing and investment banking at KKR and Deutsche Bank). Elio's distinctive model: rather than building AI tools on top of existing origination workflows, the company acquired a licensed mortgage brokerage and embedded engineers alongside loan officers, redesigning the process from within. The immediate product focus is end-to-end AI-native origination across the loan lifecycle. Proceeds will fund product development, nationwide licensing, and loan officer recruitment.

The "build from inside the workflow" approach is strategically meaningful: mortgage AI built by engineers who have never closed a loan tends to optimize for the parts of origination that are visible from the outside (document capture, data extraction) while missing the judgment-intensive steps (condition resolution, underwriter relationship management, exception handling) that dominate cycle time. Elio's co-location model attempts to close that gap before scaling. Lenders evaluating AI origination vendors should ask whether the vendor's team has ever underwritten a loan — not just whether their accuracy metrics look good in demos.

### BankSocial + Vyrdia: Embedded AI Fraud and Payments Tools for Credit Unions Within Existing Core Platforms

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/30/banksocial-and-vyrdia-bring-ai-fraud-tools-to-credit-unions/)  •  **Date:** September 30, 2026

BankSocial, a Web3 and AI-powered fintech, partnered with Vyrdia, a core processor for credit unions, to deliver embedded financial services tools directly inside existing digital banking platforms — without requiring institutions to replace their core systems. The joint offering includes Secura and Substrate, which combine real-time transaction monitoring with preventative fraud controls and AI agent orchestration designed for financial compliance; Prospera, which enables fractional stock, ETF, IRA, and digital asset purchases within credit union apps; and Remint, a payment infrastructure layer routing across FedNow, RTP, and stable-value channels. The partnership is designed to reduce vendor assessment friction for credit unions by bundling these capabilities within Vyrdia's existing ecosystem.

Credit unions face a structurally different AI adoption problem than large banks: limited internal engineering capacity, constrained vendor management bandwidth, and member bases that have low tolerance for UX disruption. The "no core replacement required" positioning directly addresses that constraint. The coupling of fraud tools with investment and payment infrastructure in a single embedded layer is also notable — it signals that AI-native fintechs are building for full financial-services capture within the credit union channel, not just point-solution replacement.
