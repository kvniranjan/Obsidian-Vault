### Q2 Positions Personal Agents as the Next Banking Channel

**Source:** [Q2](https://www.q2.com/blog/personal-agents-and-banking-how-to-adapt-to-an-emerging-channel) | **Date:** October 26, 2026

Q2 says personal AI agents are becoming a new banking channel and argues that financial institutions must retain control of identity, entitlements, brand, and fraud controls. Its current framing is read-only account connectivity through tools such as ChatGPT and Claude, with future action requiring authorized connections, interoperability standards, and institution-defined controls. These are Q2's product and market views, not independent adoption evidence.

The useful design boundary is ownership of the relationship. Banks should decide which data an agent can read, which actions it can request, how consent is recorded and revoked, how agent-originated activity appears in logs, and how fraud models distinguish a legitimate agent from an account takeover. A new channel needs a new control plane, not merely another API.

### HSBC Tests Insurance as a Risk Transfer for AI Infrastructure Loans

**Source:** [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/10/30/890898.htm) | **Date:** October 30, 2026

Insurance Journal reported that HSBC is in preliminary discussions with insurers about credit risk insurance for data-center project finance, including an SRT tied to more than $1 billion of data-center-linked loans. The report says talks may not produce a deal, and HSBC's finance chief said the bank's exposure to data-center lending is small. The article is media reporting based on people familiar with private discussions, not a confirmed transaction.

The signal for lenders and insurers is that AI infrastructure risk is becoming a balance-sheet and capital-management problem. Risk transfer can free capital, but it does not remove concentration, technology obsolescence, power, connectivity, business interruption, or correlated counterparty risk. Underwriters and credit committees need scenario tests that do not rely only on the data-center operator's utilization or residual-value assumptions.

### Pagaya Reports $3.6 Billion Quarterly Network Volume in AI-Driven Lending

**Source:** [Pagaya](https://investor.pagaya.com/static-files/5cf57357-09ab-4799-9f39-7381755bc5ab) | **Date:** October 29, 2026

Pagaya reported third-quarter 2026 network volume of $3.6 billion, up 29% year over year, along with $390 million in revenue and other income and $50 million in GAAP net income. The company said it onboarded Citizens Financial Group and First National Bank of Omaha for personal loans and point-of-sale financing, bringing the total number of partners onboarded in the prior 12 months to seven. These are company-reported financial and operating results.

The important question is whether AI lending scale is producing durable risk-adjusted returns, not just more originations. Bank partners should review approval and loss performance by vintage, protected-class outcomes, adverse-action reason quality, model-change governance, funding concentration, and who bears losses when performance moves outside the model's training range. Network volume is not evidence of underwriting quality by itself.
