Kyndryl said Suryoday Small Finance Bank will deploy agentic AI-enabled capabilities across the enterprise through an Agentic AI Center of Excellence. The bank plans to use the program for faster digitization and validation of account-opening forms, voice-enabled mobile banking support, legal enforcement request handling, suspicious-transaction investigation, and MSME loan underwriting.

This is the kind of bank AI deployment that deserves attention because it crosses customer service, compliance, operations, and credit. That creates real operating leverage, but it also expands model-risk and process-risk exposure. The lesson for smaller banks is blunt: agentic AI is no longer just a large-bank lab project, but weak governance will scale just as fast as productivity.

### American Express Frames Agentic Commerce as a Payments-Control Problem
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/paytech/what-the-fintech-s-7-episode-16-agentic-commerce-how-american-express-is-building-the-future-of-ai-driven-payments) - **Date:** August 12, 2026

FinTech Futures published a What the FinTech? episode with Margaret Ryan, SVP and Head of Product for Amex Digital Labs, focused on agentic commerce and agentic payments. The framing is not just that AI agents will shop or initiate transactions. The more important issue is how a trusted card network, issuer, merchant, and customer establish permissions, transaction context, authentication, and recourse when software acts on a customer's behalf.

Payments leaders should treat this as a control-layer race. Agentic commerce will not be won by whoever produces the flashiest assistant. It will be won by networks and issuers that can prove intent, scope, merchant legitimacy, spend limits, fraud signals, and dispute evidence at authorization speed. Banks that wait for agents to become mainstream before redesigning consent and liability models will be stuck cleaning up edge cases after customers already expect agent-driven checkout to work.

### Backbase Finds Banking AI ROI Tracking Is Still a Board-Level Gap
**Source:** [Backbase](https://www.backbase.com/blog/why-boards-cant-evaluate-ai-budgets-their-c-suite-isnt-tracking) - **Date:** August 12, 2026

Backbase summarized findings from its State of AI in African Banking 2026 report, based on 277 banking executives across 37 countries. The uncomfortable number is the split in ROI discipline: finance teams track AI ROI at 82%, while C-suite executives track it at 50% and risk and compliance teams track it at 48.1%. Backbase argues that this creates a board oversight problem because AI spending is being approved faster than outcomes are being measured across accountable owners.

This is not just an African banking issue. It is a governance pattern that appears wherever AI budgets get absorbed into transformation programs without a measurable business owner. Bank boards should stop asking whether the institution "uses AI" and start asking which AI workflows have a named manager, baseline metric, target outcome, control owner, and kill criteria. Without that, AI investment becomes a portfolio of demos that nobody can defend when costs, conduct issues, or failed controls show up.

### NTT DATA Packages Agentic AI for Governed Insurance Workflows

The company says the service targets underwriting, claims, customer service, policy administration, and other core insurance workflows using configurable AI agents, insurance-specific data models, orchestration, governance, auditability, regulatory guardrails, and human oversight.

The insurance signal is that vendors are moving from AI tooling into managed workflow layers. That can help carriers modernize faster, but it also changes vendor risk. If a service coordinates agents, employees, policy systems, claims data, and customer communications, insurers need more than standard SaaS due diligence. They need model-change controls, test evidence, traceable recommendations, deterministic decision rules where required, and proof that humans can intervene before a poor claim, underwriting, or service action becomes a customer harm.

### Appian and Synechron Target the Underwriting Orchestration Layer
**Source:** [Appian](https://appian.com/about/explore/press-releases/2026/appian-and-synechron-introduce-open-underwriting-stack-for-ai) - **Date:** August 11, 2026

Appian and Synechron introduced an Open Underwriting Stack for AI-powered connected underwriting. The architecture combines Synechron's InsureMESH data and integration layer, Appian process automation and AI agents, and Appian Connected Underwriting as a configurable application layer. The stated goal is to let insurers add a governed operational AI layer around existing core systems while keeping auditability, control, and underwriter authority.

This is a useful direction because underwriting AI often fails on integration, not model quality. Carriers have risk data, broker submissions, rating inputs, documents, exclusions, pricing rules, and legacy core systems that do not naturally line up. A parallel orchestration layer can create value if it triages, enriches, recommends, and routes work without hiding the evidence. The hard test is whether underwriters can see why the system acted, override it cleanly, and reconstruct the transaction months later.
