### Agentic AI and Banks: Who Signs Off on the Machine?
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/banking/2026/agentic-ai-and-banks-who-signs-off-on-the-machine/) - **Date:** May 29, 2026

As banks move from AI tools that support human decisions to agents that can act with more autonomy, the governance question is becoming concrete: which actions can an agent initiate, and which require explicit human approval? PYMNTS examines how institutions are drawing that boundary across loan processing, fraud analysis, and compliance servicing. The control model now being pushed by agentic AI vendors centers on permission boundaries, real-time monitoring, and named accountability when an automated decision goes wrong. The timing matters because banks are already dealing with more sophisticated fraud patterns while simultaneously trying to automate more work.

This is the transition that bank risk committees cannot hand-wave. Automation augments human judgment. Delegation replaces it for specific decisions. Banks that document which decisions agents can make, under what conditions, with what logging, and with what escalation path will be in a defensible position when regulators ask for proof. Banks that let pilots quietly expand into production are not innovating. They are creating an accountability gap.

### Visa's Pismo Says AI Has Exposed Banking's Old Tech Problem
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/payments-innovation/2026/visas-pismo-says-ai-has-exposed-bankings-old-tech-problem/) - **Date:** May 29, 2026

Visa's core banking infrastructure subsidiary Pismo argues that AI is making legacy system limitations impossible to hide. Banks running batch-era infrastructure cannot support real-time personalization, instant decisioning, or adaptive analytics at the level AI products require. Pismo points to examples including Thailand's T2P migrating roughly 320,000 customer accounts to a cloud-native platform in three months and Lunar Bank using Pismo infrastructure to support more than one million Nordic users. Pismo CEO Leonardo Collado framed modernization as a strategic choice, not only a technology upgrade.

The mechanism matters. AI does not fail only because models are weak. It fails because data is stale, system responses are batch-timed, and integration surfaces do not expose enough context for a model or agent to act correctly. Legacy infrastructure is no longer just a back-office cost problem. It is now a front-office AI product constraint. Incremental service-by-service migration is the serious path. Waiting for a perfect core replacement plan is how banks fall further behind.

### FinCEN Proposal Puts Manual KYB Compliance Under Pressure

FinTech Global reports that FinCEN's proposed rulemaking on customer identification and anti-money laundering program requirements is being read by compliance technology providers as a major signal that manual KYB processes are running out of road. Comments are due June 9, 2026. AiPrise argues that the proposal pushes financial institutions toward more systematic, auditable, and technology-enabled compliance operations, especially for business verification. The practical issue is simple: business verification remains resource-heavy, slow, and prone to fragmented evidence trails when handled through manual review queues.

This is where compliance leaders need to be blunt with themselves. Manual KYB may feel conservative, but at scale it often means inconsistent decisions, aging queues, weak documentation, and hard-to-reconstruct rationale. AI-native KYB will not get a free pass. It still needs explainability, sanctions and beneficial ownership controls, exception handling, and clear human review. But the regulatory direction is obvious: institutions need evidence-rich, repeatable compliance workflows. A spreadsheet plus analyst judgment will not satisfy that bar for much longer.

### Ballerine Launches Agenticom.org to Map Trust Gaps in Agentic Commerce
**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/ballerine-unveils-agenticomorg-to-map-and-solve-trust-gaps-in-agentic-commerce-302784315.html) - **Date:** May 28, 2026

Ballerine launched Agenticom.org, an industry hub focused on trust and readiness gaps in agentic commerce. The launch is based on research from 47 interviews across card schemes, payment service providers, acquirers, merchants, and AI platforms, plus Ballerine's merchant portfolio and public research. The headline finding is ugly: Ballerine says 73% of online merchants are not agent-ready, lacking structured data, verification signals, operational clarity, and trust infrastructure that agents are likely to rely on when recommending or initiating transactions. The company also says demand for agentic commerce is growing much faster than supply readiness.

Payment leaders should pay attention because this shifts the problem from buyer-side authorization to seller-side readiness. Even if a consumer authorizes an agent and a card network validates intent, the transaction can still fail if the merchant's policies, inventory, pricing, returns, or legitimacy signals are unclear or misleading. That becomes a dispute, fraud, chargeback, or customer harm problem for the financial institution sitting behind the payment. Agentic commerce needs merchant governance, not just smarter checkout.

### Catena Pushes Toward a Regulated Financial Institution for AI Agents
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/venture-capital-funding/catena-files-for-national-trust-bank-charter-secures-30m-in-series-a-funding) - **Date:** May 26, 2026

Catena, the agentic AI-focused fintech founded by former Circle executives Sean Neville and Matt Venables, has secured $30 million in Series A funding and filed for a national trust bank charter. The company says it is building a regulated financial institution designed for AI agents and their operators. Its model would allow agents to open accounts, hold money, and send payments through stablecoins and cards under human-defined and monitored policies. The round was co-led by Acrew Capital and a16z crypto, with participation from Breyer Capital, General Catalyst, QED, Oak HC/FT, Fin Capital, Coinbase Ventures, and IDG Capital.

This is more important than another agentic finance demo. Catena is trying to put regulated financial institution status around agent-controlled money movement. That is the right fight. If AI agents are going to hold value, initiate payments, and interact with merchants, the control plane has to include account ownership, authorization scope, monitoring, custody, dispute handling, and compliance reporting. A charter does not solve all of that. But it does force the business model into the regulated perimeter instead of pretending that agent wallets are just software.

### Major Banks Deploy Frontier AI for Cybersecurity Defense as Threat Surface Widens
**Source:** [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/banks-ai-push-accelerates-jpmorgan-stanley-citi-bny-goldman-security-worries/817923/) - **Date:** May 2026

Banking Dive reported that major banks are using or evaluating frontier AI for defensive work as Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview and similar systems raise the stakes for vulnerability discovery. JPMorgan is a named launch partner in Anthropic's Project Glasswing, while BNY Mellon is participating in OpenAI's Trusted Access for Cyber program. KPMG survey data cited in the report found that 80% of banking executives are including cybersecurity in AI budgets.

The dual-use problem is now operational, not theoretical. The same frontier models that help security teams find vulnerabilities can also help attackers move faster. Defensive AI is necessary, but it creates a governance problem of its own: who can access these tools, how findings are triaged, how disclosure is controlled, and how the tool itself is protected from misuse. Banks need AI security governance with the same seriousness they apply to model risk and privileged access. Anything less is unserious.
