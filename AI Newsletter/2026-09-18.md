### Ant International Launches Full-Stack AI-Native Platform for Global Payments and Treasury

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/18/ant-international-hands-payment-control-to-ai-agents/)  •  **Date:** September 18, 2026

Ant International unveiled what it calls the first full-stack AI-native infrastructure for global payments, spanning its Alipay+, Antom, WorldFirst, and Bettr business units. The suite covers accounts, FX, treasury, and merchant growth through nearly 100 integrated products, including a dedicated "Account for Agent" for autonomous AI operations. Two proprietary foundation models underpin the platform: the Antom 3-in-1 Transformer (10+ billion parameters, processing 90 trillion data points annually) for payment fraud and risk, and FalconTST (8.5 billion parameters) for FX and liquidity forecasting, which the company says achieves over 93% accuracy and can reduce hedging costs by 30–60%. The company also introduced AgentSafePay, a guarantee against agent-specific fraud, and a dynamic know-your-agent verification layer. Ant reports that 89.5% of its Antom merchant base deployed AI agents in the past year, with the newest risk tier cutting chargeback rates by as much as 87%—both figures are company-reported.

This is the most architecturally complete public articulation of an AI-native payments stack to date, combining autonomous agent accounts, foundation models for risk and FX, and fraud guarantees in one release. The operational questions are proportionally serious: merchants and partner banks need to assess how AgentSafePay defines covered versus excluded losses, how know-your-agent verification behaves when an agent is updated or revoked, and how FalconTST's forecast accuracy holds across currency pairs and stress conditions the training set did not cover. Banks integrating with Ant's infrastructure should also validate that transaction monitoring, dispute handling, and regulatory reporting work correctly when neither buyer nor seller is human.

### Anthropic Tests "Claude Money" Feature for Bank Account Linking

**Source:** [gHacks Tech News](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/09/17/anthropic-is-testing-claude-money-a-feature-to-connect-bank-accounts-and-analyze-finances/)  •  **Date:** September 17, 2026

Researchers discovered an unreleased "Money" tab in the Claude iOS app that invites users to link their bank accounts and ask Claude about spending, plans, and budgeting. The interface appears alongside Chats, Code, Artifacts, Dispatch, and Cowork sections and suggests the feature is designed to give Claude persistent access to a user's financial context—removing the need to manually upload statements—for analysis of spending patterns, recurring payments, balances, and financial planning. Anthropic has not made an official announcement, and the feature is not yet live for most users. OpenAI introduced a comparable feature earlier in 2026; Anthropic's is now confirmed in pre-release testing.

A general-purpose AI assistant with standing read access to a linked bank account is a materially different product from a document-based financial chatbot. The key questions before this reaches consumers at scale concern the identity of the financial data aggregator, data retention and training use of transaction history, scope limitations that prevent the assistant from acting on accounts rather than only reading them, how the system responds when a user asks for advice that crosses into regulated territory, and what consumer protections apply when Claude's analysis leads to a financial decision. Banks whose customers link accounts through this channel should expect increased account-information-service queries and should test consent scope, revocation, and customer support flows before the feature is broadly released.

### Earnix Launches Agent Hub With 25+ Insurance-Specific AI Agents

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/17/earnix-pushes-insurance-ai-from-advice-into-action/)  •  **Date:** September 17, 2026

Earnix introduced Agent Hub, integrating over 25 insurance-specific AI agents into its AI Orchestration System (AIOS) for pricing, underwriting, and customer engagement. The launch moves the company's positioning from providing AI-generated recommendations to executing decisions within insurer workflows, with the company stating that agents operate within defined permissions and traceability controls and that human oversight is retained for decisions requiring judgment and accountability. Named agents include a Model Feature Mapper connecting pricing variables to model inputs, a Product Expert Advisor providing real-time product information, and a Premium Explainer delivering personalized premium breakdowns to customers. The aim is to help insurers maintain current risk intelligence as markets and customer behavior change faster than traditional review cycles allow.

The direction is commercially important: insurance AI is transitioning from the analyst desk into the operating system of pricing and underwriting, where a misfire affects premiums at scale rather than a single quotation. Insurers deploying Agent Hub should require clear evidence for each agent of which decisions fall within autonomous scope versus which trigger mandatory human review, how traceability logs capture the data, model version, and policy basis for each action, what happens when an agent recommendation conflicts with an actuary's judgment, and whether the governance framework satisfies regulatory sign-off for automated pricing and underwriting decisions in each jurisdiction.

GenWay Home Mortgage, a US wholesale and correspondent lender specializing in non-QM and Ginnie Mae lending, went live with six underwriting automation products from Tavant's TOUCHLESS platform within three months of deployment. Tavant reports that TOUCHLESS has delivered up to 60% operational cost reduction, 4–12x underwriting throughput, and loan cycle times shortened from 30–45 days to 7–15 days across its customer base; these are vendor-reported averages, not GenWay-specific measurements.

Non-QM lending is a higher-documentation, higher-judgment segment of the mortgage market, which makes the deployment more consequential than conventional-loan automation. Lenders adopting similar automation should test whether AI document processing correctly handles the income and asset verification complexity that defines non-QM eligibility, validate that Ginnie Mae program rules are encoded accurately including any updates since training, confirm that exception workflows reach an underwriter with the correct context rather than routing ambiguous cases back through automated loops, and document how the system handles a borrower challenge or QC finding after a loan is manufactured.

### Abrigo Launches AI Automation for CECL Credit-Loss Workflows

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/17/abrigo-bets-on-ai-to-cut-cecl-workload-for-banks/)  •  **Date:** September 17, 2026

Abrigo introduced Allowance – Intelligent Automation, an add-on to its existing credit-loss platform that automates the operating work surrounding CECL compliance. Rather than only running calculations, the product manages the full cycle: launching configured tasks, refreshing forecasts, reviewing results, documenting changes between periods, and completing governance sign-off requirements. AI-generated narratives explain what changed in the allowance estimate and why. Abrigo says the tool will be available to over 1,000 financial institutions using its platform by end of September 2026.

Quarter-end and month-end CECL close is a resource-intensive process where errors carry audit, regulatory, and restatement risk, so automation that reduces manual steps while generating explanatory documentation is genuinely useful. The important boundary is between workflow automation and model governance: institutions still need to validate that the AI narratives accurately describe model drivers rather than producing plausible-sounding explanations that mask a calculation error, confirm that the sign-off workflow preserves a defensible record of human review, and test what happens when a model run produces an unexpected result—whether the system flags it or routes it forward. Regulators expect banks to explain not only the allowance number but the process that produced it.

### MIND Raises $72M to Stop Sensitive Data Leaking Into AI Tools

**Source:** [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/18/mind-raises-72m-as-ai-turns-data-security-urgent/) / [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/mind-secures-72-million-for-ai-powered-dlp/)  •  **Date:** September 17, 2026

MIND, an AI-native data loss prevention platform, raised a $72 million Series B led by Crosspoint Capital Partners, with participation from YL Ventures and Paladin Capital Group, bringing its total funding to $112 million. The company says it achieved over 17x revenue growth and 8x customer growth in the past year. MIND's platform monitors endpoints and cloud services to prevent sensitive data—including client records, model training data, and proprietary deal information—from being pasted into public AI tools such as ChatGPT or Copilot. The fundraise comes as financial institutions face regulatory and operational pressure to govern how employees use external AI services with confidential information.

As AI tools become standard in banking workflows, the risk of inadvertent disclosure of client data, MNPI, or regulated information through AI prompts is no longer theoretical. MIND's approach of intercepting and classifying data at the point of input addresses a gap that traditional perimeter controls miss, but financial institutions evaluating DLP tools for AI contexts should test classification accuracy across structured and unstructured financial data, verify that the platform's own processing does not introduce a secondary data-exposure path, assess latency impact on time-sensitive workflows, and confirm that alert and block decisions are explainable and auditable for compliance evidence. Vendor growth metrics are not a substitute for independent evaluation in the institution's own environment.
