### UK Firms Have Deployed Agents, But Broad Autonomy Is Rare

**Source:** [Bank of England and FCA](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2026/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2026) | **Date:** October 26, 2026

The Bank of England and FCA's 2026 survey of 196 UK financial-services firms found that 86% of respondents use AI, 80% reported AI agents in deployment, and another 14% planned deployment within 12 months. The autonomy headline is more restrained: 80% reported production agents that provide decision support, while only 2% reported agents with broad autonomy. Third-party implementations accounted for 39% of reported AI systems and 53% of high-materiality systems. These are aggregated survey responses, not a census or an assurance review of firms' controls.

The market is further along in deployment than in delegation. Boards should stop treating the word "agent" as an adoption metric and ask for autonomy levels by use case, tested intervention points, third-party model mapping, monitoring and recertification evidence, and the exact actions a human can still stop. The risk is not only a bad model. It is a large number of firms relying on similar providers while their post-deployment controls remain less mature than their pre-deployment approvals.

### FCA Proposes Financial Crime Guide Updates for AI-Era Controls

**Source:** [Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-38-financial-crime-guide-updates) | **Date:** October 23, 2026

The FCA opened consultation CP26/38 to update its Financial Crime Guide across fraud, cryptoassets, financial and trade sanctions, proliferation financing, data security, financial-crime systems and controls, and money laundering and terrorist financing. The FCA says the guide needs to keep pace with AI-related risks, evolving sanctions regimes, cryptoasset regulation, and firms' growing use of AI and third-party tools. Responses are due December 24, 2026. This is a consultation, not final guidance.

The practical message is that AI controls are becoming part of ordinary financial-crime supervision. Firms should map where vendors configure or materially influence detection settings, how synthetic identities and deepfakes affect control design, and whether an analyst can reconstruct an AI-assisted alert or case decision from retained evidence. A vendor's assurance pack is not a substitute for institution-specific testing, escalation, and accountability.

### Narmi Adds Read-Only Banking Data to Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Source:** [Narmi via ACCESS Newswire](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/banking-and-financial-services/narmi-launches-microsoft-365-copilot-connector-bringing-customer-fin-1239591) | **Date:** October 26, 2026

Narmi announced an MCP connector that lets bank and credit-union staff use Microsoft 365 Copilot to retrieve authorized customer information through natural-language queries. The company says the connector supports relationship management, cash-flow and line-of-credit analysis, lending and liquidity review, unusual-payment analysis, fraud inquiries, and onboarding status checks. Narmi says it preserves existing institutional permissions and is read-only, so the assistant cannot modify accounts or initiate transactions. These are company-reported capabilities, not evidence of a customer rollout or control effectiveness.

Read-only access is a sensible first boundary because it separates analysis from money movement. It does not settle the harder questions: whether prompts and outputs are fully logged, whether sensitive fields are minimized, how access is tested across customer relationships, and how the bank detects prompt injection or cross-customer leakage. Community banks should treat the connector as an access-control integration that needs its own security and privacy tests, not as a safe default for every employee.

### Tokio Marine Insures Autonomous AI Robots, While Excluding Cyber Losses

**Source:** [Tokio Marine & Nichido Fire Insurance](https://www.tokiomarine-nichido.co.jp/company/release/pdf/261026_01.pdf) | **Date:** October 26, 2026

Tokio Marine & Nichido Fire Insurance said it will begin offering AI Robot Insurance in November 2026 for autonomous mobile robots, including wheeled, quadruped, and humanoid robots. The Japanese product covers physical damage, third-party liability, and business-continuity costs such as substitute labor or rental robots when a covered physical loss stops operations. The release says cyber-incident losses are excluded and that underwriting terms and premiums will be set individually based on the robot's use and operating environment.

Insurance is starting to productize physical-AI exposure, but the exclusion matters more than the novelty. Operators remain responsible for cyber compromise, software failure, model behavior, and weak human-override controls unless they buy other protection. Underwriters will need evidence about telemetry, safety cases, incident history, maintenance, access controls, and fallback procedures before this market can move beyond bespoke coverage.

### EBRD Selects Cognizant for a Five-Year AI-Led Technology Blueprint

**Source:** [Cognizant](https://news.cognizant.com/2026-10-26-Cognizant-and-EBRD-Partner-to-Accelerate-Digital-Transformation-Through-AI-Led%2C-Outcome-Driven-Services) | **Date:** October 26, 2026

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development selected Cognizant as a strategic partner under a five-year framework supporting its Tech Blueprint. The announced scope spans managed and fixed-capacity services for digital and workplace management, cloud infrastructure, data and security, software engineering, automation, and AI. Cognizant plans to bring Neuro IT Operations, the generative and agentic AI-enabled Flowsource software-development offering, and WorkNEXT workplace orchestration to the bank. This is an announced framework and planned scope, not measured production evidence.

The important shift is organizational: AI is being placed inside a bank's managed technology estate, where resilience, security, and supplier dependence become part of the same decision. EBRD should require service-level objectives, data-boundary evidence, model-change notification, human escalation, audit retention, rollback, and a credible exit plan before calling this an AI transformation. A five-year vendor framework can create capacity, but it can also turn one supplier into a critical operational dependency.
