### Nationwide Puts a Financial-Services LLM Into Live Compliance Testing
**Source:** [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/fintech/nationwide-deploys-avenis-finllm-to-support-ai-transformation/) - **Date:** June 16, 2026

Nationwide Building Society became the first organisation to deploy Aveni's FinLLM in a live environment outside Aveni's core product suite. The UK building society is testing the model across financial-services compliance use cases, with broader applications planned after testing. Aveni says FinLLM is a family of financial-services-specific language models trained for regulated workflows, supervisory expectations, compliance, safety, and performance. Nationwide has been both an investor in Aveni since 2024 and an active co-creator of the model.

This is the right direction for bank AI. General-purpose models are useful, but regulated workflows need narrower models, defined evidence trails, and governance that fits the job. Compliance teams should care less about whether the model sounds fluent and more about whether it understands the rules, preserves source evidence, handles structured and unstructured data cleanly, and can be tested against real supervisory expectations.

### Broadridge-Backed LTX Moves BondGPT From Answers to Trade Workflow Action
**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ltx-launches-agentic-ai-in-bondgpt-turning-ai-insights-into-trading-action-302801555.html) - **Date:** June 16, 2026

LTX, the Broadridge-backed corporate bond e-trading venue, launched agentic capabilities inside BondGPT. Users can create agents that monitor real-time market conditions, surface opportunities, create trade tickets, select dealers, launch RFQs, accept prices, and execute defined workflow tasks under trader-set parameters and human oversight. The company frames the update as a move from search and analysis into implementation and execution.

Fixed income is exactly where agentic AI becomes serious. Corporate bond trading is fragmented, time-sensitive, and documentation-heavy. Once an AI system can move from insight to RFQ or execution, controls need to cover permissioning, limit checks, pre-trade review, post-trade evidence, exception handling, and accountability for delegated actions. "Human oversight" cannot be a slogan. It has to be embedded in the workflow.

### Glia Brings AI Outbound Calling to Community Banks and Credit Unions
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fintech/news/glia-ai-outreach-targets-community-banks-with-automated-calls) - **Date:** June 16, 2026

Glia launched AI Outreach, an outbound voice and SMS engagement product for community and regional banks and credit unions. The product uses banking-specific AI agents to contact customers about certificate and share certificate maturities, deposit growth opportunities, loan payment reminders, and account opportunities. It can answer routine questions, handle objections, and route qualified customers to human representatives with the conversation context preserved.

This is not glamorous AI, but it is operationally important. Deposit retention and loan-servicing outreach are high-volume, timing-sensitive workflows that smaller institutions rarely staff well. The risk is conduct. Banks need call scripts, consent records, escalation rules, fair-treatment monitoring, complaint capture, and evidence that vulnerable customers are not being pushed through automated outreach without adequate safeguards.

### SEBI Plans Responsible AI Guidelines for Capital Markets
**Source:** [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/sebi-to-issue-guidelines-on-responsible-use-of-ai-in-capital-markets-chief-pandey-says/articleshow/131710055.cms?from=mdr) - **Date:** June 13, 2026

India's Securities and Exchange Board is working on detailed guidelines for responsible AI use in the capital-markets ecosystem. SEBI chair Tuhin Kanta Pandey said AI can improve surveillance, risk assessment, fraud detection, and investor servicing, but also creates risks around opacity, bias, data protection, cybersecurity, and accountability. He said IOSCO's AI supervisory toolkit will be integrated into SEBI's strategy for regulated entities.

This is not just an India story. Market regulators are converging on the same problem: AI is useful for surveillance and investor service, but opaque models can damage market integrity if responsibility is unclear. Brokerages, exchanges, asset managers, and market infrastructure providers should expect AI governance to become part of ordinary supervision, especially where models touch trading, advice, surveillance, or customer outcomes.

### OneSpan Pushes Digital Credentials as a Defense Against AI-Driven Identity Fraud
**Source:** [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/onespan-previews-digital-credentials-solution-ahead-of-eidas-20-wallet-deadlines) - **Date:** June 16, 2026

OneSpan opened early access to a digital credentials solution for issuing, managing, and verifying identities across digital wallets. The launch is tied to eIDAS 2.0 deadlines: EU member states must make digital identity wallets available by December 2026, and banks and other relying parties must support digital-wallet onboarding and authentication by December 2027. The Paypers notes that reusable digital credentials can reduce dependence on document uploads and selfie-based checks, both of which are increasingly exposed to AI-driven fraud.

Banks should treat digital wallets as an identity-infrastructure project, not just a compliance date. Deepfakes and synthetic documents are weakening old onboarding controls. Credential-based authentication can help, but only if banks design revocation, consent, wallet trust, fraud analytics, and fallback processes properly. A weak wallet integration just moves the attack surface.
