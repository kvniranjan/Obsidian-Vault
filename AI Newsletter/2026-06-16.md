A $3.5 million hire solves a headcount problem; it does not solve the harder question of who owns the risk when a model behaves unexpectedly in a regulated workflow.

### The Fed's Updated Model Risk Guidance Deliberately Leaves Generative and Agentic AI Out of Scope
**Source:** [Federal Reserve SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm) / [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/April/OCC-Fed-FDIC-Issue-Revised-Guidance-Model-Risk-Management) / [Cutover](https://cutover.com/blog/what-sr-26-2-means-for-banks-deploying-agentic-ai)  •  **Date:** April 17, 2026

On April 17, the Federal Reserve, OCC, and FDIC issued SR 26-2, the first overhaul of model risk management guidance since SR 11-7 in 2011. The revised guidance updates validation, governance, and documentation expectations for traditional statistical models — but it explicitly carves out generative AI and agentic AI systems as "novel and rapidly evolving," stating they fall outside the guidance's scope while promising a future request for information. That future RFI has not yet been issued. Meanwhile, the same agencies' examiners are already asking banks about kill switches, kill-switch readiness gaps, and agentic AI governance during routine exams.

The gap matters operationally. Banks deploying large language models in lending, KYC, compliance, or customer workflows cannot rely on SR 26-2 to define what "adequate governance" means for those systems — but they also cannot claim a governance-free zone when an examiner arrives. Banks should be building GenAI-specific model inventory, validation records, human-oversight documentation, and incident playbooks now, rather than waiting for formal guidance that the agencies themselves describe as forthcoming.

### Colorado's Landmark AI Law Was Replaced After a Federal Court Blocked It
**Source:** [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/colorado-ai-law-in-flux-comprehensive-replacement-bill-signed-after-federal-court-blocks-predecessors-enforcement/) / [AiLawsByState](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/blog/colorado-ai-act-compliance-guide-2026) / [Hudson Cook](https://www.hudsoncook.com/article/colorado-special-session-update-ai-law-delayed-to-june-2026-what-the-rental-housing-and-financial-services-industries-can-do-next/)  •  **Date:** June 2026

A federal magistrate judge blocked enforcement of Colorado's SB 24-205 — the first U.S. state AI law covering consequential automated decisions in lending, credit, and insurance — after xAI filed a constitutional challenge and the Department of Justice argued the law violated First Amendment rights through compelled speech and imposed race- and sex-conscious obligations. Colorado's legislature responded with SB 26-189, which repeals SB 205 and replaces it with a narrower framework: impact assessments and anti-discrimination obligations are removed, developer requirements are reduced to technical documentation and material-update notices, and consumer-facing obligations are limited to plain-language disclosures of AI's role within 30 days of an adverse outcome. The new law takes effect January 1, 2027.

Lenders and insurers deploying AI in credit, underwriting, or account-management decisions get more time and far less process, but the floor is not zero. Covered deployers must implement notice mechanisms and adverse-outcome explanation processes before year-end 2026. More importantly, the Colorado sequence is a signal: the first serious attempt to impose substantive anti-bias assessment on lending AI was struck down on constitutional grounds before it took effect. That result will shape how other states and federal agencies draft AI obligations for financial services in the next legislative cycle.

### Australia's AML Perimeter Expands to 100,000 New Entities in Two Weeks — and AI Is Carrying the Compliance Load
**Source:** [Moody's / AUSTRAC Tranche 2](https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/kyc/sectors/corporates/tranche-2-reforms.html) / [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-au/knowledge/publications/bae065f5/tranche-2) / [Truth Technologies / AUSTRAC](https://truthtechnologies.com/austrac-aml-ctf-rules-2026/)  •  **Date:** July 1, 2026 deadline

Australia's AML/CTF Tranche 2 reforms take effect July 1, 2026, bringing lawyers, accountants, real estate agents, jewellers, and other previously exempt professional service firms under AUSTRAC reporting and compliance obligations for the first time. Up to 100,000 new entities must enrol with AUSTRAC by the deadline. The scale of onboarding — combined with the complexity of customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, and transaction monitoring for firms that have never run AML programs — is driving a rush of AI-powered compliance deployments. A Concentrix/Napier AI collaboration announced in June is targeting Australian and New Zealand financial institutions and Tranche 2 entities with graph-based analytics, machine-learning anomaly detection, and automated investigation workflows.

This is the largest single expansion of an AML regulated perimeter in Australia's history, happening in two weeks. Banks and fintechs serving these newly regulated sectors as customers now face a due-diligence obligation: are those counterparties actually compliant? AI can accelerate onboarding and monitoring, but the governance questions — false positive rates, model explainability, human escalation paths, data lineage, and regulator auditability — apply whether the covered entity is a multinational bank or a suburban law firm.

### Visa's Pismo Says AI Has Made Banking's Legacy Infrastructure Problem Impossible to Hide
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/payments-innovation/2026/visas-pismo-says-ai-has-exposed-bankings-old-tech-problem/)  •  **Date:** June 2026

Pismo's Leonardo Collado, whose cloud-native core banking platform is now part of Visa, argued in a PYMNTS interview that AI has reset infrastructure expectations in a way that legacy batch-processing systems simply cannot meet. About 70% of bank IT budgets, Collado says, are consumed by maintaining outdated systems, leaving almost nothing for the real-time personalization and contextual decisioning that AI requires. His examples: T2P in Thailand migrated 320,000 customer accounts to cloud-native infrastructure in three months; Lunar Bank in Denmark runs over one million Nordic users on the same stack. The core claim is blunt — "you can't just place new tech on old stack."

The implication for AI strategy is structural, not cosmetic. Banks deploying LLM-based assistants or agentic workflows on top of fragmented core systems will hit latency, data consistency, and auditability ceilings that no prompt-engineering fix will solve. Boards that approved AI budgets without also approving infrastructure modernization may find they have bought a capability they cannot reliably run. The infrastructure conversation is now inseparable from the AI governance conversation.
