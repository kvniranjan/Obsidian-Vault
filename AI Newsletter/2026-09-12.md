### US Regulators Recast Third-Party Oversight for AI-Heavy Bank Stacks

**Source:** [Federal banking agencies](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-ia-2026-77.html) - **Date:** September 11, 2026

The FDIC, Federal Reserve, NCUA, and OCC requested comment on proposed principles-based guidance for managing third-party relationships. The agencies said the guidance would help banks and credit unions tailor oversight to the risk of each relationship, replace existing third-party guidance if finalized, and encourage prudent innovation. Comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register. The OCC separately said its model-risk guidance would be risk-based and proportionate for community banks.

This is not an AI rule, but it is directly relevant to banks buying AI through core providers, cloud platforms, fintechs, and model vendors. A more proportionate regime may reduce process burden without reducing the need to understand data access, subcontractors, model use, resilience, incident reporting, and exit options. Banks should use the comment period to map which AI dependencies are genuinely critical rather than treating every vendor questionnaire as equivalent.

### Ripple Expands Governed AI Across Enterprise Treasury

**Source:** [Ripple Treasury](https://ripple.com/ripple-press/ripple-treasury-brings-industry-s-first-governed-ai-for-enterprise-treasury/) - **Date:** September 10, 2026

Ripple expanded GSmart inside Ripple Treasury across forecasting, liquidity, risk, reconciliation, and reporting. The company says deterministic engines perform the financial calculations while AI interprets policy, identifies patterns, and explains recommendations. Each agent proposes an action, cites the relevant policy clause, and waits for approval before execution. Ripple also introduced Knowledge Studio as a policy layer and said 60% of eligible customers use Risk Insights while 44% use Forecast Insights, both company-reported adoption figures.

The architecture is more credible than letting a general chatbot calculate or move money because it separates computation, interpretation, policy, and approval. Treasury teams still need to test stale balances, conflicting policies, missing data, model drift, and approval fatigue. The useful control is not simply a human in the loop, but a human who can see the source data, policy basis, proposed consequence, and full audit trail before approving a financial action.

### Figure and Sierra Add Long-Horizon Agents to Home Equity Lending

**Source:** [Figure Technology Solutions](https://investors.figure.com/news-releases/news-release-details/figure-partners-sierra-supercharge-loan-officers-ai-agents-turn) - **Date:** September 10, 2026

Figure and Sierra partnered to deploy a Sierra Horizon agent inside Figure's loan origination system to recover stalled home equity applications. The agent contacts applicants through voice and SMS over multiple days, helps with credit-check permissions, identity verification, and bank-account linking, then hands the applicant to a loan officer to finalize the loan. Figure reported that applicants who interacted with the agent progressed through friction stages 30% to 52% more often, funded 67% more loan volume, and achieved a 143% lift in funded-loan conversion when the agent worked with loan officers rather than loan officers working alone. The analysis was conducted by Figure in July 2026.

This is a meaningful move from service automation into revenue-generating lending workflow, but the reported lift is not an independent causal study. The control questions are sharper than the conversion headline: did applicants consent to repeated outreach, did the agent explain its role, were vulnerable borrowers treated fairly, and did it alter access to credit or only remove administrative friction? Lenders should separate lead recovery from underwriting, log every interaction, and preserve a clean human path for complaints and adverse outcomes.

### Trust Bank Puts Natural-Language Card Spending Queries in Its App

**Source:** [Trust Bank Singapore](https://trustbank.sg/trust-ai-help/) - **Date:** September 8, 2026

Trust Bank launched Trust AI Ask, allowing customers to ask about card transactions by merchant, category, amount, or date inside the app. The bank says Trust AI only sees information already visible to the customer, does not share the data with third parties or use it for other purposes, and hands complex questions to customer service with the conversation context intact. The bank's newsroom lists the product release on September 8.

The feature is simple, but account-specific answers are a higher-risk surface than a traditional FAQ bot. Banks need evaluations for transaction aggregation, date ranges, pending items, refunds, supplementary cards, data freshness, and prompt ambiguity. A clear human handoff and a read-only scope are sensible starting controls, but the bank still has to prove that the assistant cannot expose information a user is not entitled to see or invent an answer when the ledger is incomplete.

### Stripe Connects Meta Muse to an Agent Wallet for Internet Purchases

**Source:** [Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/newsroom/news/stripe-helps-meta-muse-shop-with-link) - **Date:** September 8, 2026

Stripe integrated Link's wallet for agents with Meta's Muse personal AI agent. Stripe says Muse can use a consumer's saved payment method at more than one million Link businesses, while other merchants can be paid through a single-use virtual card scoped to the approved purchase. Consumers approve the total in the chat interface, and Muse does not see the underlying payment details. Stripe says Link has more than 300 million users globally.

This is a concrete payment pattern for agentic commerce: narrow purchase scope, tokenized credentials, explicit approval, and reduced exposure of the real card number. It does not solve issuer visibility, merchant fraud, refunds, disputes, unauthorized-agent claims, or the question of who is liable when an agent follows a bad instruction. Banks and payment providers should require transaction-level agent metadata and preserve the user's ability to revoke authority and challenge the result.
