### ITC Vegas 2026 Closes: "Efficiency AI vs. Decision AI" Is Insurance's Real Strategic Divide

**Source:** [Zero Legacy Press](https://zerolegacypress.com/dispatches/itc-vegas-2026-why-insurers-get-value-from-ai-federato-gallagher-re/)  •  **Date:** October 1, 2026

As ITC Vegas 2026 concluded its three-day run at Mandalay Bay in Las Vegas, the clearest analytical framework to emerge from the conference's AI programming came from Gallagher Re's Freddie Scarratt and research presented by Federato. Scarratt articulated a two-category AI model for insurers: efficiency AI (saves analyst and underwriter hours by automating repetitive tasks) and decision AI (improves the quality of underwriting outcomes by incorporating signals humans cannot efficiently process). The key insight is that efficiency AI is a prerequisite for decision AI — carriers that skip the efficiency layer and attempt to deploy decision AI immediately find their teams lack the bandwidth to supervise and improve the more consequential models. Federato's survey data supplied the urgency: 93% of senior insurance leaders believe underwriting guidelines are applied consistently across their books, while 88% of frontline employees report material deviations due to missing data, disconnected systems, or workflow constraints. That 40-percentage-point perception gap is exactly where AI-assisted underwriting creates recoverable value. Scarratt also warned that "AI amplifies whatever it is given" — poor foundational data produces poor underwriting decisions at scale.

The efficiency-first, then decision-AI sequencing is practical investment guidance for P&C carriers building AI roadmaps. Carriers that cannot yet demonstrate consistent guideline application across underwriters are not ready for autonomous decision AI — they are ready for AI that surfaces the inconsistency, which is a different and more modest capability. The Federato data point — 93% vs 88% — also suggests that insurers using self-reported compliance as a governance input rather than behavioral data are systematically underestimating their own exposure.
