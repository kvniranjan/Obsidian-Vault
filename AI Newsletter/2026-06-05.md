### OpenAI Pushes Codex Into Equity Research and Investment Banking Workflows
**Source:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/codex-for-every-role-tool-workflow/) / [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/47846/openai-launches-codex-plugins-for-finance-pros) - **Date:** June 2, 2026

OpenAI launched role-specific Codex plugins for public equity investing and investment banking, alongside plugins for data analytics, sales, creative production, and product design. The public-equity plugin is designed to help investors review earnings, compare companies, track market signals, and assess investment theses using sources including Moody's, Daloopa, Datasite, FactSet, LSEG, S&P, PitchBook, and Hebbia. The investment-banking plugin is built to turn research and diligence into client-ready materials, including pitch materials, comparable-company analysis, transaction analysis, and recommendations. OpenAI said Codex now has more than five million weekly active users, with knowledge workers representing about 20% of users and growing more than three times as fast as developers.

This is a real distribution move into institutional finance, not another generic chatbot launch. For banks, asset managers, and advisory firms, the relevant issue is permissioning and source control. If an AI workspace can pull from market data, diligence rooms, and internal materials, the control surface becomes entitlement management, record retention, citation quality, and supervisory review. Finance leaders should treat these plugins like workflow infrastructure. If they are allowed into production without clear data boundaries and review evidence, the productivity upside will arrive with a compliance mess attached.

### Hong Kong SFC Issues AI Cyber Warning to Licensed Firms
**Source:** [SFC](https://www.sfc.hk/sfc/services/dod/DIDoc?DocRef=a89ff72a2c57436e9137690dba66a039) / [Timothy Loh LLP](https://www.timothyloh.com/insights/latest-news/circular-to-licensed-corporations-sfc-licensed-virtual-asset-service-providers-and-associated-entities-enhanced-cybersecurity-measures-to-address-evolving-risks-arising-from-artificial-intelligence-enabled-cyberattacks-20260602) - **Date:** June 2, 2026

Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission issued a circular requiring licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers, and associated entities to strengthen cybersecurity measures against AI-enabled cyberattacks. The SFC cited a rise in Hong Kong cybersecurity incidents to 15,877 in 2025 from 12,536 in 2024. The circular says frontier AI models can reduce the cost, skill, and time needed to identify and exploit vulnerabilities, coordinate attacks across connected systems, and scale phishing, social engineering, deepfake impersonation, and reconnaissance. Firms are expected to tighten asset inventories, patching, access controls, network segmentation, maker-checker controls for high-impact actions, threat monitoring, incident response, third-party controls, and backup procedures.

This is useful because it turns AI cyber risk into concrete control expectations. The SFC is not saying "be careful with AI." It is telling firms what supervisory evidence should exist: inventory, patch process, least privilege, segmentation, escalation, tabletop testing, third-party risk handling, and incident reporting. Firms operating across Asia-Pacific should treat this as part of a regional pattern. Australia, Japan, and Hong Kong have all moved toward explicit AI cyber expectations this year. Senior management cannot dump this on IT and hope it disappears.

### Kodesage Raises $6.6M for On-Prem AI Modernization of Bank and Insurer Legacy Systems
**Source:** [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/47858/kodesage-raises-66m-to-modernise-legacy-enterprise-software-with-on-premise-ai) - **Date:** June 4, 2026

Kodesage raised a $6.6 million seed round led by VentureFriends to expand its on-premise AI platform for modernizing legacy enterprise software in regulated sectors, including banks and insurers. The company says its platform helps enterprises understand, document, and extract business logic from COBOL, PL/SQL, and Oracle Forms systems. It performs deep discovery of complex codebases, generates living documentation, supports migration with context-aware code conversion, automates test generation, and enables AI-powered production support. Kodesage frames its long-term vision as self-healing enterprise applications where systems can learn, propose, test, and validate fixes while engineers guide outcomes.

This is not as flashy as a customer-facing agent, but it hits a deeper bank problem. Legacy systems are where cost, operational fragility, and institutional knowledge loss compound. On-premise deployment is also the right posture for heavily regulated institutions that cannot casually expose core logic or production-support context to external AI services. The risk is obvious: automated modernization can create false confidence if generated documentation, conversions, or fixes are not independently verified. The institutions that get value here will use AI to reduce discovery and support burden, not to bypass engineering controls.

### Capgemini Finds Wealth Management Personalization Still Stuck at 17%
**Source:** [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/47853/capgemini-personalisation-lags-in-wealth-management-reaching-just-17-of-hnwis) - **Date:** June 4, 2026

Capgemini's 2026 World Wealth Report found that only 17% of high-net-worth individuals describe their advisory experience as both seamless and personalized, despite rising expectations and broader AI adoption. The report draws on 6,510 HNWIs, 144 senior wealth management executives, and 1,317 relationship managers across global markets. Capgemini said 42% of HNWIs report having to restate their goals and preferences multiple times to the same firm. The report also found that 41% of advisors' time is consumed by operational tasks, 76% want AI-enabled systems to automate routine work, and 61% want access to a more integrated ecosystem of specialists.

The brutal read is that wealth firms keep talking about personalization while making clients repeat themselves like the firm has no memory. AI can help, but only if the data model and operating model change. A better assistant on top of fragmented client records will just produce a more polished version of the same bad experience. The winners will be firms that use AI to coordinate client context, specialist access, product breadth, and advisor time. The laggards will keep calling generic segmentation "personalization" and wonder why assets fragment across more providers.
