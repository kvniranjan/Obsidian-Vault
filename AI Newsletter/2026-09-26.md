### EU AI Act — First Coordinated Compliance Inspections Target Algorithmic Credit Scoring

**Source:** [European AI Office / national authorities](https://eveaicore.com/blog/eu-ai-act-compliance-guide)  •  **Date:** September 2026

The European AI Office, working with 24 national market surveillance authorities, launched the EU's first coordinated compliance inspections under the AI Act. Algorithmic credit assessment systems in retail banking are one of three named inspection targets alongside automated recruitment screening and AI healthcare triage tools. Non-compliance carries penalties up to €35 million or 3% of global annual turnover. The European Commission separately confirmed in early September that it had sent the first formal information requests to more than 30 AI companies under enforcement authority. High-risk AI systems in the financial sector were required to meet the Act's transparency, traceability, and human-oversight requirements by August 2, 2026.

Banks that have been deploying AI credit models under pre-Act governance frameworks now face active examination, not only self-attestation. The practical gap is documentation: examiners will ask for evidence of conformity assessments, technical documentation, human oversight logs, and post-market monitoring. Institutions that treated the August deadline as the finish line rather than the starting line of continuous compliance are most exposed.

Boards should ask whether the questionnaire output creates actual accountability or merely a documented but unresolved finding.

### FinCEN Identifies Nearly $13 Billion in Suspected Digital Asset Investment Scams

FinCEN issued Alert FIN-2026-Alert005 identifying approximately $12.7 billion in financial activity linked to suspected digital asset investment scams—commonly called "pig butchering" or "romance baiting"—that flowed through the U.S. financial system between September 2023 and December 2025. The schemes are operated primarily by transnational criminal organizations running industrial-scale scam compounds in Southeast Asia. The alert directs financial institutions to enhance transaction monitoring, customer outreach, and suspicious activity reporting for patterns associated with these schemes.

The scale—$13 billion over roughly 27 months—signals that this is not a niche typology but a systematic financial crime threat. Banks should review whether their transaction monitoring rules and SAR narratives are calibrated for the specific behavioral patterns FinCEN described: victim-initiated transfers, crypto on-ramp activity, escalating investment amounts, and wire transfers to unfamiliar overseas counterparties. Institutions using AI-assisted transaction monitoring should test whether these patterns are adequately represented in training data.
