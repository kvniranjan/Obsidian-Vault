### Regulators Are Enforcing AI Compliance Under Decades-Old Rules
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/04/how-regulators-are-using-old-rules-to-catch-ai-firms-out/)  •  **Date:** June 4, 2026

Analysis from Eversheds Sutherland partner and former SEC enforcement official Brian Rubin finds that regulators in the US and UK are applying existing supervision, record-keeping, communications monitoring, and anti-fraud frameworks to AI deployments rather than waiting for AI-specific legislation. Three enforcement patterns are already emerging: AI washing (firms overstating AI capabilities in marketing using existing anti-fraud provisions), operational failures (AI-generated communications leaving firms without review or retained records), and unauthorized use (employees feeding client data into public AI tools in breach of confidentiality rules).

The practical implication is blunt: the absence of AI-specific rules does not create a compliance window. Banks and fintechs that have not built governance infrastructure for AI deployments are already exposed under frameworks that have existed for decades. Documented approval processes, output review workflows, and record-keeping controls need to be in place before tools are deployed, not after an examiner flags the gap.

### ebankIT and Daon Extend Identity Verification Across the Entire Customer Journey
**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/04/ebankit-integrates-daon-to-close-the-fraud-gap/)  •  **Date:** June 4, 2026

ebankIT integrated Daon's identity fraud prevention capabilities into its digital banking platform, embedding continuous biometric verification throughout the customer journey rather than treating identity as a single onboarding checkpoint. The combined offering uses biometric matching, document verification, liveness detection to defeat deepfakes, phishing-resistant authentication, and risk-based re-verification triggers delivered through an API gateway. Banks can now verify identities at onboarding, during high-risk transactions, and whenever behavioral risk signals change.

Traditional digital banking stacks check identity once and then trust all subsequent sessions. That architecture breaks down completely against AI-generated deepfakes, synthetic identities, and session hijacking attacks that enter after the initial verification. Continuous identity assurance is the right technical direction, but it introduces a design tradeoff: friction management at re-verification events. Banks that deploy this well will tie re-verification to actual risk signals rather than arbitrary timers, and will document the logic clearly enough that their fraud operations teams can audit it.

Underwriters gain portfolio-level as well as submission-level visibility into climate and ESG risk.

Commercial property underwriting is where climate AI moves from sustainability reporting into loss prevention. The regulatory dimension is equally important: IFRS-S2 and CSRD obligations mean insurers must be able to document climate risk methodology at the portfolio level, not just describe it in a general statement. Embedding the data source into the workflow is the right way to make that documentation defensible.
