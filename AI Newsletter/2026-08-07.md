### Financial Firms Scale AI Fastest in Controlled Work
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/financial-firms-put-ai-to-work-across-27-tasks-study-finds/) - **Date:** August 7, 2026

PYMNTS reported that banks, insurers, and other financial companies have reached high AI adoption in 27 of 75 tasks studied, far ahead of healthcare and media in the same benchmark. The strongest use cases are still mostly behind the scenes: revenue recognition, credit risk assessment, and sales forecasting. Customer-facing uses such as churn prediction, identity verification, and A/B testing remain less mature.

This is the part banking leaders should not misread. The industry is not failing at AI because it avoids flashy consumer agents. It is scaling where data is easier to validate, decision paths are more traceable, and mistakes are less likely to trigger conduct, fair-lending, or complaint risk. The next constraint is data quality. Fragmented data will keep advanced AI stuck in pockets, no matter how much budget moves into model tooling.

### Enova Says Credit AI Depends on Better Bank Data
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/consumer-finance/2026/enova-ceo-says-better-data-not-ai-hype-will-decide-who-gets-credit/) - **Date:** August 7, 2026

PYMNTS interviewed Enova CEO Steve Cunningham about the lender's pending Grasshopper Bank acquisition, positioning the deal as a way to simplify Enova's operating model and extend customer relationships beyond a single loan. Cunningham argued that transaction-level bank data can help lenders distinguish irregular modern income patterns from real credit deterioration.

The useful lesson is blunt: underwriting advantage is still about information, not AI branding. Machine learning can sharpen a credit decision only if the lender sees income frequency, balances, overdrafts, repayment behavior, and cash-flow changes clearly enough to explain the outcome. For banks and fintech lenders, the hard work is consented data access, model governance, adverse-action discipline, and a product path that does not strand borrowers after one short-term credit event.

### Agentic Commerce Exposes BNPL Checkout Failure Risk
**Source:** [PYMNTS Opinion](https://www.pymnts.com/opinion/2026/splitit-ceo-says-card-linked-payments-can-save-ai-sales/) - **Date:** August 7, 2026

PYMNTS published Splitit CEO Nandan Sheth's argument that traditional point-of-sale BNPL can break inside agentic commerce because an AI shopping agent cannot reliably stop to complete a new credit application. The piece says card-linked installments can reduce checkout failure because the credit decision happened when the card was issued, while the merchant authorizes the full purchase amount and splits payments afterward.

Payment leaders should treat this as a design warning, not a BNPL category fight. Agents will route around checkout flows that require human attention, redirect loops, fresh underwriting, or unclear mandate checks. Issuers, acquirers, wallets, and merchants need to decide which financing products can work with delegated authority, which need live user confirmation, and how failed authorization attempts are logged before agentic commerce quietly sends volume elsewhere.

### Maximum Raises USD 30 Million for AI-Native Bank Infrastructure
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/fintech-start-ups/maximum-emerges-from-stealth-with-30m-seed-round) - **Date:** August 6, 2026

FinTech Futures reported that former Marqeta executive Randy Fernando launched Maximum from stealth with USD 30 million in seed funding. The company describes itself as an AI-powered operating system for banks, using custom agents to automate complex workflows, improve visibility into customer activity, and replace legacy infrastructure that mostly keeps records rather than monitoring and anticipating activity.

This is a direct shot at the core-bank vendor stack. The pitch is attractive because bank operations are full of manual stitching, brittle handoffs, and delayed insight. It is also risky if agents become another opaque layer on top of weak systems. Banks evaluating AI-native infrastructure should demand deterministic controls for regulated decisions, clean audit trails, entitlement boundaries, fallback procedures, and evidence that the system improves operational quality instead of just moving work into a harder-to-review interface.

### Rabobank Commits EUR 2 Billion to AI and Data Foundations
**Source:** [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/ai-in-fintech/rabobank-2bn-ai-commitment) - **Date:** August 6, 2026

FinTech Futures reported that Rabobank plans to commit up to EUR 2 billion over three years to scale AI capabilities, strengthen data foundations, and streamline internal workflows. CEO Stefaan Decraene framed AI and other technologies as a way to transform how the bank works.

The size of the commitment matters less than where it is aimed. Serious bank AI spending is moving into data, reusable workflow infrastructure, and operating-model change, not isolated pilots. The obvious risk is funding theater: large budgets do not guarantee adoption, controls, or ROI. Rabobank's proof point will be whether AI reduces cycle time, improves risk decisions, and creates reusable capabilities across teams without weakening accountability for customer and compliance outcomes.
