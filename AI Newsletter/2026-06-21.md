### Vantage Bank and Custodia Unveil "Hazel Network" for AI-Governed Tokenized Banking
**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vantage-bank-and-custodia-release-white-paper-unveiling-hazel-network-302804851.html)  •  **Date:** June 18, 2026

Vantage Bank and Custodia jointly released a white paper detailing the Hazel Network, a bank-architected token sub-ledger that bridges FDIC-insured deposits and stablecoins on a single smart contract. The dual-character token functions as a bank deposit within the consortium and automatically transforms into a stablecoin outside it — solving the capital-drain problem that has kept most regulated banks out of tokenized systems. The protocol enforces "Compliance by Construction" through three automated screening layers: fiat AML and fraud checks, blockchain analytics, and an on-chain sanctions oracle that blocks sanctioned addresses in real time. A reference implementation launched on Ethereum in March 2026, with full network availability targeted for Q4 2026; banks can integrate through three flexible models in as little as four to six weeks.

### FIS and Anthropic Launch Agentic Financial Crimes AI for Banks
**Source:** [FIS](https://www.fisglobal.com/about-us/media-room/press-release/2026/fis-brings-agentic-ai-to-banking-with-anthropic-starting-with-financial-crimes)  •  **Date:** May 4, 2026

FIS is partnering with Anthropic to deploy a Financial Crimes AI Agent that compresses anti-money-laundering investigations from hours to minutes by automatically assembling evidence across core banking systems and evaluating activity against known typologies. BMO and Amalgamated Bank are among the first institutions in development; general availability is planned for the second half of 2026. Anthropic's Applied AI team is embedded with FIS engineers to co-design the agent in a governed environment where client data stays within FIS-controlled infrastructure and every agent decision is fully auditable — a design pattern intended to satisfy regulatory expectations. The collaboration targets a meaningful share of the estimated $35–40 billion U.S. banks spend annually on AML operations, with a roadmap that extends to credit decisioning, fraud prevention, and customer onboarding.

### Cambridge/BIS/IMF Report: 52% of Financial Firms Already Deploying Agentic AI
**Source:** [Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/faculty-research/centres/alternative-finance/publications/2026-global-ai-in-financial-services-report/)  •  **Date:** April 29, 2026

The Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance, in partnership with the BIS, IMF, World Economic Forum, and World Bank, published its 2026 Global AI in Financial Services Report — the most comprehensive cross-sector benchmark to date, drawing on 628 respondents across 151 jurisdictions. The headline finding: 81% of financial firms are adopting AI at some level, with 52% already integrating agentic AI and 81% expecting it to be mainstream by 2030; fintechs lead traditional incumbents 47% to 30% on advanced deployment. A striking execution gap persists — only 14% view AI as transformational to their strategy, with data quality, talent shortages, and legacy infrastructure cited as the primary blockers. Regulators trail the industry, with 48% of the 130 supervisory bodies surveyed still in early exploration, while adversarial AI attacks emerged as the top systemic risk flagged by nearly half of all respondents.

### Colorado Rewrites Landmark AI Act, Eliminating Financial Institution Exemption
**Source:** [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/05/12/colorado-rewrites-its-landmark-ai-law-unpacking-sb-26-189-and-what-it-means-for-businesses/)  •  **Date:** May 14, 2026 (signed)

Governor Polis signed SB 26-189 into law on May 14, repealing and replacing Colorado's original 2024 AI Act (SB 24-205) before it could take effect. The rewrite narrows obligations to "automated decision-making technology" that influences consequential decisions — explicitly covering credit scoring, lending, and AML risk profiling — and, critically, eliminates the financial institution exemption that had shielded regulated banks and lenders under the prior statute. Firms must now notify consumers when AI influences a decision, disclose adverse outcomes within 30 days, offer meaningful human review, and retain records for three years; the heavier prior requirements for risk impact assessments and attorney general reporting were dropped. The new law takes effect January 1, 2027, giving financial institutions roughly six months to audit their AI decision pipelines and build consumer-facing disclosure workflows.
