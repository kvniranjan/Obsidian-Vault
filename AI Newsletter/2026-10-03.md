### Apollo Economist Warns AI Agents Could Trigger Soft Bank Runs Through Automated Deposit Sweeps

**Source:** [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/ai-agent-bank-runs-possible-but-unlikely-experts-say)  •  **Date:** October 1, 2026

Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok proposed that AI agents like Meta's Muse could systematically sweep consumer deposits from bank accounts earning an average of 0.1% into fintech accounts paying 3.3%–5%, bypassing the depositor inertia that has historically kept cheap funding stable. Slok framed this as a slow but structurally different version of the deposit flight seen during Silicon Valley Bank's collapse — not driven by panic, but by optimization. Most experts interviewed by American Banker view large-scale agentic bank runs as unlikely near-term due to interoperability gaps, authentication complexity, and consumer trust barriers. PSECU's chief member experience officer Erin Hennessy disagreed: "It is absolutely coming."

The debate matters for asset-liability management teams regardless of the timeline. Banks that have modeled retail deposit stickiness based on historical friction should now stress-test for an environment where AI agents continuously optimize yield on the customer's behalf. The structural question is whether deposit pricing becomes as transparent and instantly switchable as a money-market rate — and whether current models for liquidity coverage and net stable funding ratios adequately capture that scenario.

### Regnology/Oliver Wyman Study: 89% of Banks Have Not Moved AI Into Regulatory Reporting Production

**Source:** [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/new-research-89-of-financial-institutions-have-yet-to-embed-ai-into-regulatory-r-0f6173ad)  •  **Date:** September 22, 2026

Regnology published "The Agentic Gap" in collaboration with Oliver Wyman, surveying the state of AI in regulatory reporting. While 87% of institutions are exploring or piloting AI, only 16% have reached production — and 89% have not yet run agentic workflows in a live regulatory reporting cycle. The research identifies governance quality and data readiness, not model capability, as the primary blockers. Tier 1 banks could address 15–25% of their regulatory reporting expenditure — a budget that typically consumes 1–3% of total bank spend, with 30–50% going to manual processes — through governed agentic workflows.

The 89% figure will be used by regulators and vendors for years, so it deserves precise interpretation: it means production-level agentic automation specifically in regulatory reporting, not AI adoption overall. Regulatory reporting is among the highest-accountability workflows in a bank — any error can trigger supervisory action — and that accountability requirement explains both the conservative adoption rate and the emphasis on explainability and human override. The path from pilot to production runs through data quality attestation and audit trail architecture, not better models.

### Axis Bank Plans to Double Data Center Loans as India's AI Infrastructure Demand Accelerates

**Source:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/axis-bank-to-double-data-center-loans-riding-on-india-s-ai-push)  •  **Date:** October 1, 2026

Axis Bank, India's third-largest private lender, plans to double its data center loan exposure over the next three years, from a current book of more than ₹86.88 billion ($907 million) across roughly a dozen clients. The bank is shifting toward cash-flow-based underwriting for these projects — assessing long-term service contracts rather than physical collateral — to match the asset class's economics. The group head of wholesale banking has also begun outreach to Singapore-headquartered data center operators looking to finance Indian capacity, indicating an intent to capture cross-border AI infrastructure demand.

Data center lending is one of the clearest visible signals of where bank credit is following AI capital expenditure. The shift to cash-flow underwriting is the right structural move — data center assets depreciate rapidly and have limited secondary-market liquidity — but it increases underwriter dependence on counterparty quality and contract certainty. Banks building similar books should stress-test hyperscaler concentration, model the impact of a large tenant non-renewal, and pressure-test whether long-term AI demand projections embedded in DCF valuations hold up under a slower-growth scenario.
