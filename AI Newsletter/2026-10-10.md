### Banks Put AI Agents Through Performance Reviews — BNY and DBS Set the Governance Template

**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/banks-put-ai-agents-through-performance-reviews/)  •  **Date:** October 9, 2026

BNY now runs approximately 140 digital "employees," each with login credentials, a corporate directory number, and a named human supervisor. Getting an agent from concept to production requires 16 steps — review boards, compliance gates, and model risk evaluations — and each live agent is tracked on a dashboard showing processed volume, cycle times, missed transactions, and approval outcomes. BNY's payments agent has already compressed cross-border payment validation from up to six minutes to under 30 seconds, and open payment investigations have fallen nearly 80%. The bank's head of talent is building a Digital Employee Agency to decide systematically whether work goes to a person, a contractor, or an agent. Meanwhile, DBS rolled out credit agents to approximately 1,500 institutional banking staff globally in August following a 150-person pilot; the agents handle more than 70 tasks, including drafting first credit memos from annual reports, industry research, and internal records — work that can consume up to 40% of a relationship manager's time. DBS's stated target is a 30% reduction in credit assessment time, though that figure is a goal, not yet a measured outcome. Regulatory context: FINRA's 2026 oversight report added a dedicated generative AI section covering agents acting beyond user authority and hard-to-trace reasoning, and the Conference of State Bank Supervisors released an AI supervisory framework on September 16.

The governance architecture BNY is assembling — named supervisors, a 16-step approval path, live performance dashboards — is the operational answer to the FINRA oversight concerns: auditability and accountability are built into agent deployment, not retrofitted after the fact. For BFSI institutions still treating agents as experimental, the BNY model sets a concrete production standard: agents with defined roles, bounded access, and a human supervisor are the minimum governance unit. Banks that skip these controls in pursuit of faster deployment will face the governance gap in their next regulatory examination. DBS's rollout also confirms that agentic AI is moving fastest where document-heavy professional judgment has been the bottleneck — credit, compliance, and onboarding — not where simple task automation was already possible.

The 988% agent usage growth over three months is more remarkable: it confirms that lenders, once on an agent-capable platform, adopt agents at a rate that suggests genuine productivity benefit rather than experimentation. The strategic implication for the industry is that the LOS is becoming an AI orchestration layer, not just a workflow tool: lenders who remain on legacy systems without an agent-ready substrate are not just missing efficiency gains, they are building a structural cost disadvantage relative to peers who have made the switch.

### Deutsche Bank Private Bank Deploys Agentic AI for Source-of-Wealth KYC in Singapore and Hong Kong

**Source:** [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/deutsche-bank-private-bank-launches-agentic-ai-solution-for-source-of-wealth-kyc-40607b0e)  •  **Date:** September 23, 2026

Deutsche Bank's Private Bank went live with an agentic AI solution for source-of-wealth (SoW) verification in its Singapore and Hong Kong booking centers, with availability extended to Dubai-based advisers serving Singapore-booked accounts. Built into the bank's existing digital KYC platform, the system uses multiple agents to gather information from the client case and approved external sources, flag gaps or inconsistencies in SoW documentation, and prepare structured assessments for relationship manager review — automating the most labor-intensive part of high-net-worth client onboarding. Deutsche Bank estimates the deployment can improve onboarding efficiency by around 30%, though this is a target rather than a measured result from the live deployment. A broader rollout across the Private Bank's global wealth management centers is planned.

Source-of-wealth verification is among the most consequential KYC tasks in private banking: it requires interpreting complex asset histories — inheritance, business sales, investment returns — across multiple jurisdictions, and it is also one of the most examined areas in regulatory reviews of private banking KYC quality. The value of agentic automation here is not just speed; it is consistency — agents apply the same documentation standards to every case, which reduces the dispersion in SoW assessment quality that creates regulatory exposure. The architecture — agents prepare, humans decide — is the right governance model for a process where the bank bears full regulatory accountability for the conclusion. Wealth managers still running SoW preparation through manual analyst workflows should evaluate whether the Deutsche Bank architecture is achievable on their existing KYC platform, or whether platform investment is the prerequisite.
