### Five US Banks Disclaim Liability When Third-Party AI Agents Access Customer Accounts

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/)  •  **Date:** November 18, 2026

Citi updated its Online User Agreement this week with a new Section H covering third-party AI agents that access accounts through Citi Online or Citi Mobile. Under the terms, any activity from credentials handed to an outside agent is treated as the customer's authorized activity — even if the tool ignores instructions, exceeds its permissions, shares credentials with another party, or is hacked. Monitoring the agent is the customer's responsibility, and Citi reserves the right to cut off agent access at any time without notice. TechCrunch separately confirmed that JPMorgan, Bank of America, PNC, and Citizens have adopted similar positions, though the details differ across institutions. Citi carves out its own AI tools from the new liability rule.

The contractual move shifts risk before regulators or courts have settled it. Reg E limits consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers, but Citi's terms pre-emptively define all agent-initiated transactions as customer-authorized — a classification that has not been tested. Banks need to decide how to handle disputed agent transactions, what evidence they will accept that a customer never actually intended an action, and how automated activity interacts with their existing fraud-reversal obligations. The pace at which large institutions are aligning on this language suggests it is becoming industry default rather than one bank's experiment.

### Fed's Barr Identifies AI as the Top Near-Term Risk in the Financial System

**Source:** [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/11/feds-barr-calls-for-responsible-ai-development-in-financial-services/)  •  **Date:** November 16, 2026

Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michael Barr, speaking at the ACM International Conference on AI in Finance in Milan, said the Fed's most recent Financial Stability Report found that financial services firms identified AI as the top near-term risk to the system. Barr named seven specific concerns: AI returns may not justify current debt-fueled infrastructure spending; autonomous AI trading could increase information asymmetry and market concentration; AI agents with retail bank account access are already making it harder for banks to verify authenticity and customer intent; cybersecurity risks including AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and deepfakes; lending bias encoded into AI models; collusion and market manipulation; and systemic volatility from correlated AI strategies. He said he remains an optimist about AI's long-term benefits but that decisions based on generative and agentic AI "must be well controlled, numerically and legally precise, explainable and replicable."

The speech is significant because it names agent-account verification as a live supervisory concern, not a theoretical one — consistent with the same week's wave of bank terms changes disclaiming liability for third-party agents. Barr's framing of data-center debt as a potential financial stability issue also signals that examiners will treat concentrated AI infrastructure exposure as a balance-sheet risk, not just an operational one.

### AI Infrastructure Lending Is Exhausting Credit Insurance Capacity Across Global Markets

**Source:** [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/ai-lending-surge-tests-credit-insurance-capacity-as-banks-seek-cover-596963.aspx)  •  **Date:** November 11, 2026

Citing the Financial Times, Insurance Business reported that Barclays has been sounding out insurers about default protection on more than $10 billion of AI infrastructure loans to free up balance-sheet room for further lending. HSBC and Deutsche Bank are pursuing similar arrangements. More than 70 providers now write project-finance credit insurance — including AIG, Chubb, and AXA — but several have indicated they are near or at sector limits on data-center exposure. The cost of this cover runs at 50% to 70% of the margin banks earn on the covered loans, per the FT. Willis Re has flagged that reinsurers will closely scrutinize data-center concentration at the January 2027 renewals. Direct credit premium at Lloyd's rose about 25% in 2025 to roughly £1.6 billion, and AI-focused companies absorbed 83.2% of global insurtech funding in Q3 2026 even as overall funding fell 44.6% quarter-on-quarter to $1.36 billion.

This is a consequential structural shift: AI infrastructure risk is moving from bank balance sheets into insurance and reinsurance capital. The practical constraint is that insurer capacity is already a binding limit on how fast banks can originate more AI loans. Reinsurers will want to understand correlated concentration — several banks simultaneously holding large positions in the same hyperscaler debt creates a scenario where multiple large claims arrive together, which is different from the idiosyncratic project-finance credit risk the market was originally designed for.

### Corgi Applies for National Bank Charter, Becoming the Latest AI-Native Firm to Seek Direct Banking Access

**Source:** [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/external-article/ai-driven-firm-corgi-ventures-into-banking-after-etfs-insurance)  •  **Date:** November 18, 2026

Corgi, an AI-driven firm that has already built positions in startup insurance and ETFs, filed an application dated November 17 with the OCC for a national bank charter and a separate application with the FDIC for deposit insurance. The move follows a similar filing by neobank Dave and comes as the OCC has been publishing digital-asset and AI-focused charter applications. Corgi raised $108 million to launch an admitted insurance carrier and has partnered with First Citizens and SVB to offer AI-native risk management. These are application filings, not approvals; a national bank charter requires OCC review, examination planning, and FDIC sign-off before it is granted.

The pattern of AI-native firms seeking direct bank charters matters for incumbents. A firm that holds its own charter can offer insured deposits, access the Fed's payment systems, and control the full customer relationship without a bank sponsor — removing a layer of dependency and regulatory arbitrage that most fintechs currently rely on. If multiple AI-native applicants are approved, the competitive response from established banks will need to be more than product features.

### Capgemini: 59% of Financial Institutions Piloting Multi-Agent AI, But Only 10% Achieve Enterprise-Wide Orchestration

**Source:** [Capgemini Research Institute](https://www.capgemini.com/news/press-releases/financial-institutions-accelerate-transition-to-multi-agent-ai-in-drive-for-bottom-line-value/)  •  **Date:** November 2026

Capgemini's World Cloud Report for Financial Services, based on a June–July 2026 survey of 1,100 financial services leaders across 14 markets, found that 59% of banks and insurers are piloting or deploying multi-agent AI systems, but only one in ten has achieved the enterprise-wide orchestration needed to realize full value. More than half of institutions prefer hyperscalers as infrastructure partners, citing regulatory and data-residency certification requirements. Most organizations are deploying or expect to adopt intelligent model routing within a year — directing tasks to the most cost-effective accurate model — as a primary cost-control mechanism. Survey coverage includes retail banking, payments, P&C insurance, and life insurance.

The gap between piloting and enterprise orchestration is the main operational finding. A single-agent deployment in one workflow does not require cross-system coordination, audit consistency, or policy propagation; a multi-agent deployment that spans onboarding, credit, servicing, and collections does. Banks that are still counting pilots should examine whether their governance infrastructure — model registries, permission layers, versioned business rules, escalation queues — is being built in parallel with the agents, or is being left for a future phase that never arrives.

### Gallagher Re: AI Cuts Sovereign Default Lead Time and Loan-Tape Processing in Credit and Trade Finance

**Source:** [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/ai-is-closing-the-data-gap-in-credit-and-trade-finance-underwriting-report-finds-596283.aspx)  •  **Date:** November 5, 2026

Gallagher Re's Q3 2026 Global InsurTech Report, produced with CB Insights, examined AI in credit risk protection, trade finance, and financial protection insurance. Two Lloyd's-market examples: Convergence reports that a 1,300-row loan tape that previously took two to four hours to standardize now takes minutes, and portfolio modeling dropped from about a week to ten to twenty minutes. OAK Global's OCEAN platform links bordereaux standardization, obligor matching, loss simulation, and portfolio analytics to surface hidden repeated exposure to the same borrower across multiple facilities. A third finding covers sovereign risk: a model combining logistic regression with gradient boosting flagged 26 of the last 28 sovereign defaults in back-testing, with a 1.8% false-alarm rate and an average lead time of more than eight months — at a point when global mean sovereign default probability was at its highest level in more than two years.

The practical argument here is about underwriter capacity, not replacement. Gallagher Re's Jonathan Allard says AI will let the same number of underwriters handle meaningfully higher volumes at better accuracy. The sovereign-default finding is worth noting separately: an eight-month lead time is actionable for a carrier deciding whether to renew country-risk cover or reprice a facility, but only if the model's coverage universe, training methodology, and false-negative distributions are disclosed and independently validated.
