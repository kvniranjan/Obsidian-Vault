### Tradeweb Upgrades Ai-Price Bond Pricing Engine With Machine Learning Advances

**Source:** [Tradeweb](https://www.tradeweb.com/newsroom/media-center/news-releases/tradeweb-enhances-ai-price-advancing-corporate-bond-pricing/)  •  **Date:** September 22, 2026

Tradeweb announced a suite of enhancements to Ai-Price, its automated bond pricing engine for US investment-grade and high-yield corporate bonds. The upgrades integrate public TRACE data with Tradeweb's proprietary transaction datasets, apply upgraded machine learning models, and deliver more real-time, intraday-responsive bid and offer estimates that better reflect live market conditions. Fixed income desks should verify that Ai-Price's accuracy holds across low-liquidity periods, stress windows, and corporate actions before reducing manual pricing coverage, and should understand how model updates are versioned, communicated, and reflected in execution audit trails.

### Undeclared AI Is Insurance's Biggest Blind Spot

**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/25/undeclared-ai-is-insurances-biggest-blind-spot/)  •  **Date:** September 25, 2026

KYND published its white paper "The Wild West of AI Risk," finding that undeclared AI—tools used by employees or suppliers without the organization's knowledge or approval—represents a structural gap in insurance risk assessment and coverage. Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report found that regular AI use on corporate devices quadrupled to 45% of employees in a year, with 67% of that activity occurring through personal accounts businesses cannot monitor. IBM data cited in the paper puts roughly one in four malicious breaches as AI-enabled, at an average cost of approximately $6 million—about $1 million higher than a conventional breach. Capgemini found that 42% of property and casualty insurers had not measured their AI outcomes.

For underwriters and CISOs the exposure is bidirectional: insurers cannot accurately price cyber and professional liability policies when policyholders cannot declare the AI tools their staff are using, and the insurers themselves face the same shadow-AI problem internally. Boards should require shadow-AI inventories, acceptable-use policy enforcement, and outcome measurement before renewing any coverage line where undisclosed AI use could change the risk profile.

### AI Fatigue Is Reshaping How Banks Choose RegTech Providers

**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/24/ai-fatigue-is-reshaping-how-banks-choose-regtech-providers/)  •  **Date:** September 24, 2026

Analysis from Corlytics finds that, with regulatory demands mounting and AI marketing claims proliferating, banks are no longer selecting RegTech providers on AI capability claims alone. Firms now emphasize five criteria: precision with traceable results, innovation paired with proper model validation, architecture built for purpose rather than assembled through acquisition, full total-cost-of-ownership transparency, and collaborative partnerships that share accountability for outcomes. Corlytics describes the shift as moving from a software-buying relationship to a partnership model where vendors must deliver "trusted outcomes at scale."

The practical implication is that AI capabilities have become table stakes rather than differentiators in the RegTech market. Compliance and procurement teams applying this lens should require vendors to demonstrate accuracy on institution-specific regulatory texts, explain model version governance, provide measurable false-positive and investigation-time data from comparable clients, and commit to shared accountability when outputs lead to a compliance decision.

### Abrigo Adds AI Automation to CECL Allowance Workflows

**Source:** [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/17/abrigo-bets-on-ai-to-cut-cecl-workload-for-banks/)  •  **Date:** September 16–17, 2026

Abrigo released Abrigo Allowance – Intelligent Automation, an add-on to its existing CECL platform serving more than 1,000 US financial institutions. The product combines automated workflows, AI-powered analysis, and governance and sign-off controls to reduce the manual work surrounding allowance calculations—including running recurring processes, reviewing model results, documenting period-over-period changes, and completing board-level sign-off requirements—which tends to spike during month-end and quarter-end reporting cycles. Abrigo also received the 2026 Chartis Award for Managed Services: Credit Risk. The product is scheduled to be generally available by the end of September 2026.

CECL process automation matters because the surrounding workflow—not the model itself—is often the bottleneck at community and regional banks where allowance teams are small. Finance and credit teams adopting this category should verify that AI-generated narrative summaries remain auditable, that automated sign-off triggers preserve independent challenge requirements under their examination framework, and that model-change documentation captures not only calculation differences but the analytical rationale behind them.

### Hadrius Provides SEC-Compliant AI Governance for Ritholtz Wealth's Firmwide Claude Deployment

**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hadrius-ai-governance-selected-by-ritholtz-wealth-management-to-enable-firmwide-claude-deployment-302878239.html)  •  **Date:** September 15, 2026

Ritholtz Wealth Management, a national RIA managing $9.4 billion, selected Hadrius to monitor Claude Enterprise activity and archive employee interactions across the firm as it deploys Claude firmwide. Hadrius integrates with Anthropic's Compliance API to deliver real-time AI usage metrics and automatically archive employee conversations with Claude alongside all other supervised communication channels, aligned to SEC and FINRA recordkeeping requirements. This case study matters because it demonstrates a compliance architecture for enterprise AI deployment in a registered firm, not a proof-of-concept. RIAs and broker-dealers extending AI access to advisors should treat archiving, review, and supervisory obligation coverage as prerequisites rather than follow-on projects: the recordkeeping and examination exposure begins when the first employee conversation with Claude touches client data or business decisions.
