### Fiserv Brings Cognition's Devin Into Core Banking Modernization
**Source:** [Fiserv](https://investors.fiserv.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fiserv-and-cognition-partner-modernize-banking-technology-and)  -  **Date:** May 28, 2026

Fiserv and Cognition announced a strategic partnership to deploy Devin, Cognition's AI software engineer, across Fiserv's core platform modernization and other engineering initiatives. The companies say Devin will help accelerate complex engineering work, shorten release cycles, improve platform performance, expand automated testing, and strengthen governance for AI-assisted development. Fiserv positioned the move as part of a broader strategy to embed AI not only into customer-facing financial products, but also into the software delivery machinery that powers account processing, payments, digital banking, and related bank infrastructure.

This is not a chatbot story. It is AI moving into the production pipeline of a major bank technology provider. For banks, the benefit is obvious: faster platform upgrades and shorter waits for new capabilities. The risk is just as obvious: AI-assisted development has to be governed like a material change to the software lifecycle. Banks should ask Fiserv how AI-written or AI-modified code is reviewed, tested, logged, segregated, and rolled back. If an AI coding agent accelerates modernization but weakens release assurance, the bargain is bad.

### Robinhood Opens Stock Trading and Credit Card Purchases to AI Agents
**Source:** [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/robinhood-opens-platform-to-ai-agents-for-trading-credit-card-purchases-4712233)  -  **Date:** May 27, 2026

Robinhood said customers will be able to deploy AI agents to trade stocks through a dedicated trading account and make purchases through a virtual Robinhood Gold credit card. The company said agentic trading is initially limited to equities, with expected expansion into derivatives, crypto, and prediction markets. On the card side, users can authorize agents to make automatic purchases under user-set limits and optional manual approval controls. Reuters noted that Robinhood executives framed the first audience as early adopters of agents.

This is the cleanest consumer-finance test case yet for agentic authority. Banks and brokerages should stop pretending the hard question is whether customers want AI help. The hard question is who is liable when a delegated agent makes a bad trade, misreads an instruction, overpays, executes during volatility, or gets manipulated by a malicious prompt chain. Separate accounts and spending limits are a start, not a full control model. Broker-dealers and card issuers will need rules for authorization, suitability, dispute handling, surveillance, and customer complaints before agentic finance becomes mainstream.

### BofA, U.S. Bank, and Finastra Push Payments Modernization Into the AI Era
**Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/payments-innovation/2026/bank-of-america-u-s-bank-and-finastra-push-banks-past-patchwork-payments/)  -  **Date:** May 28, 2026

PYMNTS reported on a payments modernization discussion with Bank of America, U.S. Bank, and Finastra leaders. The core message: corporate clients now expect payments to operate continuously, settle faster, and sit directly inside their own digital channels. Bank of America has moved parts of its payments infrastructure from batch systems to API-based architecture supporting real-time payments across more than 50 countries. U.S. Bank is using SinglePoint to consolidate capabilities for roughly 45,000 clients and is targeting two-day onboarding. The participants also described AI as an operational tool for identifying failed transactions, surfacing repair recommendations, helping teams interrogate legacy systems, and improving product development workflows.

The important line is that payments are increasingly the product, not a back-office function. AI can compress investigation, onboarding, requirements analysis, and exception handling, but payments remain deterministic infrastructure. They have to work every time. Banks should use AI to remove friction around payments, not to make payment execution probabilistic. The governance standard should be simple: AI can recommend, explain, prioritize, and repair, but final movement of money still needs auditable authorization, resilient controls, and clear operational accountability.

### New Zealand FMA Puts AI Financial Advice Into Its Fintech Work Plan
**Source:** [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/nz/news/technology/fma-draws-on-sandbox-lessons-to-build-a-twoyear-fintech-plan-576756.aspx)  -  **Date:** May 28, 2026

New Zealand's Financial Markets Authority released results from its fintech regulatory sandbox pilot and set out an 18 to 24 month work programme. The plan includes expanding the sandbox, deepening work on virtual assets and payments, improving regulatory education, and examining how AI tools are being used in financial advice. The sandbox drew 24 expressions of interest, including KYC and AML tools, AI-driven accounting tools, advice workflow platforms, tokenized real-world assets, payment solutions, and a New Zealand dollar-backed stablecoin. The regulator also plans to introduce an on-ramp licence calibrated to early-stage firms' size and risk profile.

This is what pragmatic AI regulation looks like: not a blanket ban, not blind permission, but a narrower pathway for real firms to test products under supervisory visibility. The AI advice angle matters for banks, insurers, and wealth platforms because advice is where automation collides with suitability, disclosure, conflicts, and customer vulnerability. Regulators are going to study live usage patterns, not just policy documents. Firms should assume screenshots, prompts, escalation rules, model-change logs, and customer outcomes will become part of the supervisory record.

### AI Executive Briefing Flags Agentic Wallets, Financial Services Hiring, and Infrastructure Pressure
**Source:** [Research and Markets via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/28/3302621/0/en/artificial-intelligence-executive-briefing-intelligence-report-2026-ai-will-threaten-every-business-across-every-sector-in-the-coming-years.html)  -  **Date:** May 28, 2026

Research and Markets added a new edition of its artificial intelligence executive briefing focused on generative and agentic AI adoption. The release says the report covers AI hiring in financial services, the effect of agentic e-wallets on conventional payment systems, token consumption pressure on AI infrastructure, and sector-level disruption from more capable AI systems. It also notes that hiring in financial services and information services has plateaued since ChatGPT's launch, while AI roles have become a growing share of new job creation.

The useful signal is not the report's broad warning that AI will threaten every sector. That is table stakes. The sharper point for financial institutions is that agentic wallets and rising AI infrastructure costs are now linked. If AI agents become payment initiators, banks need identity, intent, delegation, spending-control, and dispute infrastructure. If every agentic workflow consumes more tokens and compute, banks also need unit economics discipline. AI strategy cannot be only a capability roadmap. It has to include cost per workflow, model-routing policy, resilience planning, and customer authorization design.
