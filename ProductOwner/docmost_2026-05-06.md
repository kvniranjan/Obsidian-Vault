# docmost

## Verdict
Docmost is worth a product owner's time if product decisions, specs, discovery notes, and team knowledge are scattered across chats, decks, and half-dead documents. It is a real collaborative wiki, not a product-management essay repo. The blunt caveat: it will not fix sloppy thinking, but it can give a PO one serious place to keep context, decisions, and stakeholder-visible documentation.

## What It Is
Docmost is an open-source collaborative wiki and documentation application. It is closer to a self-hosted Confluence or Notion alternative than a template library. The repository contains the main product, documentation references, contribution material, deployment paths, and product code for a real-time editor with spaces, permissions, comments, page history, search, attachments, embeds, and built-in diagram support.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners
- Requirements: keep PRDs, problem statements, acceptance criteria, and decision history in one searchable workspace.
- Discovery: collect interview notes, journey maps, opportunity notes, and customer evidence without burying them in personal docs.
- Stakeholder alignment: maintain shared pages for roadmap context, tradeoffs, scope decisions, and launch plans.
- Backlog refinement: link ambiguous requests back to the problem, customer segment, evidence, and chosen non-goals.
- Roadmap planning: organize pages by product area, initiative, quarter, or customer problem using spaces.
- Delivery coordination: keep release notes, dependency notes, meeting outcomes, and risk logs visible to engineering, design, support, and leadership.
- Metrics and experimentation: document hypotheses, success metrics, experiment plans, and follow-up decisions next to the related initiative.

## How I Would Actually Use It
1. Create one space per product area and keep active PRDs, decisions, research notes, and launch plans under that space.
2. Use a standard PRD page template with problem, target users, non-goals, success metrics, open questions, and linked backlog items.
3. Create a decision log page for each major initiative so stakeholder tradeoffs do not disappear into Slack.
4. Use Mermaid or Excalidraw diagrams for flows, dependencies, and rollout plans instead of attaching static screenshots nobody updates.
5. Maintain a discovery evidence page per roadmap bet with interview notes, survey links, support themes, analytics findings, and unresolved risks.
6. Add comments on open questions before refinement meetings so the meeting focuses on decisions, not reading the document out loud.
7. Use page history after scope changes to see what actually changed and who agreed to it.

## Limitations / Watch Outs
- This is not a backlog manager, analytics tool, survey platform, or prioritization engine. It is documentation infrastructure.
- Self-hosting needs engineering or IT ownership for deployment, upgrades, backups, permissions, and security.
- The AGPL-3.0 core and enterprise license split need legal review before company adoption.
- Documentation tools fail when nobody curates them. A messy Docmost space is just a prettier graveyard of stale pages.
- If the company already uses Confluence, Notion, SharePoint, or Google Docs well, switching may create more disruption than value.
- Some enterprise needs, such as SSO, AI, and API access, may sit behind enterprise features.
