# mogkit

## Verdict

Yes, this is worth a product owner's time if you want sharper reviews of work already in progress. It is a practical set of interrogator-style skills for specs, discovery, metrics, launches, pricing, stakeholder conflict, and customer research, with low setup and no hosted account. It is not a product system of record, and its five stars do not prove that the methods improve outcomes.

## Why This Repo Was Picked

mogkit beat the other candidates because it is immediately useful without Jira, Notion, a database, or a new hosted product. Its 21 skills cover recurring PO decisions and review jobs, while the six discovery skills share a local corpus and provenance model. The repository also has a usable CLI, a documented workspace layout, validation scripts, examples, and current maintenance through November 2026. That combination is more actionable than a generic template catalog and less operationally heavy than a multi-tool backlog agent.

## What It Is

mogkit is a local toolkit, not a PM SaaS application. It contains 15 standalone Markdown skills that interrogate pasted product work, six corpus-backed discovery skills that build and query a provenance-bearing knowledge graph, a TypeScript CLI for creating a workspace, and a static workflow library with step-by-step operating guides. The skills are intentionally reviewers rather than generators: they surface gaps, assumptions, tradeoffs, and next questions while the PO remains the author and decision maker.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners

- Backlog refinement: use `spec-stress-test` to expose edge cases, failure modes, conflicting requirements, and missing user-visible behavior before refinement.
- User stories and acceptance criteria: use the interrogation output to identify unclear actors, outcomes, scenarios, and evidence instead of accepting prose that merely sounds complete.
- Prioritization: use `metrics-tree`, `tradeoff-frame`, and `prototype-brief` to connect options to outcomes, sacrifices, reversibility, and kill signals.
- Discovery: use `interview-coach`, `graphify`, `assumption-audit`, `discovery-query`, and `synthesis-map` to preserve quotes, provenance, gaps, and follow-up questions.
- Stakeholder alignment: use `stakeholder-map` and `narrative-review` to surface incentives, logic gaps, counterarguments, and the conversations needed to unblock a decision.
- Roadmap and delivery planning: use `launch-tier`, `launch-readiness`, and `prototype-brief` to size a release, identify ownership gaps, and define rollback or kill criteria.
- Metrics and experimentation: use `metrics-tree` and `eval-plan` to define what must be measured and which AI failure modes need real traces before claiming success.

## How I Would Actually Use It

1. Run `spec-stress-test` on the next PRD before refinement and turn its highest-severity gaps into explicit acceptance scenarios.
2. Run `tradeoff-frame` on a disputed build-versus-buy or scope decision, then record the reversible choice and evidence that would change it.
3. Put interview transcripts and support tickets into a versioned workspace, run `graphify`, and use `assumption-audit` to find roadmap claims with weak evidence.
4. Before committing a quarter theme, run `metrics-tree` on the intended outcome and reject targets that have no instrumentation or baseline.
5. Use `prototype-brief` before asking design or engineering for a prototype, with the pass signal and kill signal written down first.
6. Run `launch-readiness` and `launch-tier` one week before release to expose unowned enablement, rollback, audience, and communication work.
7. Use the workflow library as a team playbook, then adapt the relevant Markdown skill into the team's existing assistant or documentation process.

## Limitations / Watch Outs

- This is an assistant layer, not Jira, Linear, a roadmap database, an analytics system, or a stakeholder-facing source of truth.
- The repository is young and lightly adopted. Five stars, one fork, and no release objects are weak evidence of durability or product impact.
- You still need an AI assistant and pay for its usage. Local files remain on your machine, but anything pasted into the chosen assistant follows that assistant's data handling rules.
- The CLI requires Node 20 or newer and pnpm for development. The simplest use is still copying a skill into an existing assistant, which limits shared execution history and governance.
- The discovery engine depends on the quality and coverage of the team's source corpus. Provenance proves where a claim came from, not that the source is representative or correct.
- The catalog is broad. Installing every skill at once can create choice overload and encourage ritualized reviews instead of better decisions.
- The repository says the skills follow named practitioners and published practices, but that is not independent validation or endorsement.
