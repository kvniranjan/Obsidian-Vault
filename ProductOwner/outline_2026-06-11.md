# outline

## Verdict
Outline is worth a product owner's time if the team needs one serious place for PRDs, decisions, release notes, research synthesis, and stakeholder-facing product context. It is not a backlog or prioritization tool, so do not pretend it replaces Jira, Linear, GitHub Projects, or a roadmap process. Its value is simple: it makes product knowledge searchable, editable, permissioned, and less dependent on chat history.

## What It Is
Outline is a collaborative knowledge base application. It is a software product, not a template library, prompt collection, or curated reading list. The repository contains the main application source, docs, plugins, server and app code, Docker-related files, tests, contribution guidance, release history, and links to hosted and self-hosted usage paths.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners
- Requirements: keep PRDs, problem statements, acceptance criteria, non-goals, and open questions in one maintained workspace.
- Discovery: consolidate interview notes, customer evidence, support themes, journey maps, and synthesis pages by product area or initiative.
- Stakeholder alignment: publish roadmap context, scope decisions, release plans, FAQs, and tradeoff notes without turning every update into a meeting.
- Backlog refinement: link tickets back to the user problem, evidence, constraints, and agreed decision history.
- Roadmap planning: maintain initiative pages that explain why work matters, what changed, what is excluded, and how success will be measured.
- Delivery coordination: keep launch checklists, dependency notes, operational readiness, and release notes visible to engineering, design, support, sales, and leadership.

## How I Would Actually Use It
1. Create one collection per product area, then keep active PRDs, decision logs, release plans, and research summaries under that collection.
2. Use a standard PRD page structure: problem, target users, evidence, goals, non-goals, acceptance criteria, risks, dependencies, metrics, and open questions.
3. Maintain a decision log for each initiative so scope cuts, stakeholder tradeoffs, and late changes are visible after the meeting.
4. Create a launch page with release scope, rollout sequence, support notes, owner list, known risks, and customer messaging links.
5. Use page history and comments during refinement to separate real decisions from casual feedback.
6. Build a reusable discovery summary page for each major customer problem, with links to interviews, tickets, survey findings, analytics, and unresolved assumptions.
7. Use docs as the product operating layer, not as a dumping ground. Archive stale pages instead of letting them rot.

## Limitations / Watch Outs
- It will not prioritize work for you. You still need a prioritization method and a delivery tracker.
- Self-hosting needs engineering or IT ownership for deployment, upgrades, backups, auth, security, and monitoring.
- The BSL 1.1 license needs legal review before company adoption.
- If your company already uses Notion, Confluence, SharePoint, or Google Docs well, replacing it may create needless process churn.
- Documentation only works when someone curates it. A messy Outline workspace becomes another place where stale decisions go to die.
- It is knowledge infrastructure, not discovery tooling, analytics, feature flagging, survey collection, or experiment management.
