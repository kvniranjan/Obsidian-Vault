# prince2-agile-templates

## Verdict

This is worth a product owner's time when delivery involves governance, regulated stakeholders, suppliers, or formal release controls. It is not a product strategy system and it will not replace Jira, Linear, or a roadmap tool, but its backlog, sprint, acceptance, stakeholder, risk, UAT, and go-live templates are immediately reusable. The 0-star community signal means treat it as a useful starter library, not a proven standard.

It won because it connects ordinary PO work to the delivery controls that are usually missing from lightweight Agile template sets. ## What It Is

This is a documentation and template repository, not an application or framework. It contains editable Markdown templates for PRINCE2 governance, Agile ceremonies, project controls, quality, change management, testing, and go-live. The README says the intended use is to copy individual files, fork the repository, or clone it locally and adapt bracketed fields.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners

- Backlog refinement: use the product backlog template to record epics, MoSCoW priority, refinement decisions, and dependencies.
- User stories and acceptance: use sprint review, quality register, and Definition of Done sections to make Product Owner acceptance observable rather than verbal.
- Prioritization: connect backlog choices to business case, stage gates, benefits, and explicit scope tradeoffs.
- Stakeholder alignment: maintain a stakeholder register, communication plan, steering update, and decision log in a consistent format.
- Delivery coordination: track RAID items, dependencies, change requests, resource gaps, and supplier commitments before they become surprises.
- Release readiness: use UAT, go-live, change management, and project health templates to make launch decisions reviewable.
- Metrics and outcomes: use benefits realization and project health artifacts to follow value after delivery instead of stopping at sprint completion.

## How I Would Actually Use It

1. Copy `product-backlog.md` into the team's working repository and add one row per epic with a capped MoSCoW priority, owner, dependency, and target stage.
2. Before refinement, use the story and acceptance sections from the sprint and quality templates to turn vague backlog items into testable Product Owner decisions.
3. Run a weekly RAID and decision-log review with engineering, operations, and business stakeholders; escalate only items that threaten a stage tolerance or release outcome.
4. Use `sprint-review.md` to record what was demonstrated, what the PO accepted, what was rejected, and which backlog items need rework.
5. Use the UAT plan and go-live checklist for a release readiness meeting, with named evidence owners and a written go or no-go decision.
6. After launch, populate the benefits realization and lessons-learned artifacts with actual adoption, quality, cost, and outcome data.

## Limitations / Watch Outs

- The repository has no stars, forks, releases, or open issues, so there is little independent proof that these templates work across teams.
- It is delivery and governance focused. It does not provide customer discovery methods, product analytics, a roadmap visualization, or integrations with backlog systems.
- PRINCE2 terminology can add ceremony that is wasteful for a small startup or a low-risk internal feature. Tailor the controls instead of adopting every document.
- The files are starter templates, not a complete operating model. Someone still has to define ownership, review cadence, evidence standards, and where the authoritative backlog lives.
- The README claims 25 templates, while the visible root listing and role description make the repository feel like a compact library. Check the actual files before promising full lifecycle coverage.
- It is a young repository with no published release, so paths and content may change without a stable versioning contract.
