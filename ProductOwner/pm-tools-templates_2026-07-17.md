# pm-tools-templates

## Verdict
This is worth a product owner's time if they need reusable working templates, not another theory list. The Product Owner toolkit is practical: product vision, story writing, backlog management, prioritization, stakeholder collaboration, product metrics, and OKRs are all directly usable. The repo is noisy and over-marketed in places, so treat it as a template drawer, not as a complete operating model.

## What It Is
This is a template library for project, program, agile, scrum, and product work. For product owners, the important area is the `role-based-toolkits/product-owner` folder. It includes Markdown templates and guides for product vision, product strategy, backlog management, user stories, prioritization, stakeholder collaboration, product metrics, and OKRs.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners
- Backlog refinement: use the backlog management template to track ready items, dependencies, priority, refinement status, and Definition of Ready.
- User stories and acceptance criteria: use the user story template with Given-When-Then scenarios, Definition of Ready, Definition of Done, dependencies, and risks.
- Prioritization: use the backlog criteria and prioritization guide to make business value, user impact, effort, technical risk, and dependencies explicit.
- Discovery and strategy: use the product vision and product strategy templates to document target users, problems, value proposition, competitors, roadmap themes, and success metrics.
- Stakeholder alignment: use the stakeholder collaboration material to structure tradeoff discussions and decision records.
- Roadmap planning: use the roadmap levels in the toolkit to separate 12 to 18 month themes, 3 to 6 month outcomes, 1 to 3 month outputs, and weekly activity.
- Metrics and experimentation: use the product metrics dashboard and OKR template to connect backlog choices to measurable outcomes.

## How I Would Actually Use It
1. Copy the product vision template before a roadmap refresh and force the team to fill in target users, needs, business goals, and success metrics.
2. Use the user story template for messy backlog items that keep bouncing between product, design, and engineering.
3. Run backlog refinement with the backlog management template and require every near-term item to have priority, estimate, dependency, and Ready status.
4. Use the OKR template to turn a vague roadmap theme into one objective and three measurable key results before committing sprint capacity.
5. Pull the product metrics dashboard into a release review so the team defines adoption, retention, satisfaction, and business metrics before launch.
6. Use the stakeholder collaboration framework when Sales, Support, Compliance, and Engineering are competing for the same release window.

## Limitations / Watch Outs
- The repo description overpromises with AI, blockchain, predictive analytics, and SaaS wording. Ignore that unless you actually find a matching file you need.
- Community signal is weak: low stars and forks, despite recent activity.
- There are many open issues, so quality control may be uneven.
- GitHub API license metadata reports NOASSERTION while the README badge says MIT. Check the actual license before commercial reuse.
- The templates are generic. A PO still has to adapt them to the team's product, data, workflow, and governance.
- Some files are advanced for small teams and may create paperwork if copied blindly.
