# compass

## Verdict

Yes, this is worth a product owner's time if the team wants discovery decisions tied to evidence instead of another loose backlog. Compass gives outcomes, opportunities, solutions, experiments, feedback, and roadmap items a traceable home, but it is still an early product with almost no public adoption proof. Use the hosted demo for a trial; do not commit to self-hosting until the workflow survives a real discovery cycle.

## Why This Repo Was Picked

Compass is unusually close to a usable product workflow rather than a prompt bundle or reading list. Its Opportunity Solution Tree model connects measurable outcomes to customer opportunities, competing solutions, risky assumptions, experiments, and a Now/Next/Later roadmap, with linked evidence such as interview quotes, support tickets, and experiment results. The repository also includes feedback voting, interview capture, docs, squads, custom fields, an API, and an MCP interface, so a PO can test the loop from discovery through delivery coordination in one system.

## What It Is

Compass is a TypeScript and Next.js web application, not a template library. It implements a product discovery and delivery workspace with five connected layers: outcomes, opportunities, solutions, experiments, and roadmap. It also contains customer-interview capture, a feedback portal with voting, hierarchical docs, squads, custom fields, branding, REST and MCP APIs, automated tests, and a hosted deployment. The repository includes the application code, Prisma/PostgreSQL data model, documentation, demos, setup scripts, and Playwright/Vitest test configuration.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners

- Backlog refinement: turn vague requests into opportunities and competing solution options before writing delivery work.
- Discovery: attach interview quotes, support tickets, and experiment results to the opportunity or assumption they support.
- Prioritization: compare solutions against an outcome and the risk of their assumptions instead of ranking feature names in isolation.
- Acceptance and delivery coordination: keep the roadmap item connected to the evidence and experiment that justified it, then record what shipped.
- Stakeholder alignment: show how a proposed initiative traces from an OKR to customer need, solution choice, and validation plan.
- Roadmap planning: use Now, Next, and Later as a decision view rather than a promise of fixed dates.
- Feedback operations: collect votes and portal feedback, then use it as input to discovery rather than treating votes as automatic priority.

## How I Would Actually Use It

1. Create one measurable outcome for the next quarter, then add the customer opportunities that are supported by actual interviews or support data.
2. For a proposed feature, add at least two competing solutions and list the riskiest assumption for each before allowing it onto the roadmap.
3. Attach a short interview quote or support ticket to every opportunity that is driving a prioritization discussion.
4. Create a cheap experiment with a pass or fail threshold for the riskiest assumption, then record the result before committing engineering capacity.
5. Run a weekly discovery review from the tree: remove unsupported opportunities, split broad problems, and move only evidence-backed solutions toward Now.
6. Use the feedback portal to collect customer requests, merge the signal into opportunities, and communicate status through the roadmap rather than promising individual features.
7. Export or query the API for a stakeholder review showing each active roadmap item, its linked outcome, evidence, experiment status, and unresolved risks.

## Limitations / Watch Outs

- Public adoption is weak: the repository has 1 star, 1 fork, and 2 open issues. Current pushes show activity, not product-market fit or operational reliability.
- Self-hosting is not lightweight. The README calls for Node 22, pnpm 10, PostgreSQL, environment configuration, authentication setup, and deployment-specific details.
- The hosted instance is easier to test, but it still requires trust in a small project and may change quickly while the product is pre-scale.
- Compass is not a replacement for Jira, Linear, GitHub Issues, sprint execution, or a mature product analytics stack. Roadmap traceability does not prove that a team can deliver.
- Evidence links only improve decisions when the evidence is real, representative, and maintained. A well-connected tree can still encode biased interviews or vanity votes.
- The README says the API intentionally excludes human decision recording, solution-plan approval, decision application, and release dispatch operations. Do not assume the API can automate the full governance loop.
- There are no GitHub releases listed, so upgrades and compatibility need to be checked from commits and the live deployment.
- The Apache-2.0 license is permissive, but the operational and data-protection obligations of storing customer feedback and interview material remain yours.
