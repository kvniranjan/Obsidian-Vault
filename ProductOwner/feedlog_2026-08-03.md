# feedlog

## Verdict
FeedLog is worth a product owner's time if the team needs a practical feedback-to-roadmap loop and is willing to self-host or have engineering own deployment. It is not just a reading resource: it gives users a place to submit ideas, vote, discuss, track roadmap status, and receive changelog updates. The catch is blunt: without a team ready to run Postgres, auth, email, and deployment, it becomes another nice repo nobody uses.

## What It Is
FeedLog is an open-source product feedback and roadmap application. It is a software tool, not a template library or prompt pack. The repo contains the Nuxt/Vue application, Docker setup, deployment guides, configuration docs, changelog, contribution docs, and a production demo.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners
FeedLog gives a PO a shared operating surface for customer feedback instead of scattering requests across Slack, email, sales notes, and support tickets. Feedback boards support discovery and backlog refinement by turning raw ideas into visible candidate work. Voting and comments help with prioritization, stakeholder alignment, and demand validation, while roadmap states give customers and internal stakeholders a clearer view of what is planned, in progress, and shipped. The changelog side helps delivery coordination because shipped work can be turned into product updates without starting from a blank page.

## How I Would Actually Use It
1. Create separate boards for customer requests, internal sales asks, and support pain points so each source can be triaged without mixing signal.
2. Use votes and comments as input to backlog refinement, but do not let vote count replace product judgment or revenue context.
3. Move selected feedback into roadmap columns before sprint planning so stakeholders can see what is being considered versus committed.
4. Use the similar-idea detection to merge duplicate requests before the backlog looks bigger than it really is.
5. Publish changelog entries after releases and link them back to the original feedback items.
6. Review high-vote items monthly with support, sales, and engineering to decide whether to build, defer, close, or research further.

## Limitations / Watch Outs
This is not a plug-and-play SaaS account unless someone hosts it. The repo expects PostgreSQL 17+ with `pgvector`, auth secrets, admin email setup, and optional email, OAuth, S3, and OpenAI-compatible configuration. It is still pre-1.0, and its own changelog says minor versions before 1.0 may include breaking changes. Also, a public voting board can amplify loud users, so the PO still needs segmentation, revenue context, strategy, and qualitative research.
