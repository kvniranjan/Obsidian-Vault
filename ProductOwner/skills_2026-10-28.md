## What It Is

This is a collection of plain-text AI skill folders for Claude Code and compatible skill installers. It is not a tracker, SaaS product, plugin, or system of record. The repository has a meta-skill for the operating model plus product strategy, discovery, architecture, delivery, AI-native product, and product leadership skills, each with focused instructions and Markdown templates.

## Why It Is Useful For Product Owners

- Backlog refinement: turn validated opportunities into time-boxed bets with hypotheses, target metrics, effort, kill criteria, and scale criteria.
- Discovery: capture interview snapshots, maintain an opportunity-solution tree, and design small assumption tests before committing engineering capacity.
- Requirements: use solution briefs to state the customer problem, desired outcome, constraints, and open questions without writing a large speculative PRD.
- Prioritization: use bet categories, portfolio allocation, strategic priority, and explicit kill criteria to make trade-offs visible.
- Roadmap planning: use capability blocks, bet backlogs, and outcome-oriented roadmap templates instead of a feature list with false precision.
- Stakeholder alignment: use strategy canvases, block portfolios, operating rhythms, and portfolio reviews to make ownership and trade-offs explicit.
- Metrics and delivery: define leading, core, and lagging metrics, plan staged rollouts, and run a post-launch bet retrospective with a scale, iterate, or kill decision.

## How I Would Actually Use It

1. Copy `product-discovery/templates/interview-snapshot.md` into the team's research folder and complete one snapshot after every customer conversation.
2. Run `product-discovery/templates/opportunity-solution-tree.md` in a weekly product trio session to separate user opportunities from proposed features.
3. Convert one roadmap initiative into the `product-architecture/templates/bet-backlog.md` format, including a baseline, target, timebox, and kill criteria.
4. Use `product-architecture/templates/solution-brief.md` as the pre-refinement handoff and reject any item with no validated opportunity or measurable outcome.
5. Build a Now, Next, Later roadmap from the repository's roadmap template, showing direction and confidence rather than pretending dates are certain.
6. Plan a staged release with `product-delivery/templates/rollout-plan.md` and `launch-checklist.md`, including guardrails and rollback triggers.
7. After launch, complete `product-delivery/templates/bet-retrospective.md` and record whether to scale, iterate, or kill the bet.

## Limitations / Watch Outs

- It does not connect to Jira, Linear, GitHub Projects, analytics, customer research systems, or roadmap software.
- The content is opinionated. Weekly discovery, product trios, bets, and staged rollouts may be too formal for a tiny team or too light for a regulated enterprise process.
- Most instructions are written for Claude Code. The Markdown is portable, but install commands, skill discovery, and runtime behavior need checking in Codex or another agent.
- The repository has only 23 stars, 4 forks, no open issues, and no release history. Recent activity is a positive signal, not proof of production adoption.
- Templates can create the appearance of rigor. A bet backlog does not validate the customer problem, and a metrics hierarchy does not make weak instrumentation trustworthy.
- The repository mixes product work with GTM, AI-native product, and leadership material. Start with discovery, architecture, and delivery rather than installing everything.
